# Pitch round and direction round

Everything else in direction converges. This step diverges. It has two rounds:

- **A. Concept round**: what the film says and does (the telling).
- **B. Direction round**: how the chosen concept looks, moves and sounds.

## A.1 When the concept round runs

- Unformed requests (see `intake-and-brief.md`, section 4), always.
- Formed requests skip it: the user's concept is the concept.
- An ad prompt that gives facts and an occasion but no telling (no picture, scene or idea of how to show it) is unformed about the telling: it always enters the round.
- Auto mode never skips it; the gate runs inside the run (A.4).
- Tier sets the size: full tier 5 concepts, light tier 3 concepts. In both, at least two concepts are unexpected.
- What the user is already picturing was asked in the stage 01 message (`questions.md` section 4); read the answer in `BRIEF.md`, never ask it here. An existing idea is one of the pitches and is never displaced.

## A.2 The sampling gate (internal, always)

Run this before writing any pitch, in every mode. None of it is shown to the user.

First, four questions about this brief, answered specifically, not generically:

1. **What does the subject look like?** Its own visual world. Illustration only: an island-travel piece has whitewashed walls and caldera cliffs; an outage postmortem has terminal green and a scarred timeline; a bakery has flour dust and the 5 am oven glow. The subject's vocabulary drives the layouts.
2. **What does the target emotion look like as a frame?** Longing is empty space the viewer wants to fill; urgency is compression; awe is one element too large for the canvas.
3. **What does the playback surface demand?** A lobby screen is ambient and glanced at; a feed fights for its first second; a story is vertical and fast.
4. **What does every other video on this subject look like?** That is the anti-pattern (use research note RQ-category). The tail pitches must not be it.

Then one concept from each path:

| Path | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Subject world | What does the subject or product live inside? |
| Emotion | What does the viewer feel before and after? |
| Audience | Who exactly, in what moment? Meet their expectation, or break it |
| Anti-pattern, inverted | What does every film in the category do, and what is the opposite? |
| Unusual format | What everyday interface or object can carry the story (a letter, a countdown, a recipe, a front page, a map, a receipt, a group chat, a ticker)? |

Light tier keeps three of the five paths, always including the anti-pattern inverted and one other unexpected path.

Estimate for each concept the probability that a model handed this brief would produce it. The numbers are directional, not calibrated, and they exist to enforce one constraint: **at least two concepts must sit below 0.10.** If all clear 0.10, every pitch is the median: start over. Then check silhouettes: sketch each concept's major elements as rough bounding boxes; two concepts with the same silhouette are one concept, so replace one. Never pitch five versions of the same idea.

Probabilities never reach the user. They are a sampling constraint, not a scorecard.

## A.3 Presenting the round

Each pitch, short:

- **Concept** in one sentence that predicts what happens on screen ("premium, smooth, cinematic" predicts nothing).
- **Visual world**: the look in a line, naming the one or two capabilities it rides in plain language (never a feature or tool name). A capability that would fit all five pitches is decoration; name it only where this concept leans on it.
- **Opening hook (0 to 3 s)**: what is literally on screen and heard.
- **Literal anchors**: each symbolic or abstract device and the real, recognisable thing it stands for, seen first or with it (the grounding rule, the ice-qa skill, `references/clarity-gate.md` section 6); or "no symbolic device".
- **Product beat** (a brand whose offer is an app, a SaaS or a product): the beat where the product does its real job, the cause seen and then the effect.
- **Why it works for this audience**, linked to a research note.
- **Risk**: cliche, claim, cultural misread, production cost.

This presentation is for the 02 review card in step mode; in auto mode the critic picks (A.4) and the pitches go into the heads-up, with no stop. All pitches appear before any recommendation: a recommendation stated first anchors everything after it. Then recommend one, with a reason. Mixing is a first-class answer ("the framing of the second with the opening of the fourth"); silence or "you decide" accepts the recommendation. One round: the pitches are an offer, not a quiz, and there is no second batch unless the user asks.

The chosen concept **is** the brief's creative core: it answers `message` and the angle, it lands in `BRIEF.md` under `## Intent` in the words the user accepted, and it gets a D-entry. Under it in `ice/plan/concepts.md` goes the viewer takeaway (the ice-qa skill, `references/clarity-gate.md` section 2): what it sells, the offer's job, the one-line idea a stranger must get, and the literal anchors. The D-entry quotes it; every cold-viewer read is matched against it.

## A.4 Auto mode

Auto mode (with or without "just build it") changes the audience, not the discipline. Walk the same gate (four questions, all paths, tail constraint, silhouette check), let the critic pick (`roles.md` section 3), and keep building. The heads-up then names the direction chosen and why, the machinery it rides, and **the most typical direction deliberately left behind**. An auto run is where the median is most dangerous: no one is present to say "this looks like every other video", so the gate has to say it.

## A.5 Concept tests

A concept that fails any test is replaced before it is shown.

- **Hero moment**: the concept predicts the one frame someone would screenshot and send to a friend. If you cannot name it, you have a topic, not an idea.
- **Brand test and competitor swap** (a film for a brand; the ice-qa skill, `references/identity-tests.md`, the one home of both): the director writes each concept's blind description (no name, logo, wordmark, UI or colour words) and the critic judges it before it sees the named pitches, at the same strength the brand film's 09 test uses. A concept that fails is changed in what the viewer sees, or replaced, and judged again by a fresh critic; a note, a promised UI moment or a later stage never clears it.
- **The "and" test**: if the concept sentence needs "and", it is two ideas; split or drop one.
- **Grounded**: every symbolic or abstract device has a literal anchor the viewer recognises, shown first or with it; every sound, action and claim has a visible source; the product's interactions happen as they really happen (`clarity-gate.md` section 6 in the ice-qa skill). An idea that exists only inside a symbol fails.
- **Product job**: for a brand whose offer is an app, a SaaS or a product, the concept shows the product doing its real job in a full beat, whatever the film type (the ice-film skill, `references/film-setup.md` section 1). A feeling or a world may carry the film; it never replaces that beat.
- **Stranger test**: write the takeaway's one-line idea as a viewer would say it after one watch. If it needs the concept's own vocabulary to make sense, the telling is not yet literal.
- **Kill rules**: kill a concept if a reference is identifiable from frame one, if it needs assets the user does not have and cannot get, or if it would fit any brief.
- **First idea aside**: write the first idea down, then set it aside; it is nearly always the name's first association or the category's costume. It may return only as one of the five, never as the default.
- **Chosen, not averaged**: never combine all concepts into one; a mix takes named parts from at most two.

## B.1 Fixed and free

| Fixed: the laws (other skills own them) | Free: everything a viewer would describe |
|---|---|
| Truth: real numbers, real assets (`ice-assets`) | Palette, and whether there is color at all |
| Sync and determinism (`ice-motion`, `ice-engines`) | Ground: void, paper, material, photographic, field |
| Readability (`ice-type`) | Typography and its personality |
| Sound measured, not guessed (`ice-audio`) | Camera: locked, dolly, orbit, continuous, none |
| Verified in the delivered file (`ice-qa`) | Whether there is UI, a cursor, a character, a presenter |
| No house style | Pacing, rhythm, shot lengths, cut style; structure; the signature move; sound palette |

The test: **if two films are indistinguishable, the fixed column was applied and the free column was skipped.** The fixed column is craft. The free column is the film.

## B.2 The dials

Pick a position on each dial before any build, and write it into the direction.

| Dial | One pole | Other pole |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | contemplative, long holds | relentless, cut on every beat |
| Density | one idea per frame, vast negative space | layered, maximal, many things true at once |
| Ground | void or flat paper | photographic, material, or a live field |
| Depth | resolutely flat 2D | deep 3D with real perspective |
| Camera | locked off, cuts do the work | continuous move across the whole film |
| Type | neutral grotesk that gets out of the way | editorial serif, mono, or expressive display |
| Texture | clinical, zero grain | film grain, halation, print, CRT |
| Color | monochrome plus one signal | duotone, or the full brand spectrum |
| Product or subject | literal (real UI, real object) | abstracted, metaphorical, or absent |
| Sound | silence and small ticks | score-led, or sound-design-led |
| Voice | narrated throughout | title cards only, no voice |

Extremes are usually more interesting than centres. A film in the middle of every dial is the default look, which is what everyone else's tool produces. The dials map onto the six fingerprint axes in `anti-sameness.md`.

The "Product or subject" dial moves toward abstracted or metaphorical only inside the grounding rule (the ice-qa skill, `references/clarity-gate.md` section 6: the literal thing first or with it). For a brand whose offer is an app, a SaaS or a product, "absent" is not available: the product's real job is shown (the ice-film skill, `references/film-setup.md` section 1).

**The richness floor bounds the dials.** Before writing any direction, open the "Richness floor" table of the film type file (the ice-film skill's `references/film-types/<type>.md`; edit and explainer workflows name theirs) and keep it next to the dials. A pole is allowed only where the floor allows it: the floor's `hero_share`, `frame_fill`, `empty_half`, `in_shot_motion` and `longest_still` decide how far "vast negative space", "long holds", "void ground" or "locked off" may go in this film type (a product launch floor, for example, keeps most frames full and moving, so a contemplative, near-empty direction cannot pass it there). Calm is still possible inside the floor: slow moves on a full frame, a large hero on a quiet ground, holds only where the floor exempts them. "Premium" and "restraint" subtract clutter, never fullness.

## B.3 Deriving a direction

Work four inputs in order; the fourth usually decides.

1. **The brand's or subject's own material.** Tokens, site, packaging, product photography, the subject's real colors and objects. Take palette and type seriously, but do not simply animate the website: a film has time, a medium the brand guidelines say nothing about. Extend the brand into it.
2. **The category's visual expectation.** Name it in one sentence from research note RQ-category. Then decide deliberately to **meet** it (fast recognition; right when the product is unfamiliar and the category is trusted) or **break** it (immediate differentiation; right when the product is a new answer in a tired category). Doing either by accident is the failure.
3. **The audience's context.** A muted feed needs the claim as picture and type in the first two seconds; a conference screen can hold a slow, quiet, wide shot; a sales attachment can be dense because the viewer chose to watch. Context changes the dials more than the product does.
4. **The claim's emotional shape.** The look should be that feeling expressed in another medium:

| The claim is about | The film should feel |
|---|---|
| Relief from drudgery | Weight lifting: crowded to clean, dense to spacious, loud to quiet |
| Precision or trust | Instrument-like: grid, restraint, tabular figures, no flourish |
| Speed | Kinetic: hard cuts, speed ramps, motion blur, arrival before you expect it |
| Craft or taste | Slow, tactile, photographic, generous negative space |
| Power or scale | Wide, deep, three-dimensional, a small human next to a big system |
| Play or delight | Overshoot, color, sound-design-led, visual jokes that land fast |

If the claim is relief and the film is relentless, the look argues against the script: the most common reason a technically clean film feels wrong.

Name the emotional register specifically ("controlled anticipation", "quiet authority", "earned relief"), never "exciting", "premium" or "inspiring". "Premium" is not a style: define it per brief (restraint, tactile print, precise type, documentary realism, controlled scale) and treat the word as a cue to subtract, not to add chrome.

## B.4 Three directions, kill two

Write three deliberately different directions, one page each, no images:

```
DIRECTION <name>
Thesis      one sentence: what this film IS
Dials       energy, density, ground, depth, camera, type, texture, color, subject, sound, voice
Palette     ground, ink, accent, with the small-text-safe accent variant (color.md)
Type        families and the role each plays (type-pairing.md)
Signature   the one move only this film makes
Sound       the palette, and whether music, sound design, or voice leads
Structure   device, arc, where the turn lands, opening state, ending (B.6)
Floor       for each measure of the type's richness floor: the floor, and this direction's planned value in its typical
            shot (hero share, frame fill, empty-half seconds, in-shot motion, longest still, depth layers)
Risk        how this fails if executed badly
Wrong for   the kind of product or subject this would be wrong for
```

Judge them (the critic does this, `roles.md` section 3):

1. Does it serve the claim, or decorate it?
2. Does it look like this brand or subject, but better than it currently looks?
3. Could a competitor use it unchanged? If yes, kill it: it is a category template (the competitor swap, the ice-qa skill, `references/identity-tests.md`, runs on the style frames at 05).
4. Can it survive a bad day? A direction that only works if every frame is perfect will be abandoned mid-build.
5. Does it pass the gates in `anti-sameness.md` (default look, distance, fingerprint)?
6. Can it pass the film type's richness floor, measure by measure, from its "Floor" lines? A direction whose planned values sit under the floor, or whose dials make the floor unreachable, is REJECT (or REVISE with the exact measures to raise). A direction that cannot pass the QA gate is never approved.
7. Can it reach studio craft under this run's constraints (engine, AI media allowed or not, assets on hand)? Name, for each direction, the axes of the studio benchmark (the ice-qa skill, `references/studio-benchmark.md` section 4) that the constraints would cap. Prefer the direction whose look the constraints reach at full craft; a direction whose look depends on craft the run cannot make is REVISE. The style frames of the chosen direction are then benchmarked at 05 against real reference ads, and a capped gap is told to the user before 06.

**Inherited directions are killed mechanically.** If the brief lands on top of a bundled template, demo, worked example or style card (any skill's), that inherited direction is automatically one of the three and automatically one of the two killed. Write it out, name what it inherited, then build something that is not it.

## B.5 The signature move

Every film worth remembering does one thing no other film does: not an effect, a move that only makes sense for this claim.

- **It expresses the claim.** If it would work equally well on an unrelated product, it is a flourish.
- **It repeats two or three times**: early, paid off in the middle, resolved at the end. Once is an accident; four times is a tic.
- **It is cheap enough to do perfectly.** An ambitious move at 70% is worse than a simple one at 100%.

Find it by taking the subject's core verb and making the film do it. Illustration only: a product that consolidates lets scattered elements converge at every transition; one that removes work lets the frame get emptier as the film runs; one that connects makes one continuous line the through-element. Give the logo or the main object a job inside the film; a logo that only appears at the end as a sticker is wasted, and puns on the name are the weakest ownership.

## B.6 Sameness hides in structure

Two films can look nothing alike and still be the same film. Surfaces differ easily; structure converges silently (same three-act skeleton, same turn point, same ending of draining to a quiet lockup, same climax of one element swallowing the frame, same copy placement, same sparse-then-sustained sound). Vary the structure on purpose:

| Structural axis | Options (a menu, never a default) |
|---|---|
| Rhetorical device | many into one; before and after; a day in the life; testimony; pure demonstration; contradiction; absence (show the work that no longer happens); one object examined |
| Arc | problem to solve; start at the payoff and work back; one unbroken demonstration; a question held open to the last frame; chronological |
| Where the turn lands | about 40% is the default; try about 15% (cold open on the answer) or about 75% (a long, patient problem) |
| Ending | quiet lockup is everyone's default; end loud; end mid-motion; end on the subject still in use; end on the problem, absent |
| Climax mechanic | one hero swallowing the frame is one option, not the option |
| Opening state | empty ground that populates is the default; start full and subtract; start mid-action |
| Human presence | a hand, a face, a voice, a real photograph often differentiates more than any grade |
| Sound dramaturgy | continuous bed that stops; no resolution; one recurring sound |

Deliberately breaking the category expectation twice, the same way, is itself a house style. Run the two-film test in `anti-sameness.md`.

## B.7 Commit

When a direction is approved, commit and stop being creative. Write it into `DIRECTION.md` and the treatment and execute it with total consistency; a film that changes its mind halfway looks like two films. Be inventive about the film; be boringly disciplined about the build.

Optional proof before full build: the workflow may render the first 2 to 4 s in two or three of the surviving treatments (opening studies) so the critic can choose on first frame, readability, brand fit, originality and phone legibility; never pick the busiest by default.

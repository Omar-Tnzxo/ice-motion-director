# Script shapes: the 22-shape bank

Proven launch clips were reversed into the spoken line each one implies, then grouped into 22 shot shapes. Each shape has a pattern for how a beat is said. Use it to draft each beat's voiceover (or on-screen copy) in a proven shape. The matching time-coded shot blueprint for each shape lives in the ice-motion skill (blueprints); this file holds only the words.

## Contents

1. How to use the bank

## 1. How to use the bank

- Story truth comes first: never invent, bend, drop or reorder a beat to fit a shape. The shape only decides how a beat is said.
- For each beat: find its role in the index, pick the shape whose pattern matches the line you would naturally write, draft the line in that pattern, and tag the shape id on the beat (`script.md` format).
- No shape fits: omit the tag and write plainly; the shot is composed freely later. Never force a wrong shape.
- Vary the shapes across the film. Kinetic-type-beats is the workhorse (it serves six roles); lean on it, but not everywhere.
- Patterns are cue-segmented on purpose: keep that rhythm so each on-screen piece can be revealed when the voice names it.
- Slots in angle brackets are filled from the truth sheet and the brief only. Never copy a pattern's wording literally.

## 3. The 22 shapes

### 1. kinetic-type-beats: the words are the motion
- Hook: a punchy claim or rhetorical jab whose key word swaps in place or escalates beat by beat; the swap is the joke. Slot form: "<pain or jab>. <same jab, escalated>."
- Problem: 3-5 short pain statements (or a "what if" framing), each landing alone before the next; no product yet.
- Product intro: hard cut through "introducing", a tagline and value beats, resolving on the brand name or logo.
- Benefits: a staccato montage of 8-12 short value phrases, each flashing and clearing at high tempo.
- CTA: a closing line or short value stack that snaps in beat by beat and lands on the logo or URL.
- Brand outro: a rapid barrage of single-word verbs asserting breadth, resolving on the brand's one defining word; no lockup needed.

### 2. typewriter-reveal: someone is typing this
- Hook: a relatable line typed live and edited mid-stream (a word backspaces and retypes); the everyday thought in the viewer's own words.
- Brand outro: the mark holds centre while a sub-line types or swaps into the final CTA.

### 3. spatial-pan-stations: a panned line of stations
- Hook: a march of named milestones across time, landing on "until now".
- Problem: pain stations traversed one by one, ending on a knot ("too many disconnected steps").
- Product intro: a labelled concept strip read station by station, the final pan bridging into the live product ("here is the idea, here it is working").

### 4. constellation-hub: nodes ring a centre
- Hook: a spread of tools or channels collapsing onto one centre ("it connects everything").
- Social proof: the product mark is the hub and partner logos (real, permitted) orbit it; variant without a hub: a claim holds centre while icons scatter and drift outward.
- CTA: the ring collapses onto the core on one click that springs the product open; the voice turns "it connects everything" into the action line.

### 5. ticker-takeover: options cycle, then a hero crashes in
- Hook: a "could be <X>, or <Y>, or <Z>?" cycle on one swapping word, then the hero claim crashes in and replaces it.
- Brand outro: use cases cycle through one slot, then the brand mark crashes in and owns the frame. A collision, not a fade.

### 6. prompt-type-submit-generate: watch me ask
- Hook: the voice frames the ask while the prompt types live; the question is the whole hook; the answer stays off screen or a second ask starts before the cut.
- Product intro: the first look is the composer; the product is introduced as a conversation partner while a long prompt types with attachments, ending on or just after submit.
- Key feature: one prompt and response round trip; the voice names the ask, lets the working state breathe a beat, then calls the result as it streams in.
- CTA: the closing invitation is a typed command or request; the card holds with only the caret blinking.

### 7. zoom-out-workspace-reveal: the mystery re-scopes
- Hook: near-silence or one slow tease over a close-up mystery; the landing line is timed to the zoom-out and answers "what am I looking at?" exactly when the workspace does.
- Benefits: the micro-story during the close-up, then the scale line lands with the zoom-out; the benefit is breadth, revealed in one move.

### 8. fixed-anchor-cycle: a roll-call around a pinned line
- Hook: one static claim holds while its audience or option list cycles fast beneath it; the brand line lands after the cycle clears.
- Benefits: one pinned claim while the whole surface re-skins around it ("works everywhere").
- Brand outro: the brand name sits immovable while tagline words cycle beside it, landing on the completed lockup.

### 9. cursor-ui-demo: a canvas already alive
- Hook: a "we are all here working" line over a live canvas; presence, not features.
- Product intro: a cursor sweeps in to introduce the surface, landing on a hovered hero element or a fresh result.
- Key feature: one specific multi-step workflow end to end across 2-4 real edits, landing on the action or result.
- Benefits: a demo, value line, demo sandwich; the voice names the value between two live interactions.

### 10. dataviz-countup: one exploding stat
- Hook: one approved statistic counts up as the voice speaks it; scale alone carries the tension.
- Problem: a count-up or chart the camera pushes through to dramatize a worsening problem.
- Product intro: open on the result; a card grid scrolls to one hero metric.
- Key feature: the feature proven by its metrics, each read as it lands.
- Social proof: adoption, rating and scale metrics tick up; the biggest number lands last.
- Every number comes from the truth sheet (ice-assets).

### 11. cta-morph-press: a lone widget does its trick
- Hook: one small widget on a bare field morphs in place and performs its payload while the voice names the transformation; small thing, big claim.
- CTA: the brand mark condenses into the single thing you click ("here is us, click here").

### 12. overwhelm-surround: buried by your own tools
- Problem: recognizable tools pile in until they surround the viewer; the pain is being swamped, not one bad number.

### 13. logo-assemble-lockup: the mark comes to exist
- Product intro: an abstract system assembles around a fixed mark; no copy or just the product name.
- CTA: the logo builds, a fast push streaks giant CTA letters past the lens, resolving on the URL or action verb.
- Brand outro: elements clear off all four edges, the mark draws on, the wordmark completes the lockup.

### 14. video-text-pivot: show it work, then the number
- Product intro: the product video plays, then slides aside to hand the frame to one impact stat.
- Key feature: a feature clip runs, then yields to a metric or impact line; the clip proves it, the number lands it.

### 15. device-surface-showcase: inside its real interface
- Product intro: the product introduced by doing its core loop once inside its real interface, stepwise; the voice narrates plainly and lands on "that's it".
- Key feature: a feature shown being used inside its real surface; the device or window is the hero and its screens advance through a flow.

### 16. titlecard-reveal: near-still cards
- Product intro: 2-3 near-still cards seamed by quick handoffs: name, one-line claim, then into the product.
- Benefits: one clean two-line value title, one slide-up, then held still; low motion is the point.
- Social proof: a busy open wiped away to a clean lockup plus a "loved by <approved count>" line.
- CTA: 2-3 near-still cards in sequence, one short closing phrase each, ending on the held logo or URL; the calm is the confidence.

### 17. grid-card-assemble: breadth at once
- Key feature: a tile or card grid self-assembles to show many capabilities ("look how much it does").
- Benefits: short value phrases populate a list about one per second, accumulating.
- Social proof: a wall of real partner logos builds, then a zoom-out reveals the ecosystem.

### 18. comparison-split: two paired capabilities
- Key feature: two complementary capabilities of equal weight together ("<X> and <Y>, in lockstep").

### 19. agent-progress-theater: the machine visibly works
- Key feature: trigger, working theater, receipt; the voice hands the work over, then reads findings as rows land and check off; present tense, the machine is the subject.

### 20. panel-edit-live-sync: one gesture, two surfaces
- Key feature: 2-4 short cause and effect couplets ("do <X>, <Y> answers"), ending held on the last edit.

### 21. transcript-scroll-artifact-reveal: the work, then the deliverable
- Key feature: a "look how much happened" line read down the evidence at traversal pace, then one pivot line cashes it in on the artifact; evidence first, payoff last.

### 22. camera-journey: a flight over the result
- Key feature: a flying camera explores the generated artifact while the voice makes one calm, sweeping claim; no hands, no clicks.
- Benefits: a cause and effect round trip: the first half of the line lands on the action, the second on the payoff the camera travels to.

## 4. Persuasion moves and beat emotions

Tag every beat with one concrete persuasion move and one specific emotion (compound allowed). Never "show benefit".

- Moves: pain agitation, negative contrast, friction reduction, show-don't-tell proof, feature-to-benefit translation, statistical proof, authority by association, social proof, risk reversal, future pacing, value stacking, rule of three, scarcity, status seeking. Invent one if none fits and explain it.
- Emotions: anxiety, frustration, overwhelm, tension, urgency, skepticism; curiosity, clarity, intrigue, aspiration; trust, confidence, control, ease, power, awe, excitement, belonging; triumph, motivation, peace of mind, inevitability.
- Social proof, statistics and authority need approved sources (ice-assets); with none, use a different move.

# Family: evidence and data looks

Styles whose authority comes from real material: imagery, scans, measurements, archives, datasets, real objects. Read `style-method.md` first. Every entry's examples are illustration only.


## Shared rules for this family

- Evidence styles need real, licence-clean material (satellite and aerial imagery, point clouds, microscopy, archive scans, CAD or bills of materials, datasets). If the brief has none and none can be sourced legally, pick another family: never fill an evidence look with generated imagery presented as evidence.
- Keep a facts file for every claim (claim, source URL, quoted wording or method, date). Numbers are shown exactly as sourced; fictional or illustrative data is labelled on every frame where it appears. The truth sheet and asset licensing are owned by the ice-assets skill; chart honesty rules are in `narration-grammars-2.md` (t3).
- The material is the main picture; graphics measure, annotate and guide the eye. Marks arrive by being made (lines grow, counters roll, outlines print), not by fading.
- Credits for imagery and data are burned into the picture where the licence requires, and listed in the film's credits file.
- **Craft profile (family default):** needs: footage (scoped: real, licence-clean material is the main picture, never generated imagery presented as evidence, rules above), data (marks that measure and annotate arrive by being made: lines grow, counters roll, outlines print; numbers exactly as sourced, rules above), type (annotations, readings and the credits a licence requires, burned into the picture, rules above), camera (the camera reads the material: the entry's camera list), foley (the real material's sounds: paper, magnets, marker, instruments, `style-method.md` section 12 row "Evidence and documentary looks"); serves when: depth (stacked material or a physical object: layers and real depth of field), light (a real object, model or table: one key, with depth of field on it), composite (cut-outs or projections on a surface: contact shadow and edge match), silence (a key reading or mark: the sound drops out before it), ambience (a real place, lab or room in the material: its room tone), music (music in the type or the brief: a restrained score in the style's own instruments, `style-method.md` section 12), identity (a brand or an institution named: its marks in their slots).

## Real-world evidence

### Satellite Briefing (`satellite-briefing`)
- **Look:** Real, licence-clean, georeferenced satellite or aerial imagery graded down to an analyst's grey, with thin white measurement hairlines (reticle, outlines, range line, one reading) sitting on real coordinates. Not a chart on paper, not a sci-fi HUD with glowing chrome, not a blueprint or isometric diorama, not a keynote with slides or chapter numbers.
- **Medium-specific moves:** camera never stops (distance interpolated in log space, eased segments, holds keep 2-5 % creep or slow rotation, no frozen frame over 1 s); nested imagery plates (world, region, detail) with feathered edges dissolve in as the camera descends; time changes are soft scan fronts (a satellite pass), north to south forward, south to north for a rewind, never dissolves, spacing tells the speed; marks arrive by being made (line grows, outline prints behind the scan, counter rolls and lands with a small pop, reticle snaps with overshoot); grade moves with the story (orbit in true colour, analysis in grey, colour back only where the story turns); hairlines 1.2-2.6 px with a dark halo, dashes for history and claims; a printed-map "claim layer" can be torn away by a scanner bar to show the ground; light grain, vignette, directional blur only on whip pans.
  - Camera: oblique orbit with limb in frame (opening, return); dive (log zoom tilting to nadir); locked nadir frame with slow creep (time running in one place); push-in on a point; track along a line (river, road, border); whip pan with blur ("meanwhile"); climb back to orbit (ending). No crossfades between unrelated places, no hard cuts inside a zoom.
- **Palette:** neutral grey ground roughly 3-65 % value; white means measured; one warm accent only for the single reading; an optional second flat cartographic hue for the claim being tested; true colour is evidence; no false-colour ramps. Example: ground #070B0E to #A1A7A9, marks #F2F5F6, reading #FFBE55, map blue rgb(46, 112, 206).
- **Type and captions:** light condensed grotesk for the reading 96-130 px (Barlow Condensed); monospace for instruments and dates 20-34 px (IBM Plex Mono); sans for labels 24-42 px; serif only for claim layer and title. Labels attach to the ground with leaders; reading in the right third, label above, source note below. Subtitles are a bottom-left transcript strip (2 px white rule, dark plate fading right, one line per cue, split long sentences). Nothing under 20 px at 1080p. Hold subtitles >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s); labels >= characters / 4.5 + 1.5 s (CJK).
- **Sound:** telemetry (FM pings, lock-on ping, dry grid ticks that subdivide to accelerate, noise scans rising for rewind and falling forward, servo whines, ratchet for a range line); weight from timpani, bass drum, gran cassa, bowed cymbal, reversed tam-tam for time running backwards; pulse bass with audible 2nd and 3rd harmonics, low-passed detuned-saw pads, risers for dives; beds of orbit hum, radio hiss, the place's own ambience as J/L-cuts; option: keep the palette dry and let one wet timbre (glass, water, bowed metal) mark the turn; suck-out 0.2-0.45 s (music and foley down ~25 dB) before the heaviest impacts. Mix: voice ~10 dB over music, 20-60 Hz about 15 dB under full band (HPF ~40 Hz), -14 LUFS, true peak <= -1.2 dB; calm analyst voice.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: camera (the camera never stops: distance interpolated in log space, eased segments, holds keeping 2-5% creep or slow rotation, no frozen frame over 1 s, entry moves and camera list), transition (time changes as soft scan fronts, north to south forward and south to north for a rewind, never dissolves; whip pans with directional blur for a change of place; never crossfades between unrelated places, never hard cuts inside a zoom, entry moves and camera list), finish (the grade moves with the story: true colour in orbit, an analyst's grey for analysis, colour back only where the story turns; light grain and a vignette; scoped: no false-colour ramps, directional blur only on whip pans; lifts `slop:N11` for that vignette, entry moves and Palette), data (white hairlines of 1.2-2.6 px with a dark halo on real coordinates, dashes for history and claims, one warm accent for the single reading, a counter that rolls and lands with a small pop; when a claim is tested, a printed-map claim layer can be torn away by a scanner bar, entry Look, moves and Palette), sfx (telemetry: FM pings, a lock-on ping, dry grid ticks that subdivide to accelerate, noise scans rising for a rewind and falling forward, servo whines, a ratchet for a range line, entry Sound); serves when also: voice (a narrator: a calm analyst voice about 10 dB over the music, entry Sound).
- **Use when:** environmental change over time, distances and scale, investigations that test a claim or map against the ground, geography, infrastructure, land use, water and climate stories.
- **Do not use when:** no real, licensed imagery of the place exists (never fill with generated imagery presented as evidence); the subject is abstract, indoor, product or character-driven; the brief wants a glossy sci-fi interface or a playful illustrated tone.
- **Common mistakes:**
  - Using restricted commercial map or satellite imagery -> use public-domain or credited open imagery, burn a readable credit into the picture and list every asset in CREDITS.
  - Visible seams between scenes from different days -> one date per path, gain-match on stable land, feather overlaps, fill holes from the same year only.
  - Plumes, haze or clouds reading as features -> swap the scene for another date of the same year, prefer the dry season.
  - Grey mush at close range -> raise local contrast for that altitude only and dim outside the measured corridor.
  - Unsourced numbers -> log every number in the claims ledger `ice/assets/facts.json` (claim, source, quoted wording or method with scene IDs and dates; format owned by ice-assets); measure on the date you show.
- **Variation slots:** (illustration only)
  - Structure: a locked time-lapse of one place ending in a push-in; one feature followed from start to end with a reading at each stop; two places compared at one scale with whip pans.
  - Opening: a ground detail that climbs to reveal its scale; a black frame a scan line paints in; an oblique orbit with a searching reticle.
  - Ending: the climb back to orbit; a last measurement that lands and holds in silence; the claim layer redrawn to match the ground.
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/satellite-briefing/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### LiDAR Survey (`lidar-survey`)
- **Look:** Real airborne laser returns of a real place floating in near-black as a measured point cloud; a scan line or cutting plane passes through and the points condense into a solid, raking-lit surface measured with hairline instruments. Not a sci-fi hologram HUD (no invented interface), not satellite photography, not a blueprint, low-poly diorama or glass product render.
- **Medium-specific moves:** on ones at 24 fps, camera never stops, keyed as a smooth C1 spline in target / distance / heading / pitch; points sized in world units (0.12-0.3 m), soft opaque depth-tested discs with distance fog; one world-space front shared by points and surface (points ahead brighten, a thin seam glows, the surface fills in over tens of metres); a cutting plane by height above ground steps on the beat (eased steps, short holds) while returns above it lift away and a ruler tracks it; points appear behind a front or by height, never by random fade-in; points never wobble or get noise displacement; readings land with a small pop, traced features light up along their length in order; dim points outside the survey window instead of black; post: 2x render, high-threshold bloom only on fronts and seams, vignette, no grain.
  - Camera: locked nadir with slow push ("what a photo shows"); crane down tilting to oblique (entering the data); low oblique close to the cloud (10-20 degree pitch, 90-150 m) for volume; slow orbit while a plane does the work; rising pull-back for scale; lateral track along a feature; push to a section. Transitions are fronts and planes, never crossfades or hard cuts inside the cloud.
- **Palette:** near-black cold background, never pure black or gradient sky; points on one desaturated ramp by height or class (intensity modulates value, not hue, no rainbow ramps); front and seams one cool near-white; solid surface warm neutral; one amber accent reserved for what was found and measured. Example: background #05080A, canopy #1F3F35 to #5C9A7A to #E2F3DC, ground returns #C8B38C, surface #BDB5A6, front #D4FFF0, accent #FFB347, chart green #83C495.
- **Type and captions:** light condensed numerals for readings 110-130 px; monospace for instruments, coordinates, counts and sources 19-22 px; sans labels 24-36 px at medium weight in a sans covering every script on screen (illustration only: a CJK-covering sans for Chinese labels, a Latin grotesk for English-only labels; Arabic: the ice-arabic skill). Families chosen per the ice-direction skill, `references/type-pairing.md`. Instruments in one corner, readings in the right third with leader and source/method line, rolling counters report landing values. Subtitles as a bottom-left transcript strip (2 px rule, plate fading right), one line per language. Nothing below 19 px; subtitles >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s); labels >= CJK chars / 4.5 + 1.5 s, Latin letters / 15 + 1.5 s.
- **Sound:** FM pings, fine crackle as points arrive, noise sweeps tied to a front, ticks dividing 8ths to 16ths to 32nds, a descending pitched blip per peeled layer, ratchet while a counter rolls, ping when a reading lands; timpani, bass drum, gran cassa for condensations and cuts, reversed swell into the main impact; low-passed detuned-saw pads, pulse bass with 2nd/3rd harmonics; option: electronics while the subject is points, one organic wooden or bowed timbre (marimba, bowed vibraphone) once the surface exists; place ambience as J/L-cuts, near silence before the main reveal. Mix: voice ~10 dB over music with ducking, impacts outside the bus compressor, -14 LUFS, true peak <= -1.2 dBTP; calm plain narrator.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: depth (a real 3D point cloud: depth-tested discs sized in world units with distance fog, a low oblique close to the cloud for volume, entry moves and camera list), light (the points condense into a solid, raking-lit surface behind one shared front, entry Look and moves), rhythm (a cutting plane stepping on the beat with eased steps and short holds while a ruler tracks it; points appear behind a front or by height, never by random fade-in, and never wobble, entry moves), transition (fronts and planes; never crossfades or hard cuts inside the cloud, entry camera list), finish (scoped: a 2x render with high-threshold bloom only on fronts and seams and a vignette; no grain; lifts `slop:N11` for that bloom and vignette, entry moves), sfx (FM pings, fine crackle as points arrive, noise sweeps tied to a front, ticks dividing from eighths to thirty-seconds, a descending blip per peeled layer, a ratchet while a counter rolls, a ping when a reading lands, entry Sound), music (scoped: the entry's own score, low-passed pads and pulse bass while the subject is points, one wooden or bowed timbre once the surface exists, timpani and bass drum under condensations and cuts, ducked about 10 dB under the voice; the cutting plane steps on its beat, so the beat grid is a need of the style, not of the brief; leaving music out takes the user's own words, entry Sound and moves); serves when also: voice (a narrator: a calm, plain voice about 10 dB over the music, entry Sound).
- **Use when:** terrain, archaeology under forest, flood channels, landslides, site surveys, building or infrastructure scans, clearances and cross-sections, volumes and corridors.
- **Do not use when:** no real, licence-clean point data exists (never invent a cloud as evidence); the subject is people, products or emotion rather than measured space; the site is residential or private (privacy), or the brief wants bright, colourful or playful visuals.
- **Common mistakes:**
  - Dense cloud reads as a flat carpet from afar -> go close and low for volume, let distance fog it out.
  - Wrong coordinates or datum -> convert web-mercator copies to a local east/north/up frame and check the vertical datum against known ground.
  - Low vegetation left as stripes after a peel -> drop noise classes and finish the cut below 0 m.
  - Traced features presented as fact -> say "traced", keep the tracing in a data file, snap it to the data, leave faint candidates out.
  - Revealing on scan striping at grazing angles -> frame reveals elsewhere; striping is real texture.
- **Variation slots:** (illustration only)
  - Structure: a corridor flown end to end with three readings; two surveys of one place swapped by a front; one object scanned, cut and dimensioned.
  - Opening: a black frame the scan paints in line by line; a ground-level photo dissolving into its own returns; a top-down map tilting up into the cloud.
  - Ending: pull back to the whole survey tile; the removed layer returns as a ghost; the last reading holds while points fade to the instruments.
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/lidar-survey/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Microscope Story (`microscope`)
- **Look:** Real, licence-clean microscopy of a real specimen (ideally one whole-slide image pyramid), seen as it is, with one continuous change of scale from the glass slide to a single cell; every scale bar and reading comes from the image's own metadata. Not a grey satellite grade (the stain keeps its colour), not a sci-fi HUD with glowing crosshairs, not a chart, not a keynote.
- **Medium-specific moves:** camera is a similarity transform (zoom, roll, position) interpolated as a log spiral between keys so every push is a true dive to one point; eased moves, 2-5 % creep on holds, no frozen frame over 1 s; focus grammar: rack-in from soft at the start, a short breath (blur plus hint of field change) at each magnification change, a tilted focal-plane sweep when the camera lands; magnification changes only where plates really change, each with a turret click and the new magnification said once, large; scale bar shortens and jumps in a 1-2-5 sequence, field-of-view readout rolls; plates drawn at no more than ~1.45x downsampling, blending the next level between 2.2x and 1.45x; marks are made (dimension line grows from its centre, circle drawn as an arc with cardinal ticks, scan front counts pixels while the reading rolls and lands with a small spring); hairlines 1.5-2.5 px with halo; frosted glass panels for readings, instruments, subtitles, credit.
  - Camera: the dive (log zoom with roll); locked frame with slow creep; truck at one magnification (a second sample); pull back to frame two marks (verdict); focal-plane sweep (arrival); the climb (reverse dive, scale bar jumping back). Never cuts or crossfades between samples.
- **Palette:** glass warm paper white, graphite bench, stain keeps its true colour; one cool accent away from the stain's hues (mint against red and blue, amber against green or purple) only for the measured reading and counted pixels; near-black ink on glass, warm-white type on frosted panels over stain. Example: glass #F4F1EC, bench #0B0D0F to #2A3036, ink #181B1F, panel rgba(12,14,17,0.74), accent rgb(120,240,205) on stain / rgb(0,122,99) on glass.
- **Type and captions:** light condensed grotesk for readings and scale bar 40-72 px; monospace for instruments and pixel counts 20 px; sans labels 20-34 px in a sans covering every script on screen (Arabic: the ice-arabic skill); serif only for the title. Families chosen per the ice-direction skill, `references/type-pairing.md`. Instruments in one frosted corner panel (scale bar, field of view, magnification with a small nosepiece glyph turning a quarter per change), credit in the opposite corner. Readings in a frosted panel beside what they measure with a leader: small label, large accent number, method or raw counts below. Subtitles in a frosted bottom strip, one line per language, split rather than wrap. Nothing below 20 px; labels >= chars / 4.5 + 1.5 s (CJK).
- **Sound:** instrument foley (objective turret click, detent, small metal ring, focus servo, pen on glass per line, band-passed scan left to right with speeding counter ticks, soft filtered sweep for the focal plane); hard-mallet vibraphone in 8ths and 16ths, marimba ticks, bowed vibraphone and water glasses for pads, glockenspiel per landed reading, pulse bass with 2nd/3rd harmonics, timpani and bass drum under landings, open light harmony; quiet lab bed (fan, mains hum); silence before the verdict and before the climb. Mix: voice ~10 dB over music, -14 LUFS; calm exact narrator, numbers spelled out for TTS, digits on screen.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: light (focus grammar: a rack-in from soft at the start, a breath of blur at each magnification change, a tilted focal-plane sweep when the camera lands, entry moves), transition (one continuous change of scale: magnification changes only where the plates really change, each on a focus breath with a turret click; never cuts or crossfades between samples; when two sources must join, a cut on a focus breath, said on screen, entry moves, camera list and Common mistakes), data (every scale bar and reading from the image's own metadata: the scale bar jumping in a 1-2-5 sequence, the field-of-view readout rolling, dimension lines growing from their centre, readings landing with a small spring, entry Look and moves), finish (scoped: the stain keeps its true colour and is never graded grey; plates drawn at no more than about 1.45x downsampling, the dive ending at the scan's real resolution, entry Look, moves and Common mistakes); serves when also: voice (a narrator: a calm, exact voice with numbers spelled out for speech and written as digits, entry Sound).
- **Use when:** biology and pathology education, materials science, coatings, porosity, fibres, lab or instrument products, quality inspection, any "zoom in to see what it is made of" story with real scans.
- **Do not use when:** only non-commercial or rights-reserved micrographs are available (never a generated specimen); the claim would amount to a diagnosis or clinical score; two different samples would have to be faked as one continuous zoom.
- **Common mistakes:**
  - Zooming from one sample into a different one -> use one pyramid; if sources must join, cut on a focus breath and say so.
  - Wrong scale from untrusted metadata -> read pixel size from the file and verify a slide photo's resolution against the scan.
  - Upsampling mush beyond ~1.6x -> end the dive at the scan's real resolution.
  - Tile seams and manufacturer logos on the slide -> frame out seams, fill or crop logos (barcodes and IDs may stay).
  - Overclaiming -> a measured share is of the drawn region and stated threshold only; record the method in `ice/assets/facts.json`.
- **Variation slots:** (illustration only)
  - Structure: a single dive ending in one measurement; two regions or samples compared at one scale; a climb that starts on a cell and reveals the object.
  - Opening: the slide on the bench under moving light; a cell already in focus with the climb revealing what it belongs to; a blurred field racking into focus.
  - Ending: the title written on the glass; a last reading held in silence; the slide lifted out of frame.
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/microscope/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Archive Light Table (`archive-lighttable`)
- **Look:** Real archive objects (glass negatives, slides, letters, telegrams, clippings, drawings) at true proportions with their real damage, lying on a cool backlit light table; a loupe lands on one and inside the glass the object changes state (negative turns positive, a line of type lights up, a photo opens into depth). Clean, modern, quiet. Not a newsprint halftone dossier, no sepia or aged-paper grade, not satellite imagery, not charts, not a keynote.
- **Medium-specific moves:** 24 fps on ones, every camera parameter on smooth monotone curves (no overshoot); one world and one 2D camera (centre, zoom, roll) through which table and marks project; the camera glides across the table, slows so the loupe can land, then pushes; loupe lands and lifts with a fast ease-out (~0.4 s), scaling ~1.35x with a soft shadow; inside the lens ~2.5x magnification with pincushion (k ~0.16 at the rim) and slight per-channel colour fringes, black ring with bevel highlight and refracting acrylic skirt; negative transmission T = 0.035 + 0.9 x (1 - pos)^1.25 with a warm silver-gelatin tint; a develop front spreads from the lens centre with a thin warm edge; photograph space gains 2.5D depth from a ground-plane nearness map plus cut-out subject layers, driven by dolly and lateral shift; lines draw on in 0.3-0.6 s, text wipes in from its leader side, numbers count up and land with the voice; the table can switch off (ambient ~0.04) and flicker on; light vignette and fine moving grain, heavier inside photographs.
  - Camera: table glide (pan and roll); loupe landing and push into the lens; tracking along a line inside the lens (reading); photograph-space dolly with parallax ("being there"); rise out of the lens; pull back to the whole table. No cuts, fades to black or page turns between objects.
- **Palette:** near-white cool table and the archive's own silver greys and paper tans, never regraded into a scheme; one signal colour for marks, readings, anchors and the verdict, never as a fill or decoration; near-black ink on light areas, paper-white on dark caption plates; positives may be gently levelled, negatives stay as the glass gives them. Example: table #F7F9FB, ink #15171A, signal #FF5A1F, caption plates rgba(14,15,17,0.74).
- **Type and captions:** monospace for identifiers, dates and readings (17-34 px; readings 74-120 px); sans for labels 21-36 px, covering every script on screen (Arabic: the ice-arabic skill; a default-look face needs a D-entry reason, the ice-direction skill, `references/type-pairing.md` section 5); serif only for titles and the one name a photograph carries (60-76 px). Labels attach with an anchor dot and ring plus a vertical leader; a reading sits on a dark caption plate with label above and source (catalogue number or original wording) below. Subtitles in a bottom-centre caption strip on a dark plate with a 3 px signal-coloured edge, kept clear of the bottom 140 px for other marks; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Quote the source (a crop of the actual line) over paraphrase.
- **Sound:** light-table ballast hum (100 Hz and harmonics), starter clicks, room tone, giving way to the place's air inside a photograph; foley of the loupe on glass (clack with a high ring), paper sweeps for glides and lit lines, ticks for scales and counters, a pen stroke for a verdict, soft clicks as labels land; restrained small music (felt piano, a few strings, glass or bowed metal for moments of seeing), built by subdivision and register not drums; real silences before a reveal, first sound after is the loupe, the place or the verdict. Mix: music ~10 dB under voice, ducked, -14 LUFS; calm narrator stating what the object says.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: light (a cool backlit light table: negatives transmitting with a warm silver-gelatin tint, a develop front spreading from the lens centre, the table switching off and flickering on, entry Look and moves), composite (the loupe lands and lifts with a fast ease-out and a soft shadow; inside it about 2.5x magnification with pincushion, slight colour fringes and a bevelled ring, entry moves), depth (photograph space gains 2.5D depth from a ground-plane nearness map, driven by a small dolly and lateral shift, entry moves), cutout (upright subjects cut into layers with the ground filled behind them, so the 2.5D move never tears a person, entry moves and Common mistakes), finish (scoped: a light vignette and fine moving grain, heavier inside photographs; never a sepia, halftone or ageing overlay, the age being in the objects; the archive's own colours never regraded into a scheme, negatives as the glass gives them; lifts `slop:N11` for the vignette, entry moves and Common mistakes), transition (the camera glides across the table and the loupe lands and rises; no cuts, fades to black or page turns between objects, entry camera list), ambience (light-table ballast hum, starter clicks and room tone, giving way to the place's air inside a photograph, entry Sound); serves when also: voice (a narrator: a calm voice stating what the object says, entry Sound).
- **Use when:** company or brand history from real archives, founding stories, historical documents, museum and collection content, timelines of records, document-based investigations.
- **Do not use when:** the archive items are not public domain or licensed (never generate an archive object); the story needs a vintage, distressed look (this style forbids sepia and halftone); scans are too small to magnify.
- **Common mistakes:**
  - Over-magnifying a weak scan -> keep the lens interior under ~1.5 screen px per scan px; choose items by scan size.
  - The loupe sweeping over empty table -> lay objects close together, drop the loupe straight onto the object.
  - Shear in photograph space tearing people apart -> cut upright subjects into layers, fill behind, keep 2.5D moves small.
  - Labels scrolling away on a moving table -> hold the camera or fix the caption on screen.
  - Vintage creep -> no sepia, halftone or ageing overlay; the age is in the objects, not the grade.
- **Variation slots:** (illustration only)
  - Structure: a founding told as several objects in date order, each dive revealing one person; a single photograph explored in three dives (place, people, date on the back); a before/after pair of plans and photographs of one building.
  - Opening: the table switching on in the dark; a loupe already on a plate, rising to reveal the table; a handwritten date on an envelope filling the frame.
  - Ending: the index of everything on the table; the last photograph held in depth with one line of text; the table switching off, leaving one object glowing.
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/archive-lighttable/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Engineering Briefing Board (`briefing-board`)
- **Look:** One real whiteboard wall, filmed: window light on enamel, ghosts of erased marker, frame and tray, with cut-out photographs of real objects pinned a few millimetres off the board, magnetic tags, a ruler and stamps, and marker ink in three colours with fixed meanings. Not a hand-drawn whiteboard explainer on a flat page, not a data chart, not a keynote (no headers, centred bullets or page turns).
- **Medium-specific moves:** 24 fps on ones, nothing stepped; camera moves overlap with velocity proportional to u^a (1-u)^b (a, b about 1.5-4, b > a gives inertia), a hand-held breath <= 3 px, shake only on an impact; prints are lowered from the camera side, land, bounce and settle as the shadow tightens; magnets clack on, tags swing, removed magnets pop toward the lens; ink draws at hand speed, dimension lines land with an arrowhead tap; rejected ideas erased to a ~7 % ghost; parallax by height (board z 0, prints z 6-14, props z 8-26, tray z 70), 180-degree shutter motion blur, light grain; every 1-2 beats something lands, draws or swings; one continuous wall, every shot is a crop of the final poster-like wall seen whole only at the end. Never dissolves, scale-pops on text, bouncy UI easing or text fading in by itself.
  - Camera: ride a line (pen or dimension); tilt along projection lines (plan to elevation); slow push-in with slight Dutch tilt (tension); pull back and sideways (state a rule); whip with the line, overshoot, settle (an outlier); rise along a leader to the verdict; staircase framing (each new item with the previous result); judgement frame held >= 2.5 s; long pull-back to the whole wall. Transitions are moves only; no cuts, fades or zoom-to-fit.
- **Palette:** enamel ground with window falloff, warm grey wall; graphite ink for names, rules, facts; blue for water and measurement; red only for the decision (two or three red marks per frame at most, never on a red object); grey for drafting lines and hatching. Full colour belongs to the photographs; props stay neutral.
- **Type and captions:** single-weight marker face covering every script used (e.g. LXGW Marker Gothic, OFL, subset), content 100-160 board px, a verdict 230-400; sturdy sans for tags (one to three words), monospace for credits. Subtitles on a frosted acrylic strip bottom centre with a blue marker tick, one line per language, measured against a 1,500 px safe width; key content stays above the bottom 15 %. Hold subtitles >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s); board text in frame for CJK / 4.5 + other / 15 + 1.5 s; final-wall text >= 20 px on screen.
- **Sound:** room bed (air conditioning, traffic through the window) about 20 dB under voice; foley of felt-tip squeaks scaled to stroke length, nib taps, magnet clacks pitched per magnet in a row, suction pops, print landings (board thump, paper slap), ruler ring, eraser felt, stamp thud; subject's own sound as an early J-cut. Music: small rhythmic ensemble (bass, light kit, wooden percussion, low brass stabs or mallets for verdicts), no piano-and-strings pad; landings, stamps and arrows on the beat. Voice calm and briefing-room confident, 8-10 dB over music, -14 LUFS.

### Data Storytelling (`dataviz`)
- **Look:** A real, openly licensed dataset drawn mark by mark on warm cream paper (fixed-seed fibre noise, faint world-space dot grid) by a physical red-blue pencil, with handwritten notes pinned to the numbers; the chart is the camera, the rhythm and the plot. NOT a dashboard (one idea at a time), not a keynote with charts (no slides), never decoration; you change the framing, never the numbers.
- **Medium-specific moves:** pick the chart the data asks for (bars or ranked race for categories, scatter/bubble for two measures, map for place, unit/waffle for parts of a whole, slope/dumbbell for before-after, histogram/beeswarm for distributions, Sankey for flows, line/stripes/area for time); every movement is a chart operation (a mark lands with a short pop and thin ripple, suppress ripples when marks arrive closer than ~0.1 s; an axis grows when the narration first names the unit; rescale = stepped ratchet clicks, each step eased; morph = staggered sweep, notes fade and return as tiny marks); pencil acting: lift before each tap, land with shadow meeting the tip, tiny rebound, hurry as data densifies, tremble at a peak, can flinch, hesitate, exit; map marks to a musical grid and accelerate by dividing the beat, with a tempo whose finest subdivision lands on whole frames (24 fps = 1440 frames per minute); no fades to blank, no dissolves; one take on one sheet of paper, hard cuts rare.
  - Camera: 2D camera {x, y, zoom, roll}; extreme close-up on one mark following its leader; full view then dive to one mark; pull back as axes grow out of the marks; track the pencil with newest mark at ~60 % of width; pedestal with creeping Dutch angle for escalation; punch-in over 2 frames with decaying shake on a rescale; fast pull-out with roll and tremor for a rush; locked-off for a morph; lateral pan across the memory zone. Subjects >= 1/3 frame height at key moments; pin off-screen tick labels to a paper strip at the frame edge.
- **Palette:** colour encodes one thing only, the value: diverging data on a red-blue ramp centred on the mean of a meaningful reference period with a symmetric span, otherwise one sequential ramp; everything else is dark warm ink on cream, pale warm grey for grid and zero line; data dots get a thin ink ring. Blue lead = memories and human notes, red lead = records and warnings; a change of lead marks a turn. One accent may bypass the ramp for a non-data mark. Honesty beats drama: pale values stay pale, never stretch a ramp.
- **Type and captions:** subtitle is the figure caption: Newsreader text serif, ink, left-aligned with the chart, <= 2 lines, hairline rule above, small mono kicker for the range drawn so far, words appear on whisper timestamps over a paper band; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Title is the chart's headline in Newsreader in world space, fades when close. Handwriting in Caveat with per-glyph jitter and graphite grain; leaders are wobbly curves ending in an un-closed ring. Ticks in IBM Plex Mono. No captions over a signature chart operation or a silence.
- **Sound:** foley of graphite, paper and wood (tick-and-paper taps, handwriting scratches, ruler hiss for axes, paper puncture for a broken frame, wooden click for pencil flip, ratchet teeth for rescale, paper sweep for morph); sonification is the music: pitch = value, rhythm = time density, low values snapped to pentatonic, middle to semitones, extremes unquantized with detune growing away from the reference; low-pass-gate "plonk" timbre (sine + FM, index rising with value, wood to metal); dense passages quieter, decay <= 1.5 x the gap; bed of detuned-saw pad, soft kick, noise shaker, low-passed hats, sine sub, drones. True digital silence before a key mark, morph or verdict. Ambience follows places the notes evoke (J-cuts in, L-cuts out). Music ducked ~-11 dB, foley light, -14 LUFS, no grain; voice never over a rush, silence or morph.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: footage (scoped: the real material is a real, openly licensed dataset drawn mark by mark; the framing changes, never the numbers, entry Look and Do not use when), finish (scoped: warm cream paper with fixed-seed fibre noise and a faint world-space dot grid; no grain added, entry Look and Sound), composite (a physical red-blue pencil acting in the frame: it lifts before each tap, lands with its shadow meeting the tip, rebounds, hurries, trembles, flinches and exits, entry Look and moves), rhythm (marks mapped to a musical grid, accelerating by dividing the beat, the finest subdivision landing on whole frames, entry moves), transition (one take on one sheet of paper; no fades to blank and no dissolves, hard cuts rare, entry moves), music (sonification is the music: pitch is the value and rhythm the time density, entry Sound), silence (true digital silence before a key mark, a morph or the verdict, entry Sound); serves when also: voice (a narrator: scoped: never over a rush, a silence or a morph, entry Sound).
- **Use when:** any story carried by real numbers: growth curves, rankings, climate or health series, budgets, populations, election or usage data, a single case inside a big pattern.
- **Do not use when:** there is no real traceable dataset (it must not invent or extrapolate numbers); the brief needs a multi-metric dashboard or slide deck; the piece is mood or product beauty with no quantity to show.
- **Common mistakes:** columns selected by index -> select by header name and print every fact the narration claims; notes collide with later data -> data-space pins and a memory zone; frame break rescaled too fast is invisible -> hold the pierced state >= half a beat; right-aligned notes written wrong -> pencil hops to the first letter and writes left to right; helpers ignore fades -> multiply incoming alpha; sound tails leak across a hard silence -> render before/after in separate buffers and verify on decoded samples; pencil body covers fresh marks in dense passages -> steepen its angle; synthesized human sounds are uncanny -> use a soft non-human sound.
- **Variation slots:** structure: ranked race with one bar as the character, or a map that morphs into a unit count, or a before-after slope dialogue with one line unfinished (illustration only); opening: the finished chart worked backwards, or a pencil drawing of the measured object becoming the first mark, or an empty plot with a handwritten question (illustration only); ending: one mark close with its note, or the chart folding back into the object, or the pencil striking a title word and writing the true one (illustration only).

### Bold-outline Sticker Explainer (`19-sticker-explainer`)
- **Look:** Inspired by the Chinese explainer channel PaperClip. Cut-out photos with thick white outlines turned into stickers, laid out with flat icons and precise data charts on one huge canvas; the camera pans and zooms between information points. Serious, dense, beat-driven knowledge explainer.
- **Medium-specific moves:** every photo cut out with an 8-12px white outline (10px default) to unify mixed sources; calm blue-gray or off-white ground; one oversized canvas (for example 6000x3000 px) with a camera layer doing long-eased pans and zooms, one continuous shot through many points; chart motion: bars grow, numbers count up, paths draw on; every narration sentence brings a new visual element, fast pace; restrained 2-3 color palette with a monoline or geometric sans for a serious tone.
- **Technical route:** one huge DOM/SVG canvas with a camera transform (long eases), SVG charts, photo stickers with white outline.
- **Palette:** cool light ground plus one hot accent and dark text, 2-3 colors total; example seed palette (replace per brief): #E9EEF2, #FF5A36.
- **Sound:** marimba tech explainer bed, recipe `explainer` 112 BPM, D major, Imaj7 V vi7 IVmaj7; SFX: sticker slap and peel, paper slide, ticks on count-ups, pen strokes on path draws, whoosh on camera moves.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: cutout (every photo cut out with an 8-12 px white outline that unifies mixed sources, entry moves and Common mistakes), camera (one continuous shot across one huge canvas, long-eased pans and zooms between information points, entry moves), transition (the camera move across the canvas carries every change; never cuts between slides, entry Common mistakes), voice (a voiceover paces the film: every narration sentence brings a new visual element, entry moves and Do not use when), rhythm (fast and beat-driven: a new element on every sentence, entry Look and moves), sfx (sticker slap and peel, paper slides, ticks on count-ups, pen strokes on path draws, whooshes on camera moves, entry Sound).
- **Use when:** science, finance and economics explainers, corporate knowledge videos, data-backed narratives with voiceover.
- **Do not use when:** there is no voiceover or factual content to pace it; the brand wants emotional or cinematic mood; photo rights are unavailable.
- **Common mistakes:** mixed photo styles clash -> uniform 8-12px white outline; cuts between slides -> one camera move across a big canvas; static charts -> grow bars, roll numbers, draw paths; too many colors -> 2-3 max.

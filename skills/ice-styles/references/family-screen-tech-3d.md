# Family: screen, tech and 3D

Interface-led films, sci-fi interfaces, terminal and retro-screen looks, glass and gradient light, and rendered 3D. Read `style-method.md` first. Every entry's examples are illustration only.


## Shared rules for this family

- This family carries the highest risk of the default AI look (blue and purple glow, glass cards, neon gradients). Every colour must come from the brand or the brief with a written reason; the blue-purple default-look gate is owned by ice-qa and the colour decision by ice-direction.
- Real product UI, recordings and screenshots beat invented UI. Invented UI is original, drawn in code, never a copy of a real operating system or app chrome.
- Use at most two of these wrappers in one film: glow mesh, frosted glass, gradient text, centred blur-in, floating cards. Glass and glow belong only where the brand supports them.
- Precision styles land on a grid and a beat; nothing floats or drifts on its own. 3D camera moves re-render the scene; never scale a rendered bitmap.
- Every object, character or scene this family draws in code follows `code-illustration-craft.md` (three tones under one light, cast and contact shadows, a texture layer, one line policy, named groups); section 8 there says how this family renders each rule.

## Interface and keynote

### Dark Tech Keynote (`dark-keynote`)
- **Look:** A keynote-grade launch film for software where the interface is the star: near-black stage with a radial lift at upper centre, one large cool glow at single-digit opacity, 50-55 % corner vignette, hairline grid (white ~3 %, every 4th line ~6 %) on a 48 px module, original code-drawn UI (rounded windows, notification cards, file icons, tiles), soft cool light and one brand accent. NOT a glass product render (no physical object), not a screencast (no real OS or apps, never traffic-light buttons, menu bars or docks), not a hologram HUD (no scanlines), not a chart film; no grain.
- **Medium-specific moves:** 24 fps on ones, no stepping, no boil; everything lands on the grid and the beat, moves start a beat early; fast-out easing with one small overshoot (back, s ~1.3) and immediate settle, no floaty sine drift; spawns typed and short (drop + bounce, fling, slide-in, pop, 0.2-0.3 s), one motion per element type; flights on curved paths overshooting along the final tangent, same-type elements bend the same way and never cross; a living element can act (anticipation >= 8 frames, 2-frame contact, spring follow-through; cursor blink as metronome); disappearing is designed (retract one part per beat, CRT-off squash), nothing just fades; light reveal (soft band, only lit pixels turn on, glint <= 20 %, <= ~1.3 s); 2.5D camera with near cards blurring, strip perspective rotY <= ~24 degrees; transitions are UI expand/collapse only, an expansion on a beat starts one frame early so the beat frame is ~80 % open; no fades, no blank frames.
  - Camera: locked extreme close-up on a tiny UI element; continuous log-space pull-back keyed per bar; push-in through a frozen frame; one-beat whip pull-out; log-zoom dive into an element + match cut; half-second inserts expanding from their source; near-locked frame while light sweeps; slow lateral track across a UI plane; top-down plan over the grid; return to an earlier framing. Dutch angle and wobble only for disorder.
- **Palette:** cool, slightly blue neutral ladder only (near-black stage, three raised surface steps with 1 px ~8 % white border and inner top highlight, text in three greys); one saturated luminous accent with its own glow reserved for what the product is or does (if two things are accent-coloured, one is wrong); a problem red may exist only while the problem exists and never returns after the turn; desaturated secondary hues only as category trails in motion. Example accents, one per film: lime `#B7F34A`, electric violet `#8B7CFF`, signal orange `#FF7A1A`.
- **Type and captions:** roles, not names: a UI-style grotesk for UI, captions and headlines (the product's own UI font when the film shows a product); the same grotesk or a tighter cut at about 600 with tabular figures for giant numbers (tracking ~-4.5 %); a monospace for labels, filenames and status. Families chosen per the ice-direction skill, `references/type-pairing.md`; a default-look face needs a D-entry reason. One line per screen; the big number gets its own screen with no caption over it. Captions are a UI toast: bottom-centre dark translucent pill, 1 px light border, soft shadow, the UI grotesk at about 500 ~40 px, small accent "speaking" dot, rise + fade ~0.16 s; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Text typed in frame is not captioned twice (srt only); lift content so toasts never clip it.
- **Sound:** tuned percussion and pads (marimba, vibraphone mallet or bowed, glockenspiel, celesta, synth pad or drone, low sine pulse, shaker, plucked synth, felt piano), clean and dry; music as visible process (phasing, additive one note per bar, subtractive, dividing the beat, one chord everything lands on), picture spawns on score notes. Two sound families = story: disorder scattered (detuned by tens of cents, random pans, varied timbres), the product one family (felt ticks, glass taps, one mode, centred). Foley: cursor tick, low-profile keys, notification glass, file plop, window whoosh, glitch, stacking thumps, felt mallet + sine drop press, light-sweep shimmer, digit grains, CRT-off. Ambience: quiet room, computer fan, rising coil whine kept down under voice. Digital zero on every track when the screen pauses; J/L cuts across UI transitions. Music ducks ~8 dB, foley ~4 dB; -14 LUFS; confident restrained voice, short declaratives.
- **Use when:** software, SaaS, developer tools, apps, AI features, platform launches, release announcements, a single headline metric, before/after workflow claims.
- **Do not use when:** there is a physical product to show (use a product render style); the brief needs the real product UI recorded or walked through; warm, handmade, playful or character-driven tone is wanted; data needs a full chart.
- **Common mistakes:** strip perspective of a semi-transparent canvas shows vertical seams -> project the opaque surface, then light it in screen space; hard white glint reads as a sticker -> soft front, glint <= 20 %; nearest-slot snapping flickers -> typed destinations, curved paths, trails; holes in a grid read as mistakes -> generate by rule and trim or pad to exactly the slot count; a flat tinted rectangle for "closing" looks like a placeholder -> squash the real surface.
- **Variation slots:** structure: a release checklist countdown ending on "shipped", or split-screen old vs new workflow, or a zoom ladder from one pixel to a fleet of devices (illustration only); opening: the number first on black, or a plan view of an empty grid filling slot by slot, or a hard insert storm (illustration only); ending: one accent tile on an empty grid, or the product running quietly in a wide with no text, or a single confirmation toast then darkness (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/dark-keynote/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Living Screencast (`living-screencast`)
- **Look:** A product walkthrough (typically 45-75 s) that looks like a real screen recording but is fully redrawn in 2D (HTML/CSS/SVG), with a low-res big-square-pixel mascot living inside the high-res anti-aliased interface; the clash of two resolutions is the look. The cursor is the user, the mascot is the software's agency. NOT a dark keynote (real product UI, no black stage), not a pixel-art game (only the mascot and its props are pixels), not a real capture (no screenshots).
- **Medium-specific moves:** 30 fps, UI on ones, mascot position stepped to its pixel grid (snaps to half-pixels); mascot stands only on top edges of real UI elements, position measured from the live DOM every frame; mascot acting: anticipation crouch, take-off stretch, tucked legs, landing squash with damped bounce and dust, idle breathing on the beat, irregular blinks, walk by leg alternation with sprite flip; eyes carry the acting (neutral, left, right, up, down, wide, happy, shut, blink), arms down/up/wave; cursor smoothed with spring-eased paths and a small push on click, never teleports; every mascot action triggers a real UI state change, every user action is a cursor move, click or keystroke on the HUD; UI animates only like the real product; motion blur only on whips, applied to an untransformed wrapper; one continuous take inside the screen, zoom >= 1, cuts only under chapter transitions; transitions are chapter pill wipes, pixel wipes, a theme reveal circle; never dissolves.
  - Camera: push-in to cursor or caret; slow drift at rest; whip-pan between panes with blur; push-in + vignette freeze on the one promise; rack focus between panes; pull back to the full window; follow the mascot along a list; split-window reframe.
- **Palette:** the product's own interface colours rule, following its real light and dark themes exactly; mascot keeps the brand mascot colour or one saturated hue no UI element uses; overlays are neutral frosted glass with at most one accent from the product; a theme switch can be a story event.
- **Type and captions:** UI type follows the product's own UI font; when it is not available, a licence-free family of the same class (grotesk, monospace, or a serif for editorial UIs) chosen per the ice-direction skill, `references/type-pairing.md` (a default-look face needs a D-entry reason). Information layers only: chapter pill (01-04), a keystroke HUD, frosted-glass caption pills at the bottom, optional time-lapse tag (e.g. "4x"). Captions never over the element discussed or under the mascot; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Any title is diegetic (empty-state heading, window title, notification).
- **Sound:** two resolutions in the music: a hi-fi layer (piano, upright or electric bass, brushed or tight kit, glockenspiel) is the world, a chiptune square/pulse voice is the mascot; they trade, duet or merge. Options: low-passed "night" band for dark theme, tom fill + crash at chapter seams, drop drums for tension, mascot notes on words it lands on, stereo split for split screens, held pad under a freeze. Foley: keyboard thock + click with human jitter, trackpad clicks, pane swishes, popup pops, notification chimes, CI ticks, shutter; mascot steps and landings are 8-bit. Short drop to room tone before the key promise. Calm voice, one feature per line, product names whisper-verified, foley kept off word onsets; music ducked; -14 LUFS.
- **Use when:** real software walkthroughs, feature tours, onboarding videos, release notes, developer tools and AI agents where the software's work should be visible, product demos with a brand mascot.
- **Do not use when:** the product UI or documentation is not available to redraw faithfully (invented features are forbidden); the brief wants an abstract launch mood rather than the real interface; the subject is a physical product or has no interface.
- **Common mistakes:** invented features or renamed modes -> check every button, mode and shortcut against the product docs; mascot floats or drifts -> measure the DOM every frame, stand only on top edges; mascot covers text or sits under a caption -> reserve a text-free lane; fake blur depth on text to be read or zoom past the screen -> rack focus only on unread panes, zoom >= 1; a hand on screen -> the cursor is the user; motion blur smears the sprite -> blur an untransformed wrapper, only on whips; a keypress masks a product name -> shift foley off word onsets and recheck; TTS mispronounces the product name -> try voices and spellings, verify with whisper.
- **Variation slots:** structure: a bug hunt in one chapter, or one user's day with theme and music shifting by hour, or a side-by-side race old way vs new way (illustration only); opening: mid-task with the mascot already acting, or the mascot inside a landing notification, or an empty state with a blinking caret (illustration only); ending: the mascot sleeping on the finished item as the window dims, or the app closing with the mascot left standing, or the mascot riding a shared result out of frame (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/living-screencast/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

## Sci-fi interface (merged: two sources)

Both sources agree: wireframe and line UI drawn on in hierarchy order, numbers that jitter then settle on the true value, lock brackets that snap in, glow and scanlines only as a finishing pass, all data seeded and deterministic. The motion-design variant is a 10 s target-lock piece; the film variant scans and explains a spec.

### Sci-fi Hologram HUD (`hologram-hud`)
- **Look:** A scene style for any object with parts: the subject is always a light wireframe above a round perspective projector pad on a near-black hue-tinted stage, read as scanned data, with three depth layers (parallax dot grid, subject with pad and floor, foreground HUD of corner brackets, faster-parallax rulers, status text). Additive ~1.3 px strokes with depth fog (near bright, far ~25 %) and two low-res glow passes; real CAD topology with every part physically connected. NOT a type-driven keynote, not a drafting blueprint, not a product render; it may gain translucent Fresnel faces once, lines still on top.
- **Medium-specific moves:** everything on ones at 24 fps, only glyph scrambling steps every frame; scan plane grows the model; target lock: oversized, rotated 45 degrees, faint, snaps to fit in ~0.2 s cubic ease-out, flashes white 3 frames on the downbeat; local explode along each piece's assembly axis (shell slides on its own axis, contents stay and glow, open over a beat or two, close fast); leader: anchor ring, diagonal, horizontal to the card in ~0.5 s; rolling value locks characters right-to-left and turns accent on the downbeat with a one-frame flash; finished values dock as chips in a side rail until the end; camera follows the box with cubic ease starting before the next lock, real sub-frame motion blur (180 degree shutter) only inside a whip while the HUD stays sharp; solid hologram (Fresnel x scrolling interlace, thickening lines, climbing light wave, a beam with rays and dust) at most once; no cuts or fades inside a scene, only scan plane in/out and a loupe iris; a 3-frame glitch at most once on the biggest change.
  - Camera: orbit camera (yaw, pitch, distance, narrow fov) with a screen-space target position; high 3/4 slow orbit craning down; top-down plan; locked eye-level profile; push-in then drift 3/4; whip following the target box; orbit around a part's own axis; lateral dolly along a long part; pull back + loupe frame-in-frame; crane to eye level; turntable 360; near-static slow dolly for reading. Subject >= 1/3 frame height; cards one side, chip rail the other, subtitles bottom centre, loupe above them.
- **Palette:** one cold hue plus one warm accent, chosen from the subject (e.g. mint/orange, violet/lime, ice-blue/red); hue carries lines, HUD and fills; the accent is spent only on locked values, turning a number accent at the frame it becomes true; white is heat (near-white hot edges and one-frame flashes for what is active); one object may keep its own colour only for a brand mark.
- **Type and captions:** Rajdhani all caps (Light for big numbers and product name with wide tracking, Medium/SemiBold for labels), Share Tech Mono for micro data; numbers monospaced so rolls never jitter, decimal point a small square. Every text block sits on its own dark plate (translucent near-black, 1 px border, corner ticks), never on the wireframe. Subtitles are HUD: dark plate with corner brackets and moving "voiceprint" bars, near-white Rajdhani Medium bottom centre, in just before the voice, hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). No title card: the name rolls in like a value, then code, category, tagline.
- **Sound:** music first on a grid (a bar = one information beat), locks on downbeats; synth palette: saw/square arpeggio with ping-pong delay (scooped 1-4 kHz for voice), square pulse, 8th bass, synth hats and claps, detuned saw pads; density follows information (pick two or three: half-time, one beat of true digital silence with no UI blips, a drop of sub glide + wide saw chord for the biggest change, a layer per part, longer note values, key change, sustained tone under a hold). Foley: light = sines and FM blips in key; machines = servo slides, pneumatic puffs, hydraulic hiss, metal tinks (J-cuts); lock = thump + clack + two-tone beep; rolls tick at frame rate. Ambience: projector hum (40/80/120 Hz) and room tone. Calm confident voice; duck music and foley with a held envelope, lock transients escape; -14 LUFS.
- **Use when:** spec walkthroughs (30-40 s, 2-5 parts, title >= 5 s, value >= 2.5 s then docked, final sheet >= 3 s), voiceless loop screens (20-30 s, 3 s per spec, 5 s final, last frame = first), teardowns (40-60 s, 4-6 s per part), upgrades (old value dim, new value accent, 3 s each), assembly order (one bar per part); hardware, devices, vehicles, machines, engineered products.
- **Do not use when:** the subject has no meaningful parts or numbers; the brief needs a photoreal or material-beauty render; warm, organic or handmade tone is wanted; the content is narrative-driven rather than information-driven.
- **Common mistakes:** target boxes balloon -> use rest pose, skip internal pieces, no box on long attachments; long diagonal leaders point at the wrong part -> ring on the part body and flash the part; two parallel moving boxes don't read as an explode -> shell slides on its axis, contents stay and glow; generic boxes don't read as the part -> give its characteristic silhouette; voice masked by foley -> duck foley too and hold the duck through word gaps; UI blips break a silence -> check cues against silence windows; a whip written inside the next segment plays as a hard cut -> start it in the previous one.
- **Variation slots:** structure: an unnamed loop screen cycling sensors, or a teardown one layer deeper per shot to a single hot core, or an upgrade with two versions sharing the pad and numbers rolling old to new (illustration only); opening: one part locked in close-up with the rest scanning out, or a 2D silhouette extruding into the wireframe, or a top-down plan with the object arriving as its footprint (illustration only); ending: one value at eye level as the wireframe dims, or parts knolled into a labelled top-down layout, or a power-down until only the pad glows (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/hologram-hud/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Cyberpunk HUD / FUI (Fictional UI) (`22-hud`)
- **Look:** Sci-fi heads-up display packaging: cyan and orange neon wireframes, target-lock brackets, radar sweeps and small monospace data streams. The default tech skin for gadget reviews, game edits, aerospace explainers and AI demos.
- **Medium-specific moves:** wireframe growth via SVG stroke-dasharray/dashoffset, elements unfolding by hierarchy with staggered frames; ring radar: conic-gradient sweep sector rotating in a loop + blinking target dots; number roll: random values jump for 3-5 frames then settle on the real value; typewriter text with blinking cursor; lock box: four corner brackets shrink from scale 1.4 to 1.0 onto the target with fast-out, slow-in; crosshairs, hexagon grid, wireframe hologram; global outer glow + slight chromatic aberration + scanline overlay; seeded random data.
- **Technical route:** SVG/Canvas FUI layers + WebGL wireframe hologram, seeded random data, glow and scanline post pass.
- **Palette:** cyan primary with orange alert accent on near-black; example seed palette (replace per brief): #00E5FF, #FF6A00.
- **Sound:** darksynth, recipe `darksynth` 110 BPM (96-124), D phrygian, i bII i bVII; SFX: HUD beeps, scan sweeps, lock-on (sync on the lock), data chirps, HUD open and alert, laser zaps, glitch bursts.
- **Use when:** tech reviews, game edits, aerospace or defense explainers, AI and security product demos, data-monitoring stories.
- **Do not use when:** the brand is warm, organic or family-oriented; the audience needs calm readable information; the piece is about food, fashion or lifestyle.
- **Common mistakes:** numbers appear instantly -> 3-5 frames of random jumps then settle; lock box just fades in -> brackets 1.4 -> 1.0 fast-out slow-in; everything draws at once -> hierarchical staggered draw-on; random data changing each render -> seed it; flat lines -> glow, slight aberration, scanlines.
- **Source:** Vincentwei1021/mg-styles-15 `prompts/22-hud.md` (MIT).

## Terminal and retro screen

### ASCII / CRT Terminal (`ascii-crt`)
- **Look:** One monochrome terminal screen where everything (titles, landscapes, faces, rooms, subtitles) is real glyphs on a monospace character grid, chosen by ink density and lit by P3 amber (or P1 green, or P4 white) phosphor on dark curved glass with scanlines, bloom and afterglow; cold, patient, machine-calm, one warm surprise. Never an image with an ASCII filter; not hacker green rain, not pixel art (no sprites or free pixels), not a sci-fi vector HUD.
- **Medium-specific moves:** the only camera is grid scale (bigger characters push in, smaller pull out; title ~20 columns, terminal 80, picture 200+; a push can go into one cell that holds its own sub-grid); characters never move off-grid except in a declared transformation; only grid scale, CRT physics and transformation particles move continuously; typing, scrolling and counters jump cell to cell, rollers flip digits fast then slowing; typing speed is performance (machine output even at ~10-40 ms/char, dramatic lines on note values, human typing uneven with pauses and bursts); particles spawn from random points inside glyphs, small, fade in, ease to their cell and flash on landing; push to a character: hold where the glyph reads, then push until its inner text reads with neighbours dimmed; afterglow decay tau ~0.1 s; ~20 density levels from dim/normal/bold glyphs with dither, blank cells below a threshold (negative space makes it readable); far sparse and dark, near dense and bright with a thin bright rim; fine scanlines, slight barrel curvature, ~1 % flicker, no RGB grille; power on as dot, line, frame.
- Camera list: static 80-column terminal; push in (cells grow); pull out (cells shrink); push into a single glyph; buffer scroll; clear screen or hard redraw as a cut; text becomes image as glyphs fall and the grid shrinks; power on/off and degauss; pull back off the glass into a drawn room. Text stays inside the curvature safe area, LOG strip kept clear; transitions happen on the screen (clear, redraw, scroll, power), never a crossfade between screens.
- **Palette:** One phosphor carries everything in its own brightness ramp (P3: dim deep orange to amber to overexposed near-white); brightness tools only: dim, normal, bold, inverse video; at most one soft second colour for one emotional object, switched on once on a downbeat and not casually taken back. Examples: amber screen with an ice blue-white for something far away; green screen with a warm pink for a living thing; white screen with pale gold for a memory.
- **Type and captions:** One monospace bitmap-style OFL font (VT323), cell 0.4 em x 0.8 em (1:2), prerendered glyph atlas with a stroke for beam spread. Subtitles are terminal output in a reserved bottom LOG strip outside the zooming grid: dim separator, inverse-video tag, prompt, the line in uppercase ~45 px, typed word by word in sync with the voice; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s); when silent it can show system state (progress bar, waiting message). On-screen story text is picture, marked as [SCREEN] in the .srt; titles typed with key clicks as a filename, command or header.
- **Sound:** Original analog-synth score, never chiptune: band-limited saw and square oscillators, resonant 4-pole ladder filter with moving cutoff, filter envelopes, square-wave sequencers, slow tape wow (+- a few cents), long reverb pads, gated reverb hits, sub-bass drone, sample-and-hold noise, detuned unison lead. Options: grow one arpeggio as an image forms, hold the only major chord for one moment, drop to digital zero on a shock, open a chord on the colour change. Silence is the main instrument (room tone under typing and a blinking cursor). Foley: relay click and degauss hum with thump, buckling-spring keys (click, ping, thock; duller space, heavier Enter), teletype ticks, modem and phone-line tones, square-wave error beeps and bell, tiny high tick clusters for particles, dot-matrix printer or tape drive, room tone with a very quiet flyback whine. Machine voice processed (light ring mod, short comb, telephone band-pass, soft saturation) and checked for intelligibility; music ducked ~-8 dB; -14 LUFS; no grain at mux.
- **Use when:** logs, launch days, rescues and migrations told as timestamps, chat or night-shift stories, archives reopened, manifestos or letters that become images, names made of many smaller names, tech and infrastructure brands wanting a calm retro register.
- **Do not use when:** the brand needs full colour or photographic product imagery; warm handmade or festive tone; audiences need fine visual detail a glyph grid cannot carry; mobile-first tiny viewing where dense glyphs turn to noise.
- **Common mistakes:** inverse video via alpha-only cut fails on upload -> opaque black canvas and letters cut with a black atlas; grayscale negative not aligned to the grid drifts across columns -> map exactly by cell width and samples; wrong cell centre -> cells are 2x cell-width tall, centre at (j + .5) x 2cw; every dark pixel becomes a character -> threshold, gamma ~1.25, local contrast; noise-textured subjects read as blobs -> silhouettes, sparse dark background, dot-only shadow side; particles from letter centres stack into bars -> random points inside the glyph, small, faded in; stroked atlas glyphs close counters -> sample a thin fill-only glyph for masks; lazily built schedules never reach the sound export -> build them before export; power-on at 0 still draws the centre dot -> multiply the dot by exposure.
- **Variation slots:** structure: a chat log across one night with timestamps as scene headers, or a system manual with one page going wrong, or one machine's day as scheduled jobs each drawing a picture (illustration only); opening: mid-scroll stopping on one line, or a crash dump then rewind to its cause (illustration only); ending: the buffer scrolls away until only the prompt remains, or the cursor simply stops blinking on a held final state (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/ascii-crt/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Synthwave, vaporwave and VHS (merged: two sources)
Neon perspective grid, striped sun, chrome type, VHS damage. The motion-design variant is a music-led title piece; the art-history card is for era segments. VHS damage is a seasoning (1-2 glitch drops per second at most), never a full-film filter.

### Retro: 80s Synthwave / VHS / Y2K (`10-synthwave`)
- **Look:** Wholesale quotation of a media era: neon perspective grid, sunset gradient and chrome type (synthwave); scanlines, color offset and tape noise (VHS); liquid chrome, acid green and purple, skeuomorphic system windows and low-fi 3D (Y2K).
- **Medium-specific moves:** scrolling one-point perspective grid looping vertically; multi-band gradient sunset sphere; double glow: tight inner 4px bright + wide outer 30px low opacity; VHS chain: RGB split (red and blue channels offset 1-2px) -> scanlines (2px spacing, 10% black bars) -> wave distortion jitter (1-2 random glitch frame drops per second) -> 4:3 rounded-corner mask; Y2K chrome type: very high-contrast multi-stop metal gradient + bevel highlight + environment reflection, with lens flare; Y2K motion: pop-up windows, cursor clicks, window drags; unstable frame rate: key passages drop to 15 fps or reverse 2 frames for tape stutter.
- **Technical route:** WebGL (perspective grid, sun, mountain shader) + canvas chrome text + CSS/SVG glow, then a VHS post pass.
- **Palette:** neon magenta, cyan and purple on deep navy or black, sunset orange to pink gradient, chrome silver; Y2K variant swaps to acid green and purple; no hex seed given.
- **Sound:** synthwave with lead, recipe `synthwave` 100 BPM (84-118), A minor, i VI III VII; SFX: VHS noise, head switch, CRT on/off, tape stop, static, gated snare hits.
- **Use when:** music and gaming content, nostalgia campaigns, streetwear and youth drops, retro tech jokes.
- **Do not use when:** the brand is modern-minimal or serious; legibility of small text is critical; the audience has no connection to the era.
- **Common mistakes:** single blurry glow -> double glow 4px inner, 30px outer; VHS as a flat overlay -> full chain RGB split, 2px scanlines, glitch drops, 4:3 mask; smooth frame rate throughout -> drop key passages to 15 fps or 2-frame reverse; flat gray chrome -> multi-stop high-contrast gradient with bevel.
- **Source:** Vincentwei1021/mg-styles-15 `prompts/10-synthwave.md` (MIT).

### Vaporwave / cyber neon with VHS (`26_vaporwave`, implemented, quality 3 stars)
- **Look:** deep purple night, magenta and cyan neon tubes with glow, sliced retro sunset, wireframe mountains, scrolling perspective grids, marble bust, Win95 dialog, VHS chroma split, tracking noise and scanlines.
- **Medium-specific moves:** render the whole frame off-screen, then run a VHS post pass (counter or UI drawn on top afterwards). Neon tube = same path stroked thick with shadow blur for glow, then a 2-3px near-white core. Sunset: yellow to orange to magenta vertical gradient with 7 horizontal slits cut out, slit width 2 + q^2 x 16 by depth, rising 0.9/s. Floor grid: vanishing point (960,600), horizontal lines y = 600 + 520/d with d = k + 1 - phase, phase = 1.3t mod 1, line width growing near camera. Neon sign glyphs drawn as polylines (do not rely on fonts); per-letter flicker when hash(floor(30t), k) < 0.07, one letter extra-flaky (22% when frame mod 17 < 5), unlit state drawn as dark purple tube. Rim light two-tone: stroke figure parts twice, pink clipped to the window half, cyan to the other. VHS pass: split RGB by multiplying copies with pure red, green, blue, recombine additively on black with offset 3 + 1.5 sin(7t), occasional +6 when hash < 0.12; tracking band 70px tall rolling at (520lt+200) mod (H+160), rows every 6px displaced +/-30 sin(pi y/bh) plus 90 noise lines; scanlines as a 4x4 tile (row 0 alpha 0.28, row 2 alpha 0.12); OSD "PLAY" with a hand-drawn triangle flashing every 16 frames, date and timecode advancing. Motif loops: sun slats rising, grid rolling forward, sign flicker, tracking band, palms swaying. Signature transition: VHS tear plus roll (first half old, second half new; p 0.38-0.62 loses sync, whole frame scrolls with a 40px black bar; tear bands 6-54px high pulled from A or B with probability p for B, shifted +/-130 sin(pi p); magenta and cyan channel copies screened at +/-14px; 160 snow lines; 0.30s, about 88ms).
- **Palette:** magenta `#ff4fd8`, hot pink `#ff2f8f`, cyan `#3ff6ff`, deep purple `#12052a / #2a0b4a`, wall `#0d0322 -> #4a0f6a`, sun yellow `#ffd84a`, orange `#ff8a3a`; garment lavender `#8f7cf6`.
- **Sound:** not specified in source; score in instruments native to the period or medium (slowed synth and tape textures fit the medium).

### Glass Product Render (`glass-product`)
- **Look:** Launch-film rendering of a single fictional hero object of clear and frosted glass, metal and light floating in an infinite black studio: long strip lights behind and beside turn clear glass into a thin white outline with rainbow dispersion edges, internals show through like a display case, light travels inside, caustics spread on a black gloss floor that fades with no horizon line, slow exploded views snap shut on a downbeat. - **Medium-specific moves:** studio = a tiny black scene of emissive strip planes re-baked into the environment every frame, never a photo-studio HDRI: tall side strips behind (silhouette), a wide far strip (horizon rim), a soft top box (volume for frosted glass and metal), a weak front card (off when concave glass faces camera), a top-front key only when parts must be read one by one, a pulse-driven accent card, and the sweep (a tall strip on an arc behind the camera, azimuth = camera + pi) so highlights, refraction and dispersion move physically, never a painted 2D highlight; objects lift out of cradles tilted toward camera and settle upright, lids hinge at the back as a backlit halo; pulses and sweeps are beat-grid events; cut only on beats, no spatial continuity needed between shots; transitions are beat cuts or a sweep passing through black, never dissolves or wipes.
  - Camera: sweep over a dark object with slow push; slow high 3/4 orbit; macro rack focus from surface to a part inside; side macro orbit; exploded view with axis ~40 degrees to view, orbiting; straight-on front with slight push; top-down macro; top-down on the floor; low orbit under the object; slow pull-back into negative space for type. One hero, centred or on a strong diagonal, never cropped in a wide.
- **Palette:** neutral world: black, greys, white of strip reflections, metals' own tints; one accent colour family reserved for light (emission, caustic rings, the dispersion line on UI), optionally drifting between two neighbouring hues with age or energy (e.g. ice blue to soft violet, amber to rose, green to cyan), chosen from what the product does, never from a brand; emitters that only illuminate inside frosted glass stay cool-white and dim; dispersion rainbows only at thick glass edges and in the caustic, never as gradients on surfaces or type.
- **Type and captions:** subtitles are a slab of frosted glass: a centred rounded bar filled with the live frame blurred and brightened, a little cool white, thin highlight stroke and an accent line on top, in a licence-free grotesk chosen per the ice-direction skill, `references/type-pairing.md`, light weight ~36-44 px near-white; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Titles in the same grotesk at ultra-thin weight 200, very large (100-140 px) with very wide tracking, centred in the upper third, a diagonal glint sweeping the letters in sync with the sweep light; small tracked caps (18-20 px) for kickers and credits. Product name and slogan are title cards, not subtitle lines.
- **Sound:** minimal electronic, never piano and strings: bowed-glass or wet-finger tones, granular crystal clouds, glass bells and FM glass plucks, a filtered tick grid for precision, warm pad, sub drone, soft short kicks and claps, sine sub-bass with pitch envelope, soft saturation and slides, noise and saw risers, a high glass ding; minor and modal colours suit the dark studio. ### 3D Render / CGI Motion Design (`04-3d-render`)
- **Look:** Solid rendered materials (soft rubber, glass, metal, cloth) with cloner arrays and physics simulation, studio lit with shallow depth of field. The default for premium product films and brand idents.
- **Medium-specific moves:** PBR materials, shallow depth of field (f/1.8 feel), soft studio lighting; cloner arrays driven by random or plain effectors for group wave motion; material trio: subsurface scattering (soft-rubber look) + AO + HDRI three-point lighting; long-tail easing where the first 20% of the curve covers 80% of the distance and the end is very slow, with motion blur; 24/25 fps cinema rate (versus 30 fps for 2D); structure: the product is the axis, particles, fluid or cloth choreographed around it; candy-colored soft-rubber look.
- **Technical route:** headless 3D DCC script render (path tracer with denoise, or real-time engine if quality holds) to PNG sequence, then encode; optional HTML overlay pass for typography.
- **Palette:** candy pastel soft-rubber colors against a neutral studio sweep, one hero accent; no hex seed given.
- **Sound:** product ambient or EDM-pop, recipe `ambient` flavor product 100 BPM, E major (alternative `pop` 122 BPM, C major); SFX: soft impacts, whooshes on long-tail moves, shimmer hit on the product reveal.
- **Use when:** product hero shots (phones, drinks, shoes, devices), brand idents, premium launch moments.
- **Do not use when:** the render budget or time is tight; the brief needs lots of on-screen text or data; a flat or hand-made brand voice is required.
- **Common mistakes:** linear or symmetric ease -> 20% time for 80% distance, slow tail; 30 fps snappy feel -> 24/25 fps with motion blur; plastic-looking materials -> add SSS, AO and HDRI; everything in focus -> shallow depth of field.

### Low-poly (merged: two sources)
Flat-shaded triangle facets, one light, no textures. The island variant is a calm game-trailer world where pieces bounce in on a note; the hand variant projects meshes by hand with a wireframe-then-faces process film.

### Low-poly Isometric Island (`lowpoly-island`)
- **Look:** A miniature world of flat-shaded low-poly geometry (pointy-top hexagonal tile columns, box buildings with prism roofs, cone and icosahedron trees) floating in a faceted sea or void, watched by an orthographic camera at an isometric angle, with no perspective and no horizon: distance dissolves into a screen-space sky gradient; light, not texture, tells time, and everything that appears arrives with a bounce and a note. NOT a voxel game (no cubes of cubes, no pixel texture), not a tilt-shift photo of a real place (no perspective, no photo detail), not a smooth-shaded 3D cartoon (no rounded normals, no subsurface).
- **Medium-specific moves:** world unit = one hex tile's circumradius, terrain height encodes material (sand low, grass, hills, rock highest), a thin top cap over a darker side colour with the side column sunk below the cap, small gaps between tiles so AO draws seams; props as primitive assemblies, emissive parts with their own material per object; flat-shaded rough (~.9) materials, no textures, each tile's lightness jittered a few %; water as a dense flat-shaded sine-displaced plane (not too low roughness), a coarse plane below for zoom-outs, a per-frame shallow map with deep-to-shallow colour and faint foam rim, a thin surf ring at each tile's water line; sky haze composited from linearised orthographic depth into a vertical screen gradient keeping the horizon band in the top third at any zoom, stars only where haze is thick; post: MSAA + depth, GTAO (mandatory), sky/haze, very light edge tilt-shift, bloom (stronger at night), pastel grade, neutral tone mapping, 2x supersampling; light beams as camera-facing Gaussian ribbons plus a faint halo; on ones at 24 fps, smooth, charm in the arrival curves: rise from below (short cubic ease up, damped-sine overshoot, splash ring and spray on breaking the surface, event time = settle), drop and stack (fast ease-in fall, damped bounce, volume-preserving squash and stretch), pop with scale 1 - e^(-k s) cos(w s) at ~25 % overshoot; life (mills turn, smoke puffs, birds circle, boats bob with the same wave function as the water); growth scheduled on the music grid with at most one piece per smallest slot, accelerate by density (halves, quarters, eighths), not tempo; the projection never changes; transitions: hard cuts on the beat, a time-of-day change, a zoom through the sky, no dissolves or perspective whip-pans.
  - Camera: orthographic, frustum height is the zoom (a few units close, a dozen+ for the whole world, hundreds for a scale reveal), camera >= 3x frustum height away; turntable azimuth sweep; pure orthographic zoom; elevation change (low ~20-25 degrees widens the sky band, high ~50 degrees reads as a map); locked 3/4 wide; tight close-up on one arrival; side angle trailing a mover (never toward the lens); lateral truck along a row of tiles; time-lapse sun sweep with the camera still. Intercut wide and close on beats.
- **Palette:** pastel, low contrast, a handful of hues: one ground family (sand/grass/earth or equivalent), one neutral rock, a small set of wall and roof colours repeated; time of day is a keyframed set of colours (zenith, horizon, deep, shallow, sun colour, intensity, elevation, azimuth) interpolated: day airy and cool, dusk warms the horizon, night desaturates toward blue-violet; at night warm emissives (windows, lamps, a beam) are the only bright things; keep the sun sideways to the camera in key shots; water receives no shadows; no outlines, no black, shadows and AO are the only darks. Examples: sand, grass and coral roofs under a pale-blue day; snow tiles, pine greens and red cabins under a lilac sky; terracotta mesas, sage scrub and turquoise pools under an apricot dusk.
- **Type and captions:** geometric OFL sans: Quicksand 600 for subtitles, Josefin Sans caps with wide tracking for titles. Subtitles have no box: warm white with a soft dark-blue drop shadow, centred low, with a small flat-shaded tile icon whose top colour follows the time of day; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s), never overlapping the next line or a title. Titles behave like tiles: letters rise one by one from under a water line, overshoot and settle. A text card over the world leaves a gap where the world stays visible.
- **Sound:** event-driven music where growth events carry notes so the picture writes the tune; pitched percussion suits the facets (marimba, kalimba, woodblock, bells, vibraphone, celesta, steel drum) over a soft pad, gentle ostinato, light shaker or soft kick; pentatonic or modal material keeps any note order consonant; no piano, no strings; a single owned sound (bell, chime, horn) can return changed as a motif. Synthesizable foley by material: bubble sweep + splash with droplet ticks for rising tiles (bigger = lower), wooden knock plus quieter bounce knock for floors, ceramic clack for roofs, hollow plank knock, heavy stone thunk, glass tink, low whump with electric hum for switching on. Beds: low-passed brown-noise water with swells, birds as downward-gliding FM chirps, creaking wood, crickets or wind at night. Silence: drop to a low drone and a bed, or digital zero on the music bus before a big downbeat; or thin the ostinato to one voice. Clear gentle voice, few short lines, avoid words that collide with the subject; voice compressed above music; -14 LUFS, grain 0.
- **Use when:** growth and building stories (a first year, a neighbourhood planned, a product assembled), calm meditative brand pieces, a day, season or year on one place, networks or towns lighting up, scale reveals from one object to the whole world, music-led pieces where each event is a note.
- **Do not use when:** the brief needs perspective drama, realism, photo detail or human faces; dense text or UI must be shown; a high-contrast, dark or gritty tone is wanted; voxel or pixel aesthetics are expected.
- **Common mistakes:** perspective depth conversion in shared post code -> use the orthographic depth conversion; GTAO renders sprites and transparents as solid quads -> hide them during the AO pass and set invisible at zero opacity; side column and top cap share a plane and z-fight -> sink the column; a double-sided additive cone reads as two white lines -> camera-facing Gaussian ribbon + halo; camera too close for its frustum shows below the sea -> distance >= 3x frustum height; two pieces in one grid slot muddy the melody -> one piece per slot; `half` used as a GLSL identifier -> it is reserved, rename it.

### Low-poly, hand-projected (`lowPoly`, hand)

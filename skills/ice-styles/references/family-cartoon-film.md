# Family: cartoon, anime and film eras

Cel animation, cartoon traditions, comic markers, variety-show captions and old film formats. Read `style-method.md` first. Every entry's examples are illustration only.


## Shared rules for this family

- Characters are original. Never imitate an existing studio's characters, costumes, colour combinations or title compositions; references teach grammar only.
- Characters are built once (model sheet or frame library) and posed; a rough character is worse than none. Character route and fidelity checks are owned by the workflow and ice-qa.
- Two frame-rate layers: drawings on twos (or threes for holds), camera smooth every frame. A stepped camera reads as lag.
- Anticipation, action and follow-through on every meaningful move; smear frames only for one frame; expressions change on a drawing (or a blink hides the swap), never by crossfading faces.
- Film-era damage (grain, scratches, gate weave, flicker) moves only while the film "projects" motion and stays seeded and deterministic.
- Every object, character or scene this family draws in code follows `code-illustration-craft.md` (three tones under one light, cast and contact shadows, a texture layer, one line policy, named groups); section 8 there says how this family renders each rule.

## Rubber hose (merged: three sources)

1930s studio cartoon: uniform ink line, noodle limbs without elbows, white gloves, pie-cut eyes, everything swinging to the beat. All three sources agree: 12 fps stepping is half the style; key poses land on the beat; the body leads and limbs follow about 2 frames behind.
- **Use when:** food and drink, retro ads, playful brand mascots, children, music-led shorts.
- **Do not use when:** serious topics, modern tech precision, photoreal products.
- **Sound:** hot jazz or ragtime band (muted brass, clarinet, banjo, tuba, snare), slide whistle, cartoon impacts, film projector hum.
- **Scoped variants:** the film variant tells a full short; the cel-and-print hand variant shows a rough, ink, paint and projection process; the art-history card is for era segments (desaturate everything after the characters are drawn; smooth all bodies before stepping).

### 1930s Rubber Hose Cartoon (`rubber-hose`)
- **Look:** Hand-inked black-and-white early-sound-era animation in a worn 35 mm print look: rubber-hose limbs (equal-width tubes about 1/20 of character height, no elbows or knees), white four-finger gloves with flared cuffs and stitch lines, pie eyes (black pupil ~64 % x 74 % of the eye with an upper-right wedge cut), huge round shoes, balloon squash and stretch, flat white/grey/ink characters with one hard light-grey form-shadow band over soft grey watercolour backgrounds; everything breathes to the music. NOT a 1950s flat cartoon (no limited animation, no colour), not a silent film (synced music is the engine), not a retro filter over modern animation; object heroes use the whole object as the body (never an object head on a human body).
- **Medium-specific moves:** 1920x1080 output with a 4:3 gate (1440x1080 centred, rounded corners r ~28 px, soft inner shadow); ink outline constant in screen space (~6 px at 1080p) with light line boil (+-1.2 px, 3 drawings at 12 fps); characters on twos (12 fps), camera on ones (24 fps); tempo where one beat is an even whole number of frames (8, 10, 12, 16 or 20); every idle object breathes with cos(2 pi beats), extreme on every beat, amplitude ~5-7 %, all props in phase, up to x3 at a joy peak with the whole background squashing ~2.5 %; walk one step per beat with body bob, run as windmill legs with lean and speed lines; anticipation-hold-action (crouch to ~0.8, band stops, snap), impacts squash with exponential spring-back; chains of held glances on eighth notes read as thinking; freeze everything on a band stop; film damage pass (gate weave +-1.2 px with rare jump, flicker +-4 %, 0-3 scratches, 3-8 dust specks, occasional hair, vignette, jump on every cut); transitions: iris, hard cut on a beat, a title card rolling up like a window shade; no dissolves.
  - Camera: theatre camera, mostly locked; stage-wide locked eye-level proscenium; hard cut to a face close-up for the reaction; eased trucking pan beside a runner; medium follow shot (character ~1/4-1/3 frame height); tilt with a fall or rise; slightly high single shot with hazard and answer; tabletop pan with flat characters before a turning model set; pull-back from close-up to packed medium-full; iris in/out that a character can grab.
- **Palette:** strictly greyscale, about 6 grey steps plus a separate character white, no hue anywhere; characters own the extremes (whites and inks brighter and darker than anything in the background), backgrounds in middle greys, furniture one or two steps darker; mild contrast boost ~1.1-1.15 in the composite; form shadow one flat band, never a gradient; choose one grey temperature for the whole film (warm paper, cool silver-nitrate, or faint sepia).
- **Type and captions:** subtitles are a period title card: small near-black plate, white double-rule border, dot-and-ring corner ornaments, IM Fell English SC ~36 px paper white, drawn in the scene so it weaves and flickers, pops on/off with no fades, bottom by default, top when action is low; one card may span two short clips; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Titles in Shrikhand fat retro script, white face, heavy ink outline (~14 % of cap height), ink drop shadow down-right, letters hopping in a travelling wave; sub-lines in Limelight caps.
- **Sound:** 1930s hot dance band / ragtime: stride piano, clarinet, trumpet (open + plunger "wah-wah"), trombone smears, tuba oom-pah, banjo on 2 and 4, brushed snare with rolls, xylophone, woodblocks, slide whistle, cymbal, kazoo, washboard, pit organ; no string pads, no modern synths; one short original syncopated theme re-arranged. Sync every action to a note: band members answer characters (trombone raspberry, trumpet laugh), hard stops (reverb tails too) before gags, a stinger per gag, slide whistle for every stretch or fall, props as instruments, a solo for tenderness, a key change for a lift. Synthesized foley by material (porcelain/glass inharmonic partials, wood = woodblock, metal bells, spring boing, bubbly water, rubber sawtooth creak). Period voice (radio announcer, vaudeville MC, crooner) through a radio chain HP ~260 Hz / LP ~4.6 kHz with saturation; music ducked ~8 dB. Optical soundtrack pass: band-limit ~110 Hz-6.2 kHz, wow ~0.6 Hz, flutter ~7 Hz, then hiss, crackle and a 24 Hz projector clatter (the only sound allowed in a silence); -14 LUFS.
- **Use when:** playful brand or mascot introductions, jingles and slogans to remember, safety rhymes, "the whole place comes alive" stories (factories, gardens, cities waking), transformation gags (idea into product), nostalgic or musical comedy pieces.
- **Do not use when:** colour is required for meaning or branding; the content is serious, technical or data-heavy; the brief needs realism, modern UI or a calm explainer tone; wide establishing shots must carry detail (gags die in wides).
- **Common mistakes:** flat grey frames -> push backgrounds to mid grey, keep pure white and ink for characters, add a contrast boost; wide shots kill gags -> medium shots and packed medium-full for crowds; sad brows drawn as angry -> define brows by inner/outer end, not left/right; a limb behind the body gets hidden -> kick and reach outward past it; characters collide with background props -> check every shot's last frame; smoke puffs in a row read as thought bubbles -> scatter and grow them; short clips make subtitles flash -> merge consecutive lines into one card.
- **Variation slots:** structure: a bouncing-ball singalong carrying the message, or a talent show of objects where the smallest wins, or a relay hand-off with a new instrument per hand (illustration only); opening: a pen draws the hero out of the inkwell, or a curtain rise with the band tuning, or an iris on one blinking eye (illustration only); ending: a character drags "The End" card over itself, or the film snaps and a gloved hand splices it for a bow, or the band packs up to one note (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/rubber-hose/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Rubber hose, cel and film print (`rubberHose`, hand)
- **Look:** a blue-pencil rough on animation bond, traced in India ink at ONE pressure onto a clear cel, painted on the back in flat opaque greys, photographed and printed on black-and-white film.
- **Medium-specific moves:** uniform ink line with slight hand wobble, reseeded per drawing (line boil on twos); contours stop where a nearer shape starts; union outlines for overlapping parts; limbs as solid black noodles (one arc shoulder to wrist, no elbow), white four-digit gloves with stitch lines and flared cuffs, pie-cut pupils, big oval shoes with one shine. Order: rough, ink, paint flooded region by region, the pull-down through the projector gate, a short dance on twos landing on the key pose, the hold. Motion: on twos, key poses on the beat (15 frames), the body leads and limbs follow about 2 frames behind on the same arc, a hop of about 26 px with stretch in air and squash on landing; loops land exactly on the key pose. Film: per-frame grain, lamp flicker, dust (dark and light), a hair, a sprocket scratch, a lamp vignette, the rounded gate; gate weave plus or minus 1.7 px only while motion projects.
- **Palette:** greys only: ink `#16130f`, paper whites, five greys; print toned warm (multiply `#ecdfc6`).
- **Source:** alexgreensh/anidoodle `references/styles/rubberHose.md` (Apache-2.0).

### 1930s rubber-hose cartoon (`33_rubberhose`, implemented, quality 3 stars)
- **Look:** monochrome sepia-grey, pie-cut eyes, noodle arms of constant thickness, white four-finger gloves, round heads, everything bouncing on the beat, animated on twos, film jitter, flicker, scratches and dust in a rounded gate.
- **Medium-specific moves:** beat bounce bnc(tq, ph) = |sin(pi(tq/BEAT + ph))|, BEAT = 60/128; each object bounces up from its ground point, squashing on contact (sx 1+0.09k, sy 1-0.09k) and stretching about 5% in the air; phases staggered by 0.25 beat, accessories 0.05 behind for follow-through. Step all animation tq = floor(t x 12)/12 (on twos); film layer at floor(t x 24). Heads as r 82 circles; pie eyes = white socket plus black pupil with a wedge cut out (arc -1.45 to -0.85) plus 3 lashes; arms a single quadratic curve shoulder to hand, constant width, black edge +10px; gloves with flared cuffs and 3 back lines; tails as wavy hoses sin(7q - 9tq) x 26q; smooth all polygon bodies. After all drawing, one saturation pass to grey plus multiply `#f2e8d2` (avoid leftover warm colours). Film layer: jitter +/-1.5/+/-2px, per-frame white flicker alpha 0.02-0.09 and black 0-0.06, 3 vertical scratches repositioned at 6fps, 14 dust specks plus an occasional hair per frame, grain shifted +/-20px, rounded gate r 60 with vignette. Motif loops: sun face rays turning 1.6 rad/s and nodding to the beat; dancing flowers (stems +/-0.35 rad, leaves waving like hands); smoke rings one per beat; curtain hems flicking on the beat. Signature transition: iris (p < 0.5 old frame goes sepia(q) grayscale(0.7q) contrast(1+0.3q) while a black iris closes on the focus R = R0(1 - easeIn q); p >= 0.5 the new frame opens with outBack; 48-segment circle with +/-1.5% hand wobble at 12fps; film flicker, scratches and dust throughout).
- **Palette:** monochrome; body mid grey `#a39a84`, stripes `#5e574b`, whites `#fbf7ec`, paper `#f2e8d2`.
- **Sound:** not specified in source; score in instruments native to the period or medium (128 BPM bounce).
- **Use when:** vintage cartoon homage, playful brand mascots, swing or jazz music videos, 1930 stop.
- **Do not use when:** modern premium look; colour is the message; serious tone.
- **Common mistakes:** polygon bodies show straight edges -> smooth; desaturating per element left warm accents -> one global pass after all characters; most measured motion (40% area) came from film noise -> make characters carry the motion.
- **Known weakness:** motion is mostly film noise.
- **Source:** alchaincyf/huashu-art-motion `references/风格配方/33_rubberhose.md` (MIT, translated).

## Cel and anime

### 80s Cel Anime (`cel-anime-80s`)
- **Look:** Late-80s OVA-era cel animation re-created in code as four contrasting layers: airbrushed background paintings (painted once with a matching emissive plate, only slid), flat cels with hard-edged shadow and highlight crescents and coloured (never black) outlines, backlit light glowing through the frame with flares and red-orange halation, played back as videotape on a CRT (chroma bleed, scanlines, grille, phosphor glow, dark corners, faint noise). NOT moe anime (no round giant irises, no hatched blush), not vector clip-art (no uniform black lines, no gradients in shapes), not a film-print pastiche (no gate weave or scratches: this is tape).
- **Medium-specific moves:** characters on twos (12 fps), performance beats (blinks, eye openings, lip flaps, gestures) on threes (8 fps), camera, light sweeps, flares and rain on ones (24 fps); hair and cloth as travelling-sine ribbons in a 4-drawing cycle (6 when calm), bounce = 12 fps jitter of a few px; move light, not drawings (coloured bands sweeping across a held cel); impact frames: 2 frames of inverted or monochrome silhouette over radial lines; hold the peak as a painted "harmony" still with a slow pan, only cycles moving; fast blinks (one closed drawing), eye openings in three drawings, lip flaps of 3-4 shapes ending on a held expression; moving plates get ~16 px horizontal smear; outlines ~2-3 px at 1080p, scaled 0.45-0.8x in extreme close-ups; rim light 2-4 px; optional power-on (dot, line, picture) and tracking noise; cut on bars; transitions are cuts, white flashes, impact frames and tape tracking noise, no digital dissolves or wipes.
  - Camera: multiplane crane over a tall plate; side tracking with 3-4 parallax layers; rear view into a pseudo-3D canyon; held bust with light bands; insert with bokeh and sliding highlight; extreme close-up on eyes; slow pan across a painted still; radial speed lines + one-beat freeze; truck-in through a window or screen; locked two-shot with lip flaps; an earlier composition repainted. Keep lower ~120 px clear for subtitles.
- **Palette:** painted plates carry gradients and atmosphere, cels are flat, few-colour and saturated; each time of day gets its own colour model per character (lit, shadow and line multiplied separately, plus a backlit silhouette set with warm rim), never a filter; protagonist has one accent colour that pops in every set; line colours are dark versions of their fill (skin thin warm dark brown, hair dark purple, only hard props near-black); skin gets its own colour per set. Examples: night city (indigo, violet, neon magenta, cyan, window gold); seaside noon (cobalt, white cumulus, sand, coral); classroom dusk (amber, chalk green, brown wood, purple shadows).
- **Type and captions:** subtitles composited inside the CRT pass: semi-condensed sans (e.g. Barlow Semi Condensed 600, ~50-56 px), cream-yellow with thick dark outline and soft shadow, ~96 px above the bottom; a second speaker may take a second colour and a boxed tag; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Titles: heavy italic Latin in a chrome gradient (sky, white horizon line, violet, pink, gold), dark + neon outlines, optional katakana line (e.g. Dela Gothic One), glowing through the emissive layer, flickering on at 12 fps with a light sweep and star glint.
- **Sound:** city pop, synth funk, anime-opening pop: saxophone or synth-brass lead, FM electric piano with bell transient, slap bass, chorused 16th guitar chanks, gated-reverb snare and big toms, drum machine, arpeggiated analog synth, string pad, FM bell sparkle; extended jazz-pop harmony (maj7, 9ths). Options: band-pass the score to cassette/AM for a diegetic source then open it, brass hit on a title, open drum fill for an effect, one beat of true silence before a peak, semitone lift for a last chorus. Foley: engines from an RPM curve, rain, tyre hiss, wind, bells, radio squelch, impacts, sparks, cassette clicks, crowd murmur, door buzzers, wet footsteps, tape thunk/whine/hiss. English-dub feel voice, short lines, phone/radio voice band-passed ~380-2800 Hz; voices ~10 dB over music ducked ~9 dB, beds ducked ~3 dB; two-pass -14 LUFS.
- **Use when:** nostalgic, romantic or night-city moods, coming-of-age and slice-of-life stories, a small hero in a huge world, speed and chase moments, music-driven brand films, mock opening credits, before/after of one place at different hours.
- **Do not use when:** the brief wants modern clean digital anime or cute moe characters; dense UI, data or text must stay pin-sharp (tape and CRT softness); front-facing talking-head close-ups dominate (hardest drawing in this style); a film-print or grainy cinema look is wanted.
- **Common mistakes:** white cels bloom pink -> bloom from the emissive canvas, weak high-threshold bloom on colour; neon bleeds through characters -> stamp cel silhouettes into the emissive canvas; black holes in highlights -> clamp before S-curves; thick rim light swallows thin shapes -> 2-4 px offsets; tentacle hair, paper-mask faces, moe eyes, uniform black lines -> follow the 80s drawing rules (egg face, almond eyes, layered locks with angel ring); pink faces under neon -> skin colour per set, weaker sweeps on faces; front views are hard -> hide behind a visor, windscreen or backlight; pseudo-3D points behind the camera flip -> clamp z to the near plane; canvas falls back to a serif -> load every font weight before painting; CRT detail inflates files -> noise in the shader, CRF ~23, two-pass loudnorm.
- **Variation slots:** structure: slice of life in four seasons with four colour models, or a locked two-shot comedy breaking into one fantasy sequence, or a mock opening-credits roster with pose and name cards (illustration only); opening: mid-conversation with lip flaps already moving, or a TV switched on to an in-world broadcast, or a close-up of a drawing hand (illustration only); ending: freeze-frame on a laugh with a "to be continued" card, or the tape running out to blue screen, or a slow pan up to a starry sky (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/cel-anime-80s/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Cel Animation / Frame-by-Frame & Line Boil (`05-cel-boil`)
- **Look:** Hand-drawn frame-by-frame animation, or vector animation with hand-drawn texture on top; lines boil (jitter slightly every few frames) so the image seems to breathe. Anti-digital, hand-made warmth.
- **Medium-specific moves:** timing on twos at 24 fps (12 drawings per second) as the base, ones for fast action, threes for still shots; fake boil: turbulent displace amount 2-5, size 50, evolution re-randomized every 2 frames, posterize time 12 fps; true boil: 3-4 slightly different line drawings cycled every 2 frames; smear frame: on fast moves stretch the object 150%-300% for exactly 1 frame; hand-drawn cel FX layer (smoke, splash, lightning, speed lines) over vector subjects; paper texture multiply 10-15% over everything; variable-width pressure brush lines.
- **Technical route:** Canvas2D with a custom variable-width brush renderer (centerline + pressure -> outline polygon), boil variants cycled every 2 frames, paper texture multiply.
- **Palette:** 3-4 colors: ink black, tomato red, sunny yellow, cream paper; example seed palette (replace per brief) is that four-color set.
- **Sound:** lo-fi hip hop, recipe `lofi` 84 BPM (70-96), F major, ii9 V13 Imaj9 vi9, rhodes or piano keys with vinyl; SFX: cartoon boings, swishes on smears, paper rustle, pencil strokes, soft pops.
- **Use when:** warm or playful brand stories, editorial and culture pieces, character moments, adding life to otherwise clean vector work.
- **Do not use when:** the brand demands slick precision (fintech UI, luxury); the deliverable needs pixel-exact UI; very long runtime with no budget for drawings.
- **Common mistakes:** static perfect lines -> boil every 2 frames; everything on ones -> twos as base, ones only for fast action; smear held several frames -> exactly 1 frame; texture missing or too strong -> paper multiply 10-15%.
- **Source:** Vincentwei1021/mg-styles-15 `prompts/05-cel-boil.md` (MIT).

## Cartoon traditions

### Sci-Fi Sitcom Toon (`scifi-toon`)
- **Look:** Adult-animation sci-fi sitcom in the grammar of late-night adult cartoons (inspired by Adult Swim era shows): uniform thick near-black (slightly purple) outlines that boil, flat fills with hard-edged shadow shapes (no gradients), flat colour-band skies, big white eyes with tiny pupils, elastic parametric mouths, grotesque-but-cute creatures, sci-fi hardware treated as household junk, and one glowing travel device (portal). Comedy is dialogue-driven: a tiny mundane goal pursued with absurdly large sci-fi means. NOT a kids' cartoon (dry humour, long pauses), not anime (no sparkly eyes), not a vector explainer (lines boil). Invent your own cast, props and portal; never reproduce any existing show's characters, silhouettes, colour combos, catchphrases or portal look.
- **Medium-specific moves:** line ~7 px for characters and props, 4-6 px for background detail, round joins, constant in screen space (close-ups redrawn at the same weight); boil: resample outlines every ~7 px, displace along the normal ~1.7 px, 3 drawings at 12 fps, everything boils including holds and backgrounds; characters on twos (12 fps), camera, portal swirl, flying props and wipes on ones (24 fps); acting over moving (held poses with eye twitches, sliding pupils, gulps, sweat drops), idle breathing +-1 % squash, nods driven by speech loudness; anticipation before every gesture; landings squash with decaying cosine ~22 %, stretch flying out of a portal, "sucked in" = scale toward the portal centre while stretching; lip-sync from audio RMS (open) and spectral centroid (wide vs round) at 24 Hz sampled at 12 fps, screams x1.5-1.9; nervous jitter 3-6 px at 12 fps plus trembling pupils; in a cold pause only the boil moves; portal opens with a gentle ~12 % back-ease overshoot in ~0.3 s; every absurd image is followed by a reaction close-up; transitions are swirl wipes through the device or hard cuts, no dissolves.
  - Camera: medium two-shot; hard cut to a close-up for an interruption; insert extreme close-up of an absurd object; reaction close-up; tight close-up + short shake on one word; rush zoom into the portal into a swirl wipe; same framing repeated across worlds; very slow push-in on a still two-shot for a cold silence; wide with device and both leads; over-the-shoulder onto a screen; whip pan between two faces.
- **Palette:** one palette per world, 3-5 colours, maximally different from the previous world in hue and value so a jump reads from colour alone; line colour and eye white never change; the glow colour belongs only to the travel device; a deliberately bland beige/brown palette is the joke for the "suspiciously normal" world. Example sequences: sage lab, magenta and lime jelly, orange and teal; or grey office, ultraviolet and gold, mint and coral, beige.
- **Type and captions:** subtitles bottom centre in Baloo 2 ExtraBold ~50 px, white with ~11 px black outline, a small boiling speaker pill in the character's colour on the left; lines over ~1300 px wrap to two; overlapping lines stack with the older dimmed to ~70 %. Timing: narration >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s); rapid dialogue max(audio + 0.35 s, 0.9 s + chars / 17); clip at world changes and before a silent beat. Title: chunky display lettering popped on 12 fps steps on a hit; avoid acid green + slime drips + wobbly bubble lettering together. Screen graphics as a retro-terminal tag in VT323, coloured per world. Keep faces out of the bottom ~170 px.
- **Sound:** synthesized score: theremin-like lead (sine + a little 2nd/3rd harmonic, ~70 ms legato portamento, vibrato fading in ~150 ms after onset, spring reverb) over analog bass, square-wave arpeggio, retro drum machine, minor modes and chromatic lines; a genre cue per world (surf guitar, polka accordion, toy-piano lullaby, elevator bossa on plucked nylon, brushed swing with vibes, tuba + slide whistle, warm electric piano + vocal "aah", tremolo strings for panic); a cue's first beat is the cut; rule of three turning machine-gun. Silence is the punchline: hard-cut music and reverb tails, leaving room tone (fluorescent hum, clock tick, fridge). Foley: portal open (sub boom + down-swept noise + rising swirl), hum, "fwump" close, swirl whoosh, pop/boing, wet glorp, slurp, chomp, blink "blip", click-beep, kazoo sting, squeaky toy. Two contrasting voices (deadpan low flat slow with light saturation and ~180 Hz bump; nervous higher faster with presence boost), early reflections, music ducked ~-7 dB; -14 LUFS.
- **Use when:** comedic brand or product stories with absurd escalation, versions or alternatives compared across "worlds", duo dynamics (veteran and new hire, parent and teen, AI and user), satire of overkill solutions, callback-driven short skits for adult audiences.
- **Do not use when:** the audience is young children or the tone must be earnest, sincere or premium; the content is data, instruction or precise UI; the brief cannot avoid resembling a specific existing show (copyright red line).
- **Common mistakes:** TTS reads hyphen stutters as words -> write syllables and respell mangled words; whisper mishears short clips -> pad with ~0.6 s silence; fake interruptions -> generate the interrupted line longer and truncate where the other voice cuts in; springy overshoot swallows actors -> gentle back-ease; food and liquids read as other objects -> add drips, steam, surface detail; square-wave cymbals alias into harsh fizz -> band-passed noise plus a little FM; sustained bass below ~40 Hz rumbles on laptops -> end an octave higher; cut cue without cutting its reverb tail -> cut the tail for true silence; 1 fps contact sheets offset up to 0.5 s -> don't misread shot boundaries.
- **Variation slots:** structure: the duo stays home while a device skips them through worsening times of day, or a job-interview panel with candidates from other worlds, or a split-screen tech-support call between two worlds (illustration only); opening: mid-disaster with the portal already open, or a terminal readout explaining the device first, or starting in the absurd world (illustration only); ending: stuck elsewhere and happy, or a creature rating the film on a screen, or a quiet shot of what the errand cost someone (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/scifi-toon/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Mid-century Cartoon (`midcentury-toon`)
- **Look:** 1950s flat-colour limited animation for "follow along" films: every colour fill is a separate cel printed a few px off (down-right) its thin, breathing, broken ink line, re-rolled on twos so the picture shimmers like hand-registered paint; geometric people (egg or bean heads, nose part of the face outline, four-finger gloves); a presenter whose body is a held drawing while only arms, hands, eyes and mouth animate; rooms as one colour plane, a floor band, a gapped horizon line and one or two props; light dry-brush tooth, paper grain, warm vignette. A scene style that teaches a real task. NOT the 1930s rubber-hose cartoon (no black-and-white, no bouncing world, no hot jazz), not a whiteboard explainer (shows how to do, not why; pictures are printed, not written on).
- **Medium-specific moves:** off-register offset jitters +-1-2 px on twos, on everything including text (off only for a crisp diagram); ink ribbons 3-5 px with +-40 % width breathing and 12-25 % breaks (characters ~4 px, props 3-4.5 px); shading = one flat darker plane, drop shadows = shape copy offset down-right in paper shade ~50 %; ornaments: 4-point atomic sparkles, 8-18-point starbursts, ~30-wedge semi-transparent sunbursts, boomerangs, kidneys, polka dots; poses, hand shapes and cel jitter at 12 fps, camera, iris wipes, arrows, routes and rays at 24 fps; held body, moving hands: pose switches with a single in-between, hand action = anticipation, fast action 0.15-0.25 s eased out, follow-through, landing exactly on a beat; pop-ins scale with strong back-ease ~0.25 s; floor reach with a kneel pose and 2-bone IK, never stretched standing arms; script lettering writes on left to right, headers slide in on twos; transitions are circles, colour-block wipes and cuts on a beat, no dissolves.
  - Camera: the stand, never handheld; slow push of a few %; iris through a numbered medallion; round call-out with leader line; tilt led by a pointing finger; push into a device and pan along signal rings; iris from a round object into a top view; pull-back with counter-rotation from detail to plan; colour-block wipe as a page turn; split frame in two colour blocks for right/wrong; dead still >= 3 s for the screenshot frame; iris-out onto one object. Key subjects >= 1/3 frame height and clear of the caption band.
- **Palette:** warm paper ground, warm near-black ink, soft atomic-age complements, nothing pure or neon; one ground colour per scene (lighter tint for walls, darker shade for floors); never place the product on its own colour or the presenter on their own accent; the product keeps one colour through the whole film, the accent (garment, stars, bullets) is a different hue, step grounds rotate through the rest. Examples: coral / mustard / turquoise on cream; tomato / olive / powder blue on oatmeal; plum / lime / sand on off-white.
- **Type and captions:** (font names illustration only; choose the families per the ice-direction skill, `references/type-pairing.md`) Oleo Script Bold for a kicker; Alfa Slab One for titles, numerals and headers in ink over an offset colour plate; Jost 500/600 for body, labels and captions. Caption card: paper-coloured rounded slab with thin ink border (its colour cel offset too), small turning stars at the ends, ~40 px sans, bottom-centre, quick fade with small rise; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s, chars/12 + 1 s). Step header: ink disc with paper numeral, letter-spaced "STEP n OF N", step title on a cream plate, sliding in on twos. Detail tags: paper card with star bullet popping on the action beat beside the call-out; data lands on the picture, never as a slide.
- **Sound:** always three layers. Music: original West-Coast cool-jazz combo (flute or muted trumpet, vibraphone chords, arpeggios, glissandi and tremolo, pizzicato walking bass, brushes or sticks on ride, celesta, bongos or finger snaps), not ragtime, spy big band or piano and strings; options: one vibraphone chord per step brought back one per summary item, halve or double the feel instead of tempo, step up for a payoff. Foley by material: cardboard creaks and thumps, appliance plastic clicks, floor scrapes, glass and ceramic clinks, pours, damped rubber stamps for medallions, paper swishes for wipes, slide whistles for dimension arrows, rising pips for "connected", pen scratches for routes, an iris "shhk". Ambience: very quiet low-passed brown-noise room tone, a mantel-clock tick on the grid, device hum when it works. Cut everything including ambience the beat before the most important action. J/L cuts; narration compressed on top, music ducks ~8 dB, foley unducked; -14 LUFS. Voice: calm classroom authority, one clause per line, whisper-checked.
- **Use when:** product set-up guides (30-40 s, 2-5 steps, title >= 4 s, summary >= 3 s clean), safety or rules cards (3-4 s per rule, 3-6 rules), recipe or ritual cards (3-5 s per step), app or service onboarding (3-4 s per action), store or event notices (>= 3 s per card); instructions, how-tos, checklists, warm informational cards for services and public spaces.
- **Do not use when:** the piece tells an emotional story rather than teaching an action; the brief needs explaining "why" with diagrams on a board; a neon, high-contrast or modern tech aesthetic is required; fast frenetic pacing is wanted.
- **Common mistakes:** a route ignores obstacles -> follow the product's real rules, streamlines bend and never cross, lay one region per beat; separate nose wedge looks glued on -> one open spline from the bridge round the skull closed at the nose tip; thin arms read as sticks -> shoulders at torso corners, sleeves as thick ink ribbon with colour ribbon on top; fat-blob fingers -> stroke all parts first, fill second, slender fingers with separation lines; zooming the world triples the ink -> close-ups in call-out circles drawn at native scale; detail text without reading time -> data in the detail, context in title and voice, chars/12 + 1 s; a transition draws the next scene early -> clamp local time to just past the start.
- **Variation slots:** structure: a rules card with no product (one held figure and a medallion per rule), or a side-view tabletop recipe ending in a top view, or a do/don't split in two colour blocks (illustration only); opening: the finished result first then "here's how", or the problem in one fumbling gesture, or a numbered list stamped on then opened item by item (illustration only); ending: a checklist ticking itself, or the hands at rest beside the finished thing, or a QR code held dead still in a starburst (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/midcentury-toon/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Variety Show Kinetic Captions (Hanazi) (`18-hanazi`)
- **Look:** Emotional decorative captions from TV variety post-production: multi-stroke rounded fat type popping in elastically, changing color and shape with the speaker's mood, punctuated by hit sounds. Now the common wrapper for vlogs, pet, interview and comedy edits.
- **Medium-specific moves:** three-layer type: fill color + thick white stroke + colored outer stroke or drop shadow (multi-layer text-shadow or SVG stroke); high-saturation clashing colors with white edge and shadow; overshoot entrance scale 0 -> 1.15 -> 1.0 in about 8 frames, or per-character bounce staggered 2 frames; emotion matching: sarcasm in tilted handwriting, surprise in a radial burst frame, awkwardness with sweat-drop symbols, sparkles for delight; captions land 2-3 frames after the spoken line or gag beat; long-held captions get a slight wiggle; footage layer with slow push, handheld shake and punch-ins.
- **Technical route:** DOM/SVG multi-stroke caption components + spring pops + SVG burst, sweat and sparkle decorations over a footage layer.
- **Palette:** saturated clashing pairs (yellow, pink, cyan, red) always with white stroke and dark shadow; no hex seed given.
- **Sound:** bouncy variety bed, recipe `variety` flavor bouncy 128 BPM, F major, I vi ii V (cute ukulele flavor 112 BPM); SFX: boing, sproing, duang, slide whistle, squeak, rimshot, pop, text pop hits.
- **Use when:** vlogs, pet and lifestyle clips, interviews and reaction edits, comedic social shorts.
- **Do not use when:** the tone is premium, corporate or serious; there is no footage or speech to react to; text must stay calm and readable for long passages.
- **Common mistakes:** single-stroke plain text -> fill + thick white + colored outer layer; captions landing on or before the line -> 2-3 frames after; linear scale in -> 0 -> 1.15 -> 1.0 in about 8 frames; dead long captions -> slight wiggle; one decoration for every mood -> match burst, sweat or handwriting to the emotion.
- **Source:** Vincentwei1021/mg-styles-15 `prompts/18-hanazi.md` (MIT).

## Film eras and found footage

### 1920s Silent Film (`silent-film`)
- **Look:** An engraved-illustration one-reel silent film as seen projected in a picture house: a 4:3 print (1440x1080 in the centre of 1920x1080, rounded aperture, soft dark rim) between velvet curtains lit by screen spill and footlights; scenes painted in grey values and inked by a redraw pass (dark-side contour lines, ~5 posterised wash steps, 45 degree and crossed hatching in the darks, redrawn every two frames); silver print with flicker, weave, scratches, burn and grain; nobody speaks, beats are told by action in wide shots and by intertitles. NOT a noir (no hard-shadow mood, no voice-over), not a sepia photo filter on modern animation (the drawing itself is engraved), not a pastiche of any famous silent comedian.
- **Medium-specific moves:** print densities roughly ink 0.07, dark cloth 0.15-0.3, mid wood 0.45-0.6, skin 0.8, white linen 0.9-0.95; print pass: sepia ~0.1-0.36, print black ~0.055, white ~0.93, S-curve, highlight halation, uneven density, flicker, gate weave with occasional perforation jumps, broken vertical scratches (white = scraped emulsion, dark = dirt), brown edge burn, vignette, two-octave silver grain, dust, a hair, a rare blotch; real footage can go through the same ink pass; hand-cranked sampling: character and camera motion at 16 fps (18+ to undercrank a fast scene) while flicker, weave, grain and scratches change every 24 fps output frame; speed is emotion (undercrank chases and panic with faster flicker, back to 16 fps and a calmer print for tenderness); deadpan performance (face barely changes, body acts: crouch before effort, held poses, two-frame snap of a double take, hands on knees); objects act too; keyframed eased poses, only deliberate gags snap; 2.5D figure rig, 7.5-head adults, ~5-head children, silent-era make-up, identity by silhouette marks; transitions: iris, fade to black, straight cuts, the curtain; no dissolves within a scene, no modern whip pans.
  - Camera: heavy period camera, mostly locked; locked-off extreme wide (cause and effect seen whole, the wait is the joke); locked full shot; tracking shot with foreground passing; iris in/out; half-open iris held for a look at the audience; slow motivated push-in; masked shot (keyhole, binoculars); split screen or double exposure. Wide and medium shots; keep any close-up small or inside an iris.
- **Palette:** monochrome silver tinted only by the print: colder and harsher (more damage, less sepia) when things go wrong, warmer and cleaner when tender; tint and damage are per-section mood controls; values carry everything (dark clothes on pale walls, pale faces in dark interiors), check every shot as a thumbnail; at most one colour via a separate mask for the single thing the film is about; period whole-scene tinting (amber for day interiors, blue for night, rose for romance) is a valid alternative, one tint per section.
- **Type and captions:** the intertitles are the subtitles (srt lists their text); a card holds 2.5-3.75 s (>= max(1.8 s, reading time + 0.6 s)); black painted card with a deco border whose ornament follows the emotion (one heavy rule and shaking text for a shout, double rule with stepped corners and fans for narration, vines and small flowers for tenderness); Playfair Display SC for titles and shouts, Old Standard TT Italic for narration and dialogue; a few words per card, three to five cards in a short film; a card never explains what the picture already shows.
- **Sound:** the instrument is the sound design: an upright piano (optionally reed organ, theatre organ, violin or small ensemble) plays every event with a hit-sync vocabulary (chromatic bass creep for sneaking, grace note wink, forearm cluster crash, swelling diminished tremolo for a wait, low rumbling tremolo for storm or machine, "plink... plonk" for emptiness, diminished sforzando shock, rolled chord reveal, descending glissando fall, low "boing" bounce, nervous trill, minor second for breaking); photoplay idiom with stride left hand, mood sections like a cue sheet (Hurry, Misterioso, Agitato, Tenderly, Maestoso), a theme that returns changed; original music only. No foley, no voice; the projector is the ambience (lamp click, motor spin-up, claw-and-motor purr, film flapping on the take-up reel). Silence = music stops, projector alone remains. Tame hammer transients with light compression; -14 LUFS; grain in the picture.
- **Use when:** comedic cause-and-effect chains, near-miss gags, melodrama, newsreel-style reports, trick or magic reveals, archival or heritage stories (real footage redrawn as period drawings), nostalgic brand histories, wordless storytelling that must work without dialogue.
- **Do not use when:** the content needs spoken explanation, voice-over or sound effects; colour is essential beyond a single masked element; detailed close-up facial emotion or small UI/text detail must read; fast modern pacing with many beats is required (too much content kills it).
- **Common mistakes:** too much content (many-step chains at small figure scale) -> one legible chain with a pause in the middle; puppet arms -> abduction before flexion, shoulder rises and swings forward, slim the raised-side torso corner, hide the arm contour over the torso, raise arms sideways so they don't cross the face; pose pops -> eased keyframe lists, never if-branches; floating props -> hold between the palms at the depth between the arms, tie rolling rotation to distance; brow angles flip with facing -> check expressions in a crop at final size; iris loses its target after zoom -> track it in film coordinates; grabs miss -> the hand must be exactly where the object is on the beat; closed curtains go pure black -> add footlights; heavy grain bloats files -> re-encode tuned for grain at a higher CRF.
- **Variation slots:** structure: a newsreel of reports with headline cards, or a three-reel melodrama each reel a tint, or a one-stage trick film with substitution-cut transformations (illustration only); opening: mid-chase with the scratched film already running, or a single intertitle question before any image, or the projectionist's view of the beam then the screen (illustration only); ending: the print burns through to white lamp light, or a final card addressing the audience, or fade to black on a locked wide with the piano holding one chord (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/silent-film/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Liminal Found Footage (`liminal-found-footage`)
- **Look:** Inspired by the Backrooms found-footage genre (name for recognition only; never on screen). One first-person 1990s consumer camcorder recording (VHS, 4:3 pillarboxed in 1920x1080, 3D rendered low, e.g. 720x540, and upscaled by the VHS pass), handheld and continuous, inside an empty, familiar-but-wrong, repeating fluorescent-lit interior (office, hotel floor, school, mall, parking level, hospital wing) with no people; dread from emptiness, repetition, original bureaucratic rules and the camera's own machinery failing at the wrong moment, a chill not a jump. NOT a horror film (no monster close-ups, gore, jump scares or score stings), not an ASCII/CRT terminal piece (tape and lens artefacts, not phosphor and scanlines), not a ruin (clean is part of the style). Never use internet-folklore names, level numbers, community entity names or real organisations; everything on screen is original.
- **Medium-specific moves:** axis-aligned partitions with random gaps and stubs, pillars, drop-ceiling tiles with recessed light panels at regular intervals, a hand-authored route through procedurally filled space; lighting = thousands of shadowless panel lights summed per fragment (few % warm/cool/green variation, few % dead, few % flickering) with AO in seams; fog colour follows local light, background = fog x ~0.15 so corridors dissolve into darkness; restrained single-digit-percent decay (tide marks, discoloured ceiling band, peeling seams, yellowed or missing tiles, dead insects in diffusers); printed notices on yellowed laser paper with form numbers, numbered rules and a polite "Management" closing; VHS pass: line jitter <= 0.25 px, head-switching band, tracking tears on demand, barrel + vignette on image but not OSD, luma blur and ringing, chroma smeared right, drifting white balance, AGC noise in darks, dropouts, lifted blacks, soft clip, noise at 30 fps tape rate, no scanlines or RGB mask; one continuous take, cuts only where the tape breaks (power-on roll-in, tracking tear, REC off); handheld = walking gait (head bob a few cm, sway, small roll) + breathing ~0.27 Hz + tremor whose amplitude follows a fear curve, all frozen on a held breath; looking down shows only shoe toes and floor; autofocus hunts with overshoot (blur, lock, slip, lock), zoom narrows FOV and opens the aperture; flickers are ~0.1 s blackouts with ~0.45 s auto-exposure lag and pumping gain; things move by themselves slowly (> 1 s, eased), never snap.
  - Camera: walking POV down a corridor; slow pan off a detail to the space; glance down at the feet; crouch and hold on printed text; zoom + focus hunt into the distance; identical framing after a tear with only the OSD changed; turn a corner and hold; silent slow push-in; backing away while filming; camera set down recording alone; whip toward a sound, then nothing. Any presence small, far, in a region of dead lights.
- **Palette:** one dominant sickly hue from the location's materials (mustard wallpaper, institutional green, hospital blue, beige laminate) with darker trim and a mottled floor of the same family; near-white slightly warm ceiling and panels; exactly one saturated signal colour (red alarm lamp, green emergency sign, amber warning) which is the only thing that blooms (drive its emissive high); white balance drifts, never graded clean. Example material palettes: wallpaper `#d5bd66`, trim `#4d3d26`, carpet `#8c763c`; pool tile `#9fc7c0`, grout `#e6ece6`, water `#5e8f96`; school corridor `#b9c49a`, lockers `#6f7f66`, linoleum `#a89a7d`.
- **Type and captions:** CEA-608-style closed captions: monospace (e.g. IBM Plex Mono 600) ~42 px, white on a solid black box per row, <= 32 characters, centred in the 4:3 frame above the OSD, untouched by tape noise; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s); speaker marks [PA] for announcements, italics for the operator, SDH descriptions ([LIGHTS FLICKER], [TAPE NOISE], [SILENCE]). Rules on notices are not captioned: they are the picture, readable at 1080p, >= 3-4 s uncovered, shot with the lens parallel to the page. OSD: hand-made bitmap font with black outline (REC dot, battery, time, date); title = camcorder title-generator text over the first shot.
- **Sound:** no score, only what the tape recorded; music only inside the world (hold music, radio through walls, tinny PA jingle), filtered and reverberant, possibly slowing with tape wow; fluorescent bed of 120 Hz (or 100 Hz) + harmonics, buzzy band-passed component, faint ballast whine, bad-tube bursts, gated by the picture's light curve; PA voice calm and corporate through a ceiling speaker (band-pass ~300-3500 Hz, cone resonance, saturation, room reverb), an original chime before announcements that may drift flat after a time jump; operator few quiet lines with proximity bass and breath, breathing rate following fear, a held breath and one long exhale; foley by material (carpet thud + squelch or hard-floor squeaks, cloth, camcorder clack and motor, zoom servo, AF ticks, ballast tink on flickers, stick-slip hinge, overhead drag, vending machine, pipe). Digital zero for >= 1 s (tails included), captioned [SILENCE], is the strongest tool; at most everything restriking when sound returns. Drone options: rising low brown-noise band with beating sines, pitch-shifting room tone, or nothing. Bed and in-world music ~-8 dB under voices; -14 LUFS, no added grain.
- **Use when:** unsettling or mysterious atmospheres, rule-based or procedural storytelling, empty-space walkthroughs, night-watch logs, time-loss and alibi stories, eerie campaigns for games, events, podcasts or fiction, institutional spaces after hours.
- **Do not use when:** the brief is upbeat, commercial, family-friendly or product-focused; content must be clearly lit and colour-accurate; it needs monsters, action or jump scares (style forbids them); the piece needs music-driven pacing or multiple shots and cuts.
- **Common mistakes:** fully bright scene reads as a hospital corridor -> partitions with gaps, far areas fall dark; lights leak through walls -> a dead zone needs every light within ~+-5 m dead and the space behind dim; bright rectangle at corridor end -> background = fog x ~0.15; uniform fog lifts silhouettes to grey -> fog follows local light; line wobble skews text and pitching down italicises it -> <= 0.25 px wobble, lower the camera; primitive figures read as toys and legs as tubes -> organic silhouettes, only shoe toes; pure red never blooms at low emissive -> drive it far higher; normalising by pre-filter peak blows up a bus -> normalise the processed signal; heavy PA reverb kills intelligibility -> ASR-check processed lines and the full mix; billboards stamp dark AO rectangles -> exclude them; time-compressing a finished timeline breaks cues -> map original-time keyframes through one warp function.
- **Variation slots:** structure: two tapes of the same route a year apart, or a cheerful property walkthrough while the place keeps adding rooms, or a checklist read aloud and ticked until an item is not on the list (illustration only); opening: mid-sentence with tracking still rolling in, or a test pattern and date slate before pickup, or lens cap on with sound only (illustration only); ending: battery dies on a frozen frame, or the camera set down recording an empty room, or the tape looping to the first shot now showing the operator's shoes (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/backrooms/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

## 1. y4 Storytime (first-person reaction cartoon)

- **Look:** one character, 5-6 poses and three mouths can carry an episode; the audience wants "this person", so identity is carried mostly by hairstyle. Shape language all round and blunt (head about 1.0R x 0.93R, bean body about 1.0R x 1.2R, total height about 3.2R, head about 58 % of height, tube arms without elbows, a circle for a hand). Expressions mostly via eyebrows; mouths: smile arc, flat line, wavy (worried), half open, full open. The character is drawn from an approved frame library (never improvised code-drawn chibi); without a character route do not use this grammar.
- **Framing:** default medium shot (waist up, head 25-35 % of frame height), character off-centre, the other side for what they talk about; the background only says where (3-5 big flat blocks; white or flat is fine); the reaction shot removes the environment (face fills the frame, head radius about one third of frame height, flat colour plus vignette); punchlines often cut to a huge wide where the character is tiny.
- **Colour:** background always lighter and quieter than the character: low-saturation, high-value walls; background line `#5B6470` 3 px versus character line `#1B1B1F`; character line width = head radius x 0.05 (minimum 4 px) scaling with camera, at least 2x the background line; reaction void `#3D405B` plus radial vignette; the mouth is the only saturated colour.
- **Timing:** measured shot length ASL 2.43 s (median 1.87, IQR 1.03-3.07), about 70 % of adjacent frames fully static, still runs median 0.40 s, motion runs median 100 ms (pose to pose: basically switching between drawings; the risk is too soft and too slow). Character steps at 24 fps, camera smooth at 60 fps; pose switch 0.10 s expo-out with a 7 % vertical squash and a spring settle (about 0.35 s, volume preserving); mouth from the VO loudness envelope, shape change at most every 1/12 s (closed under 0.06, half under 0.22, else open); nod scaled to mouth level; breathing plus or minus 1.2 % over 1.6 s; blinks every 2.4-3.6 s, 2 frames closed (speechless slow blink 3 frames); gaze shift about 0.09R; sweat drop 0.9 s; glasses glint 0.3 s; "?" pops in 0.26 s back-out with about 30 % overshoot then a small wobble. No entrance animation: the character is already in place and talking on the first frame of a cut; props and symbols pop with 15-30 % overshoot.
- **Transitions:** hard cut dominant (one VO sentence per shot); smash to reaction close-up (new frame at 1.22x springing back to 1, two frames of 55 % white flash, a low thud; clamp scale at or above 1 to avoid black edges); whip toward what the character looks at (expo in-out 0.26 s with motion blur and a whoosh); jump-in about 2x on the same camera (needs one world canvas); over-shoulder view with a recognizable back of head and rim light (a pure black silhouette reads as another shadow person).
- **VO sync:** shots follow sentences, not music; transitions in the breath between sentences, subtitle and cut change on the same frame; reaction shot in the silence after an absurd line (leave 0.8-1.5 s); hold at least 0.9 s after a punchline. In-picture numbers align to the spoken syllable (a number slam = a low thump plus a short screen shake decaying in about 0.3 s).
- **Captions:** some platforms expect no burned subtitles, others require them (then: plain white text with a dark stroke, whole sentence cut in and out, no animation). Caption rules are owned by ice-type.
- **Use when:** first-person anecdotes, failures, comedy, relatable everyday stories, personal brand storytelling.
- **Do not use when:** dense tutorials or data, no first-person voice, an unfunny or formal script, no approved character frames.
- **Common mistakes:** full animation where everything moves -> micro-motion only (breathing, blinks, background props at 1-2 %); a background as interesting as the character -> lighter, flatter, thinner lines; no silence for reactions -> at least 0.8 s, camera held, one or two micro details, emphasis by jump-in or a 0.17 s slam, never a uniform slow push.

## 2. y5 Kinetic type

- **Look:** words are actors. One full-screen base colour per chapter (colour = chapter number); 4 bases plus ink plus white; always exactly one accent. Shapes: rounded rectangles (card radius about 26, bubble 30, keycap 22), circles, slanted highlighter blocks (slope about 0.18); an optional 64 px dot grid at 6-10 % opacity; no outlined or drop-shadowed text (card shadows 8-18 % only).
- **Hierarchy:** main word at most 4 characters (2 is ideal), supporting line at most 14; only one thing enters at a time; split into more pages rather than shrinking type. Size ladder main : support : label about 3.4 : 1 : 0.4 (for example 340 / 96 / 40 px at 1080p); the main word reads in 0.3 s; at least 130 px margins.
- **Entrances:** main word slams from 1.7x to 1 with an underdamped spring 1 - e^(-10t) cos(2 pi 2.2 t) (about 8 % overshoot, settles about 0.35 s), opacity 0 to 1 in the first 0.06 s; a title's first word adds a -8 degree to 0 rotation; supporting lines and labels rise out of a slot mask by 1.2 cap heights (expo-out 0.30-0.35 s); numbers, icons, bubbles and keycaps pop with back-out s = 2.6-3.0 (17-20 % overshoot) over 0.30-0.42 s; cards slide from 700 px or 260 px below with a 3.4 degree rotate-to-zero (expo-out 0.40 s); typing 26 characters per second with a 2 Hz cursor; highlighter or underline left to right expo-out 0.30 s, 1-3 eighths after the word; emphasis scale to 1.35 pinned with a spring; a giant background stage number (about 900 px at 5-13 % opacity) drifting slowly; checkmark stroke 0.22 s, strike-through 0.08 s later.
- **Exits:** faster than entrances: expo-in 0.18 s, 0.03 s stagger, shrink to 0.6, drop 60 px, fade.
- **Continuous motion:** words never move after landing (no drifting or breathing while read); life comes from a camera micro-drift (about 5 px, periods about 7 s and 2.7 s, plus or minus 0.5 degree), a beat micro-punch (1.2 % per quarter note decaying in about 0.12 s), a slowly panning dot grid, page changes and evidence objects entering; final beat pull-out 6 % over 0.5 s.
- **Rhythm:** one shot per bar at the film's own tempo (<tempo from ice/plan/cue-sheet.json>; the measured channels sat near 120 BPM, evidence only) with the same beat slots in every shot so viewers can predict: beat 1 main word slam (plus giant number), 1.5 evidence object, 2 supporting line, 3 evidence action, 3.5 keyword emphasis plus highlight, 4 rest or exit. Main words on downbeats with a low impact; small elements on eighths with pops; a 0.3 s riser before transitions; every visual landing has a sound and every accent has a visual. Every landing is on a beat number or a spoken word; landings on neither are moved or deleted. Measured lyric kinetic type: only 10-21 % static frames, a big visual event every 0.66-0.83 s (about one per beat at 99-105 BPM); explainer openers may go one per eighth, long films one per beat.
- **Transitions:** fill-zoom (the camera crashes into a shape whose colour is the next base: 55 % expo-in to about 30x, 45 % expo-out settle from 1.25); colour bands (three slanted bands relay left to right with 12 % stagger, the last one becomes the new base, 0.5 s expo in-out); push (0.35 s expo in-out); mask wipe; a letter becomes a colour block. Place each transition so its reveal point lands on the bar line (segment start = bar line minus reveal fraction x transition duration; perceived arrival of a push is at about 90 % displacement, p about 0.58).
- **Reading time:** English lower bound about 13 characters per second; Chinese subtitle heuristic 7-9 characters per second (unverified); a 2-character main word at least 0.5 s, a 7-8 character supporting line at least 1.0 s. Global reading-time gates are owned by ice-type.
- **Use when:** lists, stages, quotes, number openers, chapter cards, hype openers, music-synced statements.
- **Do not use when:** stories and emotion, long dense voiceover, more than 5 list items, or a section that would stay all text for more than 30-60 s (switch grammar or insert an evidence page every two pages).
- **Common mistakes:** all words the same size moving together; words drifting while read; animation off the beat or accenting the transition start instead of the reveal frame; the same page type ("big word + support + top-left label") more than 3 times in a row reads as slides -> insert a number slam, an evidence page or a fill-zoom; code cards in proportional fonts -> monospace.

## 3. t2 Keynote UI

- **Look:** one thing per screen; the product screenshot or object is the hero and gets pushed into. Optional wrappers: glow mesh, frosted glass cards, gradient text, centred blur-in, floating cards. Together those five read instantly as an AI template: use at most two per film, and with real screenshots, recordings or photos go full-bleed and push in instead.
- **Real material:** screenshot width at least 80 % of the frame (portrait at least 90 %); push only into the part being explained (slow about 0.9 s; fast 0.28 s only for reveals or comparisons); at most one or two pushes per screenshot; a small card floating on a large glow is a dead background. A number about a screenshot pulls back to the full screenshot, dims it and slams the number on it, never a separate glowing number page.
- **Specs when the dark keynote wrapper is used:** near-black base (about `#050508`, or a neutral `#000` to `#1d1d1f` family); optional glow mesh of 4 radial gradients screen-blended, blurred about 90 px at quarter size, about 3.5 % static grain against banding, a darker centre for text contrast; glow drift amplitude 90-260 px with periods 9.5-13 s (avoid oscillation near 0.2 Hz); glass card = blurred backdrop sample (about 40 px) + about 7.5 % white fill + a light top band + a 1.5 px gradient stroke + a big soft shadow; card radius about 36 with concentric inner radius = parent radius - padding; titles weight 600 with tight tracking (-0.015 to -0.03 em at 64 px and above); text off-white, secondary at about 60 % opacity; big numbers at least 55 % of frame width; a three-card row only when there is no screenshot. Glass behind nothing is a grey rectangle; blend glow colours so they do not average into grey-purple mud.
- **Colour:** colours follow the product brand; never a default purple-blue-cyan glow; no dark keynote base for bad news, serious events or non-launch topics.
- **Motion:** spring with mass 1, stiffness (2 pi / d)^2, damping 4 pi (1 - bounce) / d; bounce 0 by default, 0.15 crisp (cards d 0.75 s, bubbles d 0.5 s), 0.3 visible bounce, above 0.4 reads cheap. Useful UI curves: navigation about 0.32 s cubic-bezier(.4,0,.6,1); ease-out-quad (.25,.46,.45,.94) about 0.16 s; product-site transitions under 500 ms (.2,1,.2,1). Optional blur-in text: blur 18-26 to 0, rise 16-40 px, 0.7-1.0 s; eyebrow, product name, subtitle staggered 0.2-0.4 s. Cards enter from about 170 px below with a Y rotation, scale 0.9, spring, 0.1 s stagger. Count-ups start 0.25 s after a card settles, 0.9 s expo-out, tabular digits; never recount from 0 when the same number reappears. Light sweep: a -20 degree band 110-140 px wide over 0.8-0.9 s. Hold after settle: no floating cards, no uniform slow push.
- **Transitions:** blur push 0.55 s (old frame to 0.92 with blur and fade, new from 1.06 blurred to sharp, a heavily blurred enlarged old frame underneath to avoid black edges); shared-element expand (the chosen card scales to 1.06, others recede, it expands to full screen with its radius going to 0; stop all floating before the expand); match cut; mask or light-sweep cut; refraction instead of a fade.
- **VO sync:** text starts 0.2-0.3 s before its noun is spoken (a 0.7 s blur-in is clear on the word); numbers settle when spoken (count start = spoken time - count duration); three cards map to "first, second, third". Title at most about 6 characters or a product name; subtitle at most 16; a card = a 4-character title + one line + a big number + a smaller grey unit; give a quiet empty screen after dense parts.
- **Layout variety:** list each screen's layout (title, three cards, big number, screenshot); the same layout on 2 screens in a row means switch; looping "title, three feature cards, big number" is a slideshow.
- **Use when:** product and feature launches, release notes, tutorial steps, specs.
- **Do not use when:** mechanisms (t1), trends (t3), bad news, serious public topics, or no hero object or screenshot to enlarge.

## 4. t3 Finance chart

- **Point first:** write the chart's one point before anything; the title is that conclusion; annotation marks only it. No sentence, no chart.
- **Honesty:** bars always start at 0; a non-zero axis is stated in the subtitle; values change only how they appear, never how much; multiples must be derivable from the shown values and the same number matches across shots ("rose to 8.7x" means "rose by 7.7x"); fictional data carries a sample-data badge in every shot and real data never does; a source line on every chart. The Lie Factor must stay within 0.95-1.05. Data and claims integrity is also gated by ice-assets (truth sheet) and ice-qa.
- **Look (editorial chart layout):** a thin rule along the top (about 8 px) in <accent from ice/DIRECTION.md>, a small flag top-left in the same accent, a bold conclusion title, a subtitle with metric and unit, source bottom-left; palette roles: one colour for the hero series, all other series in a light neutral, a near-black ink, a few categorical colours only when each series needs its own, and a ground (light paper, tinted paper or dark) as the direction decides; never copy a named publication's palette; horizontal grid only (about 13 % alpha, 1.5 px); only the zero baseline dark and heavy (3 px); y labels on the right above the gridlines; condensed numerals; main line 5 px, moving average 4 px, leaders 2 px.
- **Up and down colours:** mainland China convention is red up, green down; Hong Kong and US are green up, red down; state the convention on the chart for that audience; colour-blind alternative: blue and orange plus triangles.
- **Staging:** layout 0-0.5 s, gridlines left to right (lag 0.12, quint-out), x labels fade-rise, then data, then the accent colour change, then the annotation; 0.5-1.3 s per stage (staged, eased, about 1 s per step). Bars grow from 0 with quint-out, lag 0.22, total about 1.3 s, labels counting at the bar tops. Lines are drawn by x (time), not by arc length, so steep parts draw faster (about 1.5-2 s in video), with a 9 px head dot and a value label; area fill gradient from 0.14 to 0 opacity. Data grows when its word is spoken, one cue per series point that matters, never a title at 0 s and everything at once.
- **Annotation:** triggers when the line head passes the event point: the dot pops (spring, bounce about 0.25) with a pulse ring, the leader follows 0.12 s later, text 0.3 s later; one annotation per shot. Candles lag 0.06 s each, body from open to close then wicks; count-ups expo-out 0.9-1.3 s with tabular digits; bar-chart races at about 250 ms keyframes with about 0.5 s reorders. Hold at least 0.5 s after numbers settle.
- **Transitions:** slide push 0.5 s cubic-bezier(.7,0,.2,1) with an accent bar leading and the new panel building its layout while it enters; zoom through (set the start scale so the new frame does not float as a small card); morph the same data between chart types (move, then morph; never morph between different metrics, which implies the same units); re-scale axes before moving data; hard cut keeping the layout (the most common in this genre).
- **In the film's world:** charts keep the film's colour family (dark theme or margin fill), or better, render with transparency onto a screen, a blackboard or paper inside the scene; a white chart dropped into a night film disconnects. The same layout at most 3 charts in a row; insert a push into detail, a slammed number or a return to real material.
- **Narrative arc:** establisher, initiating event, peak, release.
- **Use when:** revenue, prices, market share, inflection points, any quantitative comparison over time.
- **Do not use when:** a single number (use t2 or y5), unreliable or unsourced data.
- **Common mistakes:** truncated axes; too many colours; flashy animation (bouncing bars, 3D bars, rotating pies); annotations that pre-assert a number before it lands.

## 5. y6 Presenter explainer

- **Look:** a cartoon presenter links paper documents, charts and keywords, with a top bar (for example a running year) and section titles. Requires the presenter's own approved frame library; generate at least full-body and bust framings, and real matching frames for sitting or leaning.
- **Layouts:** assign them in the storyboard: side-on presenter, full-screen number, left/right comparison, character entering the chart, reaction close-up. Never a fixed "person left, chart right" for the whole film; moving the presenter in post cannot fix a monotonous storyboard.
- **Motion:** pose changes are hard cuts with a slight pop (cross-dissolving different silhouettes makes double images); mouth on or off follows the VO and stops in pauses; walking uses full gait frames; heights normalized by the median frame; reaction close-ups use bust frames, never an upscaled full frame. Use a scene camera, not a bitmap push (bitmap pushes crop).
- **VO sync:** numbers settle when spoken; during counting never pre-assert "over" or "+"; an emphasis circle appears after the number settles; pauses after highlights are planned in the script and the VO.
- **Transitions:** a page turn that keeps the presenter and the top bar in place.
- **Checks:** the first presenter keyframe of each segment sets position and scale explicitly (avoids cross-segment inheritance); top-bar year, subtitles and section titles read the same time source; check text framing and that the presenter never covers text or gets cropped by pushes.
- **Use when:** business and company histories, product mechanisms with a human guide, emotional reactions to data.
- **Do not use when:** no presenter frame library can be made; very short pieces; formal institutional tone.

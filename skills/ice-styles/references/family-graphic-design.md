# Family: graphic design motion

Flat vector, grid systems, poster movements, title design, morph and liquid shape languages, isometric. Read `style-method.md` first. Every entry's examples are illustration only.


## Shared rules for this family

- Design styles are judged on precision: grid, type, timing. Write signature features as numbers (curves, frame counts, module sizes) and check them.
- Transitions are carried by elements (a shape wipe, a morph, a field that becomes the next background), not crossfades.
- Easing family is style-specific and conflicts are scoped, not averaged: flat vector uses anticipation and elastic overshoot; Bauhaus and Swiss grid styles ban overshoot and land on grid lines; morph uses a slow in-out with short cushions; liquid is flung, never uniform. Keep each style's own family for the whole film.
- One subject acts at a time in flat and grid styles; everything moving at once reads as a template.
- Every object, character or scene this family draws in code follows `code-illustration-craft.md` (three tones under one light, cast and contact shadows, a texture layer, one line policy, named groups); section 8 there says how this family renders each rule.
- **Craft profile (family default):** needs: rhythm (precision as numbers: module distances, frame counts and the style's own easing family; one subject acts at a time, rules above), transition (carried by an element: a shape wipe, a morph, a field that becomes the next ground; never a crossfade, rules above), light (form by flat tone steps under one light, no gradients needed, `code-illustration-craft.md` section 8 row "Flat vector, isometric, pictogram"; an entry whose Look is flat fills with no gradients lifts `art:tones` and `art:shading`), finish (light grain or print noise, or none declared with its reason on grid entries, `code-illustration-craft.md` section 8), sfx (clicks, pops, ticks or whooshes locked to landings, `style-method.md` section 12 rows for this family); serves when: type (words on screen: set on the style's grid in its type system, moving in its easing family), data (a number, a process or a comparison: built from the style's own shapes, pictograms, isometric blocks or bars on the grid), depth (several items or a space: overlapping planes and atmospheric colour, or the isometric 2.5D of those entries; an entry whose Look or moves ask for large or clean negative space lifts `film:frame_fill` and `film:empty_half`), camera (a layout larger than the frame: a move that lands on the grid, never a float), composite (a flat object on a ground: long or cast shadows as separate shapes), music (music in the type or the brief: `style-method.md` section 12 rows for this family), silence (a reveal or the key line: everything lands and the sound stops).

## Flat vector (merged: three sources)

Flat fills, edges only where fills meet. Scoped variants: the explainer variant (elastic overshoot, 2-4 frame staggers, shape-wipe transitions) for SaaS and how-it-works pieces; the pen-tool illustration hand (paths closing into fills, long shadows, grain on shade sides) for editorial illustration films; the contemporary flat-with-grain card (stipple grain by density, no outlines, acting characters) for character gags and homage endings.

### Flat Vector 2D Motion Graphics (`01-flat-vector`)
- **Look:** Solid-color geometric characters and scenes, no outline or minimal outline, big clashing color blocks, everything built from vector shapes. The mainstream explainer baseline.
- **Medium-specific moves:** flat fills with no or almost no gradients and clean negative space; elastic overshoot easing cubic-bezier(0.34,1.56,0.64,1) (or 70-85% influence bezier handles); three-phase entrance: anticipation, burst out, rebound; staggered entrances of 2-4 frames; only one subject moves at any moment; around 10% squash and stretch on translations; 30 fps; limbs rigged by anchor-point rotation under parent nulls; transitions by shape wipe or an element flying out and dragging the whole scene with it.
- **Technical route:** SVG + GSAP (custom ease, bounce, shape morph), motion blur with 4 samples on fast moves.
- **Palette:** high-saturation brand palette of 4-5 solid colors plus one light ground and one dark ink; example seed palette (replace per brief): #2B2BFF, #FF5A4E, #FFC62B, #2EE6A8, #FFF6E9, #151433.
- **Sound:** EDM-pop with supersaw drop, recipe `pop` 122 BPM (110-130), C major, I V vi IV; SFX: pops, swishes, whooshes, soft impacts, UI blips on each landing.
- **Use when:** SaaS or app explainers, science or how-it-works pieces, annual-report style corporate videos, friendly brand stories.
- **Do not use when:** the brief needs premium material realism; the subject is solemn or luxury; footage must carry the story.
- **Common mistakes:** everything enters at once -> stagger 2-4 frames and keep one mover; linear or plain ease-out -> use the 0.34,1.56 overshoot with anticipation; gradients creeping in -> keep flat fills; static hard cut between scenes -> shape wipe or fly-out transition.
- **Source:** Vincentwei1021/mg-styles-15 `prompts/01-flat-vector.md` (MIT).

### Flat vector, pen-tool hand (`flatVector`, hand)
- **Look:** the pen tool in a vector app: closed paths of hand-placed anchors, one flat fill landing the instant the path closes, long shadows as separate shapes with a transparency ramp, a raster grain brush over shade sides clipped to each shape.
- **Medium-specific moves:** no strokes anywhere: an edge exists only where two fills meet; depth from overlapping planes and atmospheric colour; order: background planes, midground, subjects, long shadows and shade facets, grain shape by shape; process film shows the path being drawn (blue path, square anchors, handles) and the UI vanishes when the path closes.
- **Palette:** about a dozen swatches in one key; low sun from one side, every shadow thrown away and ramping to nothing.
- **Sound:** soft clicks, pops on fills; light pop or acoustic bed.
- **Use when:** modern illustration for apps, travel, editorial, SaaS when a hand-made feel is not wanted.
- **Do not use when:** the brand needs texture or craft; documentary realism.
- **Common mistakes:** torn edges or lift shadows (that is cut paper) -> perfect edges, cast shadows only.
- **Source:** alexgreensh/anidoodle `references/styles/flatVector.md` (Apache-2.0).

### Contemporary flat vector illustration with grain (`16_2026`, implemented, quality 3 stars)
- **Look:** Dribbble-style flat colour blocks, no outlines, form shown by one-step-darker same-hue blocks, stippled 2px grain whose density varies by position, big-head proportions, snappy character acting with damped secondary motion.
- **Medium-specific moves:** stipple: scatter 2px square dots inside a shape with density as a function of position (for example 0.12+0.75 clamp((y-cy+120)/420), denser toward the bottom-right); static parts cached; a static grain texture (5% dark dots alpha 70, 3.5% light dots alpha 90) applied only onto figure pixels. Light cone of 26,000 dots, width 56 to 150 by y, alpha falling with y, cached and swung with the lamp. Heads scaled about 1.2x around the neck. Animal pose library (local keypoints: body 10 points, chest, thigh, stripes, head, legs, tail Bezier, foot offset) blended per value; squash and stretch around the feet so they stay planted, switch pivot to the centre of mass in the air. Acting beats: anticipation squash sy 1 to 0.86, sx to 1.07; take-off sy 1.12 rotation -0.75; landing sy 0.70 sx 1.18; flip facing on the flattest frame (snap, cleaner than a 2-3 frame card flip); overshoot 1.07 then settle. Secondary motion as damped oscillators (lamp 0.055 rad period 26 frames decay 22; plant shake period 9 decay 26); falling object with measured gravity about 1.75 px/frame^2, shatter with 7 impact lines over 8 frames, parabolic shards, spreading stain 140 to 235px eased out. Signature (handwriting) revealed by a clip that follows a measured writing front, underline 24 segments tapering 3.4 to 1.4px. Ambient: clouds 15px/s, small plane 177px/s with dashed trail, sun rays 0.35 rad/s, 17 decorative symbols boiling at 4fps (+/-1.5px, +/-0.09 rad). Signature transition: three-colour concentric ring iris.
- **Palette:** flat brand-style pastels; example highlight block `#f08b7d`, thigh `#f3ad4c`.
- **Sound:** not specified in source; score in instruments native to the period or medium.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: finish (stippled 2 px grain whose density varies by position, laid only on figure pixels, entry moves), light (form by one-step-darker same-hue blocks and a dotted light cone, entry Look and moves), rhythm (snappy acting: anticipation squash, take-off, landing, overshoot then settle, damped secondary motion, entry Look and moves), transition (a three-colour concentric ring iris, entry signature transition); serves when also: foley (a physical action on screen: soft material sounds by medium; research: validate), silence (a gag or a reveal: a beat of silence before it lands; research: validate).
- **Use when:** product explainers, app and SaaS ads, character-led social comedy, onboarding, present-day endings of speedruns.
- **Do not use when:** heritage or fine-art gravitas; photoreal needs; brands that forbid grain textures.
- **Common mistakes:** computing a paw target before applying the body lean put it 60px into the table after 0.38 rad lean -> apply the lean, then invert the current transform to map the target into local space.
- **Known weakness:** none listed. Fast: 5-12ms warm.
- **Source:** alchaincyf/huashu-art-motion `references/风格配方/16_2026.md` (MIT, translated).

## Grid and modernist systems

### Swiss Motion Graphics (`swiss-motion`)
- **Look:** International Typographic Style in motion: pure 2D design on a modular grid, flat paper, black type and bars, light greys and one saturated signal colour; flush-left ragged-right type, extreme scale contrast (giant numeral beside tiny technical notes); every element snaps to the grid and lands on a beat; the one element that breaks the system becomes the character. Not a keynote (no gradients, glow, device renders), not lyric kinetic type (words don't fly, spin or bounce), not a chart unless the grid gives bars that meaning, not a primary-shapes pastiche.
- **Medium-specific moves:** two curves only: precise ease cubic-bezier(.7,0,.2,1) for snaps and linear for lines drawn, playheads and the exception; no overshoot, spring or bounce; durations are note values, a snap starts one note early and lands on the beat; entrances are wipes or clip-reveals (words rise out of a clip rect from below the baseline and leave upward, lines grow along their length, giant numerals climb in eased module steps), never fades; deletions are knife cuts (a thin line crosses, the element collapses to its midline); ripples ordered by distance from their cause; the exception moves linear and unsnapped, maybe with an "on grid / off grid" readout; smooth 24 fps, no stepping. Frame grid 1920x1080, 12 columns, ~96 px margins, 24 px gutters, 24 px baseline; frame may act as a spread (programme left, artifact right); bars overdrawn 1 px at seams; no shadows, particles or grain.
- Camera list (precise ease, on beats only): locked spread; hard pan by one page or column; rotate 90 deg and stop; pull out to a wall of artifacts; push into one module; grid extends past the page; split frame into equal cells; vertical scroll through a column. Transitions: wipes, knife cuts, camera moves, re-flows; never dissolves.
- **Palette:** Paper, ink, two or three light greys for grid lines, and one signal colour carried by only one thing (red, orange, ultramarine or green, chosen per topic, never a second); black type may overprint the signal colour; light-grey elements under black ones give depth without a second hue. Sets: page `#EAE9E5`, paper `#FFFFFF`, ink `#111111`, signal `#E30613`; `#F2F0EA` / `#141414` / `#FF5A00`; `#E9ECEF` / `#0B0B0B` / `#1F3FFF`.
- **Type and captions:** One open-licence neo-grotesque family for the whole film, chosen per the ice-direction skill, `references/type-pairing.md` (a default-look face needs a D-entry reason), several weights, ~-2 % tracking at >= 90 px, headlines may be lowercase, giant numerals optically flush left; load all weights before drawing. Subtitles are part of the layout at a column start (small grey lead-in plus large bold statement, or a museum-label caption), each word clip-revealed on its spoken timestamp; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Caption rows ~19 px medium with a 1 px rule above; technical notes in a corner.
- **Sound:** Music with its own grid: steady pulse (motorik kit and bass, minimal techno, marimba or vibraphone patterns, mechanical piano ostinato, drum machine with glockenspiel), one instrument added per new rule; picture and score can share one data file. Options: drop the drums for the exception, near-silent bar before a reveal. Dry close print foley, no reverb: letterpress clack, ruling-pen "tss", guillotine snick, paper slap and slide, numeral thunk, block snap, felt thump, rubber stamp, typewriter carriage; the exception may be the only thing that sings (a sine glide). Hard stop on a downbeat. Voice compressed >= 6 dB above music, music ducked ~10 dB, -14 LUFS; cool crisp rule-like delivery.
- **Use when:** brand identity systems and programmes, feature lists, timetables and schedules, specs and manifestos, records and statistics as chapter cards, reorganizations and re-flows, outlier stories, cuts and edits, design and architecture content.
- **Do not use when:** warmth, handmade texture or nostalgia is the point; a playful bouncy consumer tone is required; photographic or 3D product hero shots are needed; the story needs many colours to code categories.
- **Common mistakes:** too many thin columns look like a chart -> fewer, wider modules; a "better" composition that isn't clearly better -> one strong diagonal with long/short rhythm, larger signal element, bleeds, type over colour (tilting alone is not enough); rotating and labelling at once reads as noise -> rotate, hold, then label; merged bars show seams after rotation -> overdraw 1 px; elements sharing the camera matrix leak into other shots -> draw only when on screen; letter-spacing skews measurement -> measure after setting the font; onsets between frames flicker -> put visual onsets on whole frames; speech-timing tools split or pad words -> align by letters, clamp to 0.
- **Variation slots:** structure: a 24-row timetable filling one row per beat, or an A to Z specimen sheet, or two systems on a split spread until one element crosses the gutter (illustration only); opening: a single dot placed and the grid growing from it, or a giant numeral revealed as a detail of a small layout (illustration only); ending: the empty grid after every element is cut, or the exception leaves and the system closes over its space (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/swiss-motion/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Bauhaus geometry (merged: two sources)
Both sources agree: pure primary geometry, moves on a strict module, no black in rotating colour sets. The motion-design variant is a beat-locked kinetic poster; the art-history card is for era segments.

### Geometric / Bauhaus Motion (`09-bauhaus`)
- **Look:** Circles, triangles and rectangles translating, rotating and scaling precisely on a strict grid, primary colors plus black and white. Mechanical, rational order; the language of kinetic posters.
- **Medium-specific moves:** limited red, yellow, blue, black palette; every move anchored to the grid: distance is an integer multiple of the module and every landing sits on a grid line; restrained easing: easeInOutQuad or plain linear, overshoot forbidden (mechanical feel is the point); beat-driven: each element appears or flips instantly on a beat, one action per beat, beat grid at 120 BPM; rotation pivots alternate between own center, an edge vertex and frame center to create rhythm; generative variants driven by trigonometric phase offsets across arrays.
- **Technical route:** SVG/Canvas + GSAP on a strict modular grid, beat grid at 120 BPM.
- **Palette:** off-white paper ground with red, yellow, blue and black; example seed palette (replace per brief): #F1E9DA, #E03C31, #F2B705, #1E4FA3.
- **Sound:** minimal or click techno, recipe `techno` 124 BPM (118-132), A minor, i9 i9 iv9 i9, dub stabs; SFX: hard clicks, ticks, switch toggles, short impacts on every beat landing.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: rhythm (one action per beat on a fixed beat grid, distances in whole modules, every landing on a grid line, no overshoot, rotation pivots alternating, entry moves), light (pure flat primary fills with no modelling, so the family's flat-fill lift holds, entry Look), music (minimal or click techno that sets the beat grid, entry Sound).
- **Use when:** kinetic posters, event and culture identities, design or architecture brands, beat-synced loops.
- **Do not use when:** warmth or character emotion is needed; the brand palette conflicts with primaries; the story needs organic or realistic imagery.
- **Common mistakes:** elastic overshoot -> easeInOutQuad or linear only; off-grid landings -> integer module distances; motion off the beat -> one action per beat; one pivot for every rotation -> alternate center, vertex, frame center.
- **Source:** Vincentwei1021/mg-styles-15 `prompts/09-bauhaus.md` (MIT).

### Bauhaus poster geometry (narrow use) (`12_bauhaus`, implemented, quality 3 stars)
- **Look:** pure geometry on aged cream paper, primary red-yellow-blue plus black, vertical rotated wordmarks, Mondrian-like grid panes, Schlemmer-style figurines built from circles, trapezoids, rods and joint discs; no curved outlines.
- **Medium-specific moves:** paper with upscaled small fbm at alpha 0.5 and dark speckle grain (density 0.02 alpha 0.35, old print spots); red circle cached with lighter fbm spots at alpha 0.55 painted only onto it (uneven screen-print ink); chrome tubes as three-tone gradients, cantilever chair stroked 15px dark / 10px chrome / 3px highlight offset 2px. Figures: circle head, trapezoid torso, fan-shaped skirt, thick round-ended black rods for limbs, cream joint discs with 5px black ring and dot. Motif loops: a long black bar turns like a clock hand 28 to 49.5 deg with easeOut over 0.22s about a fixed hub; grid panes swap colours at 13fps only when (step+2 sw) mod 9 < 6 (irregular rhythm), colours limited to red, yellow, blue, cream, pale blue, grey; 6 concentric arcs pulse like sound waves (radius +/-3, length sweeps 55% to 100% in 0.4s); a rolling ball whose rotation = distance / radius with two speed lines; steam as a 2.2px sine wave. Signature transition: blue square, red circle, yellow triangle and a round hole rotate and scale up from the red circle (0.20s).
- **Palette:** paper `#ebe3cf`; black `#161514`; red `#d3262b`; blue `#2a58b4`; yellow `#efc12f`; pale blue `#b9cee0 / #a6c0da`; grey `#c6c3bb`; cream `#f2ecdf`; orange `#ee8a2a / #c25a14`; chrome `#c9c9c6`.
- **Sound:** not specified in source; score in instruments native to the period or medium.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: finish (aged cream paper with speckle grain and uneven screen-print ink on the red circle, entry Look and moves), type (vertical rotated wordmarks set in the poster's geometry, entry Look), transition (the primary shapes rotate and scale up out of the red circle, entry signature transition); serves when also: foley (a shape landing or a figure moving: paper and print sounds by medium; research: validate), silence (the beat before the signature transition: the sound stops; research: validate).
- **Use when:** design and architecture brands, data or process explainers built from shapes, education on modernism, 1923 stop.
- **Do not use when:** warmth and organic softness are the message; detailed character expression; photographic products.
- **Common mistakes:** grid cells sized at 107px overflowed the frame by 16px -> derive cell size from inner width minus gaps; black in the swap set made the grid suddenly heavy -> remove black; a long mouth bar extending outside the head read as a cigarette -> short bar inside the head.
- **Known weakness:** a figure head drifted off its skeleton anchor (about 50px offset).
- **Source:** alchaincyf/huashu-art-motion `references/风格配方/12_bauhaus.md` (MIT, translated).

### Russian constructivist poster (narrow use) (`22_constructivism`, implemented, quality 3 stars)
- **Look:** aged cream paper, red wedges driving into black circles, diagonal bars, geometric block Cyrillic lettering, halftoned grayscale photo collage cut out with a white border, flat paper-cut clothing with 3px black lines.
- **Medium-specific moves:** halftone: sample grayscale on a 45 deg rotated grid, dot diameter = cell x gain x sqrt(1-L), only where alpha > 128, all dots merged into one path filled once (about 3,000 dots under 2ms); photos cell 7 gain 0.8, skin cell 4.6 gain 0.74, with a 7px cream outline as the cut-out edge; features re-drawn in crisp black, lips hand-coloured red. Shading on flat clothes by dots inside the path. Collage: crisp hand-drawn line structures on top of halftoned photos (photo plus drawing). Geometric letters on a 5x7 module, each part filled separately. Motif loops: red wedge thrusts along a 0.34 rad axis 70(0.5-0.5 cos(2 pi 1.6 lt)); red dashed ring [46,22] dash offset -260 lt; radio arcs r = 18+((1.4lt+k/4) mod 1) x 230 fading; slogan letters stamp in one by one (letter k starts at 0.045k s, 1.5x to 1x in 0.12s) then the line waves -10 max(0, sin(11lt-0.8k)); every 4th letter black. Signature transition: used as an opening in the source; none tested.
- **Palette:** paper `#e9dfc4` (dark `#d9c9a4`); red `#c8231c / #8f1711`; black `#151311`; cream `#f4ecd6`; gold `#e3b03e / #a87a1c`; orange `#e2741c`.
- **Sound:** not specified in source; score in instruments native to the period or medium.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: cutout (halftoned grayscale photos cut out with a cream border and collaged under crisp drawn lines, entry Look and moves), type (geometric block letters on a 5 x 7 module stamping in one by one, entry moves), finish (aged paper, with halftone dots as the only shading on flat clothes, entry moves); serves when also: footage (a real person, place or event in the brief: its photographs halftoned into the collage, entry moves), foley (a slogan or a shape landing: stamp, paper and press sounds by medium; research: validate), silence (before a slogan lands: the sound stops; research: validate).
- **Use when:** bold announcements, kinetic typography, protest or rally aesthetics, design history education, 1924 stop.
- **Do not use when:** the political iconography would mislead or offend; soft lifestyle brands; text in scripts without hand-built glyphs.
- **Common mistakes:** a breve placed above the letter cell got covered and misread -> keep the whole glyph inside its 7-unit cell; overlapping strokes in one path cancelled under nonzero fill (A read as B) -> fill each part separately; slogan baseline too low cut off letters -> position by letter centre; cheekbone shadow too strong became a halftone beard -> halve shadow alpha in the grayscale source.
- **Known weakness:** lacks the megaphone-shaped text cone.
- **Source:** alchaincyf/huashu-art-motion `references/风格配方/22_constructivism.md` (MIT, translated).

### Pictogram Motion (`pictogram-motion`)
- **Look:** A beat-locked flash-card catalogue film built from three things only: a square-grid core pattern of discs, half-discs and small patterned motif cells in five tones of one hue with whole rows shifted sideways; faceless geometric pictogram figures of round-capped bars and a disc head standing on a large sun disc; bold bilingual type with mono broadcast meta text. Flat colour, light grain, one soft light sweep as the only gradient. Not an isometric infographic (no depth), not pure Swiss type motion, not a cartoon (figures never emote), not a data chart.
- **Medium-specific moves:** one tempo for the film; every shot starts on a beat and every key pose (release, contact, landing) lands on an integer beat; pace ladder: normal cards last one bar, related sub-items run at half length; second tempo only by dividing the beat; exponential ease-out entrances, exponential in-out whips and wipes, a back ease once at most; type enters by mask slide-up (title first, second-language line ~0.08 s later, then tag; meta types on without a caret); figures assemble as horizontal bands sliding into register from alternating sides; background rows drift in alternating directions and jump a quarter-cell on beats on chapter cards; grid transitions lasting a few frames around the cut (sliding rows, dropping columns, push, quarter-discs from cell corners, iris from the last figure, tile flip), rotated through; motion blur only inside transition and whip windows. Grid cell ~1/6 frame height; far limbs mixed ~40 % toward the background, a second person ~60 %.
- Camera list (mostly locked flat): locked flat frame; lateral track with a one-screen whip per beat; slow drift along a row; vertical scroll by grid rows; push into one motif cell until it becomes the next frame; pull back to a tiled wall of items; tiles collapsing to one point; split screen along a grid line. Text and figure never overlap (one text column, one stage), HUD in corners; never dissolves, 3D moves, handheld shake or depth of field.
- **Palette:** A set of five hues plus one dark neutral, each hue in five tones (middle is base); a chapter owns one hue (multi-hue chapters rotate per card); pattern at low contrast under type, full contrast on chapter cards; light hues take dark type and dark figures with the disc in the lighter tone, dark hues take cream; on the dark neutral the disc uses a lighter tone and the accent is one warm hue; one cream and one ink serve every hue; no outlines or highlights except the sweep. Example sets: warm festival (red, violet, forest green, gold, ochre); coastal (navy, teal, coral, sand, sky); kitchen (tomato, basil, saffron, aubergine, flour cream). Invent your own hue names.
- **Type and captions:** 900-weight grotesk title 150-330 px at 1080p, tight tracking, auto-fitted; heavy second-language line under it; mono meta ~22 px wide-tracked (families chosen per the ice-direction skill, `references/type-pairing.md`; a default-look face needs a D-entry reason), tags as mono chips; HUD with corner text and an N-tick progress bar (current tall, done dimmed, rest faint) with N from the item list. Bilingual switch chooses the second language per version. With narration, captions are a mono line on a solid band in the chapter hue at the bottom, never over the title; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s); cards that must be read hold >= 1.6 s; deliver an .srt of title cards from the edit list.
- **Sound:** Type and music can carry the film; voice optional, calm, one line per chapter at most. Percussion is the spine (choose a family: frame drums, taiko, marching snares, steel pans, body percussion, electronic kit) plus rim clicks, claps, shakers, crashes; colour from plucked strings with pitch bends, marimba, glockenspiel, detuned-saw chord stabs, synth bass, pads, risers, drone. Every shot onset gets a per-chapter material accent (splash, pok, clank, crack, whoosh, sizzle); score placed from the edit list, onsets checked to +-15 ms. Silence: half a beat of drums and bass muted before a big hit, or one bar stripped to a single tick under a count. Glue compression, limiter, -14 LUFS, <= -1 dBTP.
- **Use when:** catalogues and "all N" lists (tools, dishes, stations, species, departments), sports and event identity pieces, how-to steps and techniques, ranked countdowns, a day in hours, year summaries and rosters, bilingual communications.
- **Do not use when:** the story needs emotion, faces or character acting; long explanations or continuous narrative; photographic product shots; content that can't be split into countable items; when it would imitate a real games or event identity too closely.
- **Common mistakes:** figure environments spread into the text column -> render the figure layer in its own buffer and fade it before the text; cream on light hues fails contrast -> dark type and figures with the lighter disc tone; the sun disc vanishes on the dark neutral -> lighter tone for it; large per-card pattern canvases exhaust memory -> bounded LRU cache; horizontal poses cross the title -> move the stage outward and narrow the text column; imitating a real identity -> your own palette, mark and slogan built from grid motifs.
- **Variation slots:** structure: a countdown from N to 1 scrolling down rows and speeding up, or one figure through a day with the palette moving dawn to night, or each card adding one part to a growing pictogram machine (illustration only); opening: a single motif cell filling the frame then splitting into the grid, or grid hairlines drawn on the beat before any colour (illustration only); ending: the last card held while pattern rows stop one by one, or the grid disassembles into rows leaving cream (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/pictogram-motion/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

## Title design and poster eras

### 60s Spy Title Sequence (`spy-titles`)
- **Look:** A cut-paper, four-ink opening title sequence: flat opaque inks on paper with no gradients, hand-cut edges on every shape, pieces layered with ~2.4 px cut gaps and small paper shadows; figures abstracted to profile silhouettes; typography is the set (credits as architecture, props, vehicles); brass stabs are the edit points; one goal, a few locations, one reveal. Not flat vector motion graphics, not a halftone print, not a Swiss grid (diagonals, jaunty type), not a gadget-and-gun-barrel parody.
- **Medium-specific moves:** puppets on twos (12 fps: figures, hands, landing letters, vehicles), camera, credit slides and grid growth on ones (24 fps); run cycles of ~8 drawings, one step per beat, strong lean, accent ribbon flying back as a multi-segment travelling-sine streamer; credits slide along grid lines and stop dead on the beat (short ease-out); letters land with a paper slap; stop-time: at a stab the whole picture freezes (background included) for a couple of beats in total silence, then resumes; shattering: a scene sliced into strips parallel to a cut line sliding off alternately along the diagonal; scissor edges displaced ~1.2-2.2 px every ~9-10 px with rare 1-2 px notches, cut once in local space and cached so edges don't crawl; figures ~7 heads tall, merged outline with thin joint slits, wedge hands, no faces except an eye slit in close-ups; credit letters re-cut with +-0.8 deg rotation and +-1.5 px baseline jitter; light film grain (~6).
- Camera list: wide on a full credit line then truck in; lateral tracking in one screen direction; hard cut on a brass stab; graphic match cut keeping circle centres and sizes identical; locked single shape on flat ground; silhouette against a giant circle; diagonal split into a grid; vertical pan down a column of type; push into a letter until it becomes a place. Strong ~30 deg diagonals, big flat grounds, figures small against type; no dissolves.
- **Palette:** Four inks: ink black, paper cream, one hot colour (reserved for one accent on the hero and for danger), one warm secondary (marks the object of the chase); darker shadow-paper variants only for depth; one dominant ground per scene changing with location; background props in a darker ground tone, never black. Sets: `#1b1714` / `#efe4c9` / `#d23a22` / `#e2a52a`; `#161a22` / `#ece6d6` / `#1f8a8a` / `#f07a28`; black / `#f3d6cc` / `#2a46b8` / `#f0d23a`.
- **Type and captions:** Credits live in the set, one line per location, fully readable at some moment, fictional roles only, condensed OFL display (League Gothic) re-cut per letter; the title uses custom geometric cut glyphs, never a real title's lettering. Subtitles on a narrow scissor-cut paper strip bottom-left, slightly tilted, a small glyph as bullet, geometric OFL sans (League Spartan 600, ~44 px), light strip on dark scenes and dark on light, slides in from the left in ~0.18 s; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s).
- **Sound:** 1960s spy big band with original motifs: stacked staccato trumpets and trombones in octaves with crash (the stab), surf guitar low twang through spring reverb, walking upright bass, brushes or sticks, bongos and shakers, vibraphone, flute, organ stabs, harpsichord; minor keys, chromatic lines, dotted rhythms; avoid the most famous spy theme's crawl and its signature ending chord. Place stabs ~8 ms early so the peak hits the frame; shrink cut intervals as chases accelerate; stop-time silence is true zero. Foley: paper cuts, slides, card slaps, blind flips, tears, keys, chains, locks, tape click and hiss; environments only hinted. Briefing voice through a tape chain (band-pass ~220-5200 Hz, saturation, wow, hiss); music ducks ~7 dB; limiter before -14 LUFS.
- **Use when:** opening titles and intros, event and conference programmes, festival line-ups and nominee lists, product launches where the object pursued is the reveal, heist, courier or deadline narratives, team or puzzle assembly stories.
- **Do not use when:** content needs realism, faces or emotional close acting; calm or clinical information delivery; more than four colours must carry meaning; long text or detailed data.
- **Common mistakes:** every internal piece outlined looks like a mannequin diagram -> merge pieces, cut only joint slits, outline the whole figure; a canvas clear that resets transforms kills the camera -> preserve the transform; a figure hidden behind a letter reads as empty -> let hat, nose and accent stick out; a stiff streamer reads as a tongue -> multi-segment ribbon with travelling sine; a credit seen only partially is uncomfortable -> always open wide on the full line; black background props read as letters -> darker ground colour; object in its ground's ink vanishes -> black backing; circular props cover neighbouring letters -> lay the word around a gap; match cuts fail when circle centres move -> lock them; flat inks plus grain bloat files -> higher CRF.
- **Variation slots:** structure: a countdown of credits each with a number built from type, or an object returned location by location, or two agents on a diagonal split until they meet (illustration only); opening: a full-frame title that shatters into the sequence, or the hero already falling through a column of type (illustration only); ending: the title never assembles because one piece walks off, or a pull-out reveals every location was one giant credit page (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/spy-titles/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Art Deco (`art-deco`)
- **Look:** A 1930s poster that moves: engraved gold keylines on warm lacquer black, filled with airbrushed hard-edged volumes (directional shade-to-light inside the shape, thin gold rim on the lit side), organised around one vertical axis; ornament is the picture (sunbursts as backgrounds, stepped arches as frames, dials as progress bars, bulb marquees as reveals); real one- and two-point perspective. Not a gold-foil slideshow, not Art Nouveau (no whiplash curves or flowers), not a flat vector poster, not a 60s spy title.
- **Medium-specific moves:** characters on twos (12 fps), camera, light, bulbs, gold draw-on and sheen on ones (24 fps); everything unfolds from the centre (point, hairline, bars opening symmetrically, text rising, wings and sparkle); gold lines draw on at the speed of the musical phrase and land on the hit; gold is a banded metal gradient (dark, light, hot, light, dark) with an animated sheen sweep; the signature stroke is a dark engraved underline ~1.6 px wider, the gold line and a hairline highlight, never a black cartoon outline; bulbs ignite with ~1.6x flash overshoot settling in ~0.18 s plus a relay chunk, then a chase may run; mechanical easing (firm ease-in-out with a small settle, nothing floats); performance beats with anticipation and escalation in threes; the centre axis never drifts. Characters ~7 heads tall, tapered streamline bodies, faceted lacquer planes, almond or arc eyes, one-stroke nose, one or two silhouette marks readable at a quarter frame height.
- Camera list: symmetric door split or revolving-door wipe; leaves sliding shut over the live shot; extreme low-angle steep tilt; architectural section pull-out to a cutaway; mirrored rotating overhead kaleidoscope of people; push-in on a dial, medallion or clock; match cut on shape; low oblique along a sign or marquee; pull back from detail to skyline. Asymmetric frames resolve to the axis within the shot; at an emotional peak the protagonist fills >= ~1/3 of frame height; transitions are splits, revolves, arch openings and shape matches, never a generic dissolve or slide.
- **Palette:** Three constants: warm black ground (never neutral grey-black, may drift to deep emerald near a horizon), gold line and metal, ivory for paper, gloves, light and type; one saturated accent reserved for the protagonist or followed object (the only large saturated area); a cooler jewel tone may carry secondary surfaces without competing; one-colour exception: a single element in its own colour through the same fill, keyline and glow. Triads (accent / jewel / metal): burgundy / emerald / gold; coral / sapphire / pale gold; jade / plum / rose gold. Always use six-digit hex in code.
- **Type and captions:** OFL faces: Limelight (titles), Poiret One (numbers, sign skeletons, plaques), Josefin Sans (subtitles, labels), Italiana (numerals). Titles are architecture: a stepped gold title bar over an arch or sunburst, drawn on in gold then swept by sheen. Subtitle card: lacquer bar (~88 % black) with stepped ends and double gold keyline, Josefin Sans SemiBold ~40-42 px ivory, optional small speaker icon, unfolding from the centre; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Numbers carry plot via dials, clocks, medallions, badges.
- **Sound:** 1920s-30s symphonic jazz, not 60s big band: stride or concert piano, clarinet trills and glissandi, strings (tremolo, rushes, pizzicato), muted trumpet commentary, trombone and sax section, walking pizz bass, brushes, tubular bells, harp glissandi, banjo, xylophone, celesta; foxtrot, Charleston, tango, torch ballad or newsreel fanfare. Options: one hit per unit of counted light, accelerate by dividing the beat, plant a theme in fragments, low-pass a distant band and open it on approach, move a period narrator through horn, radio, PA and live mic. Foley: brass clicks, bronze clang and chain, iron treads, marble heels, silver trays, wooden doors with spring twang, knife-switch chunk and filament hum, relay ticks, elevator ding, champagne cork. Silence drops to one small diegetic sound before the key moment; J/L cuts through doors. Music ducked ~-9 dB, beds -6 dB, -14 LUFS, light grain.
- **Use when:** glamorous launches and countdowns, galas, awards and trophy nights, hotels, hospitality and luxury, departments or floors of an organisation, milestones and funding goals shown on dials, teams forming a logo, routes engraved on maps.
- **Do not use when:** the brand is casual, eco, organic or minimal-modern; content is humble or everyday where glamour misleads; dense data or long text; a cool or neutral palette is mandated.
- **Common mistakes:** bulb signs built from glyph skeletons break on curves -> detect junctions with the crossing number and weld chain ends within ~14 px; a generic cartoon character looks pasted in -> make characters deco; fish-scale reads as brickwork -> overlapping lower half-circles row by row with an inner arc; door leaves over a flat interior read as a blank frame -> close leaves over the live shot; a gold point on pure black trips black-frame detection -> lift background wedges and glow slightly; period loudspeaker distortion is unintelligible -> gentle drive, check every line on the final mix.
- **Variation slots:** structure: a world's fair of pavilions behind doors, or an ocean liner climbing decks with a horn per stage, or nominees as sunburst panels with the winner revealed in light (illustration only); opening: a gold machine room building to the first beat, or ticker tape typing the premise (illustration only); ending: letters switch off in reverse until one bulb remains, or the last shot flattens into a dated printed poster (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/art-deco/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

## Shape languages

### Morphing / Shape Morph (`08-morph`)
- **Look:** One shape turns smoothly and continuously into another (a device into a map pin, a cup into a sunset). The core grammar of visual storytelling and the hardest craft in premium explainers.
- **Medium-specific moves:** contours transition continuously with no jumps; paths aligned first: equal vertex count and matching first-vertex position, otherwise they knot; complex A -> B goes through an intermediate simple shape (circle or capsule): A -> circle -> B, each half 8-12 frames; 10-15% squash and stretch plus slight rotation throughout the morph to hide mechanical interpolation; morph travels with translation and rotation; speed curve fastest mid-way cubic-bezier(0.7,0,0.3,1) with 3 frames of cushion at each end; optimal vertex matching via a dedicated morph library instead of naive SMIL.
- **Technical route:** SVG + a shape-interpolation library or morph plugin (tune shapeIndex), color-field wipes.
- **Palette:** solid flat fields with one color change per morph stage, color-field wipes between scenes; no hex seed given.
- **Sound:** future bass, recipe `future_bass` 150 BPM, D major, IVmaj7 V iii7 vi9; SFX: morph and stretch sounds, whoosh on each transform, pop or ding on the settle.
- **Use when:** product-to-benefit stories, logo builds, connecting ideas without cuts, keynote-style feature reveals.
- **Do not use when:** shapes have no meaningful relation; the piece is data or text heavy; very complex illustrated assets that cannot be simplified to paths.
- **Common mistakes:** knotted or twisting morph -> equalize vertex counts and set the first vertex; direct complex A to B -> go through a circle or capsule, 8-12 frames each half; robotic interpolation -> add 10-15% squash and stretch and slight rotation; abrupt starts -> 3-frame cushions on 0.7,0,0.3,1.
- **Source:** Vincentwei1021/mg-styles-15 `prompts/08-morph.md` (MIT).

### Liquid Motion (`07-liquid`)
- **Look:** Shapes flow, stretch into strings, drip and merge like liquid; transitions are paint sweeping over the screen and a logo forms from a gathering drop. Organic, satisfying, high energy.
- **Medium-specific moves:** wavy-edge mask transition whose front has 3-5 out-of-phase bulges; metaball merge faked by Gaussian blur 20px+ then a tight alpha threshold (levels or feColorMatrix) so nearby blobs fuse; stretch, snap and rebound: a thin neck forms as a shape leaves and both ends rebound 2-3 frames after the break; easing fast-then-slow cubic-bezier(0.22,1,0.36,1), liquid is flung never moved linearly; secondary droplets follow the main body 2-3 frames late and squash flat on landing; no straight lines anywhere.
- **Technical route:** WebGL2 fragment shader with 2D SDF metaballs (smooth-min), fake 3D lighting from field-gradient normals, text SDF for the logo.
- **Palette:** 2-3 saturated fluid colors with glossy highlights on a contrasting ground; no hex seed given.
- **Sound:** future bass, recipe `future_bass` 150 BPM (130-165), D major, IVmaj7 V iii7 vi9, optional pre-drop gap; SFX: gloop, drip, bubbles, splash, squelch, stretch.
- **Use when:** music videos, food and drink ads, energetic transitions and packaging, logo formation.
- **Do not use when:** the content is data-heavy or needs crisp UI; the brand is rigid or institutional; the target device cannot run shaders and no pre-render is planned.
- **Common mistakes:** blobs overlap without fusing -> blur 20px+ then threshold; linear sweeps -> 0.22,1,0.36,1 fling; droplets moving with the body -> 2-3 frame follow-through and landing squash; clean break -> neck then 2-3 frame rebound.
- **Source:** Vincentwei1021/mg-styles-15 `prompts/07-liquid.md` (MIT).

## Isometric (merged: two sources)

True isometric or 2:1 dimetric projection, three tones per material, no perspective. The motion-design variant builds a living miniature world with growth animation; the hand variant is a construction time-lapse cutaway with no camera moves. For data and system diagrams in isometric, see the iso infographic entry in `family-evidence-data.md`.

### Isometric / 2.5D (`03-isometric`)
- **Look:** A miniature world in 30 degree isometric projection (small city, office slice, server room), no vanishing point, everything at equal scale. Dense information that stays cute.
- **Medium-specific moves:** true 30 degree isometric angle with no perspective; faces built by SSR: scale vertical 86.6% -> shear/skew +-30 degrees -> rotate -+30 degrees, three faces assembled; fake depth by layer order where Z order equals screen y; movement along the grid keeps a 2:1 pixel slope; building growth: base lands first, walls scaleY from 0, roof caps 2-3 frames later; camera only pans as a group (no rotation) with foreground, mid, background parallax speeds 1 : 0.8 : 0.6; soft shadows and ambient occlusion.
- **Technical route:** WebGL orthographic camera at the true isometric angle with soft shadows and AO post-processing, or SVG with SSR matrix faces.
- **Palette:** soft pastel set (lavender ground, mint, peach, sky blue, white) with soft shadows and AO; no hex seed given, pick 4-5 pastels plus white.
- **Sound:** marimba tech explainer bed, recipe `explainer` 112 BPM (100-124), D major, Imaj7 V vi7 IVmaj7; SFX: pops for each building, soft clicks, UI confirm, whoosh on camera pans.
- **Use when:** system or architecture diagrams, app feature tours, logistics or city-scale stories, data-flow explainers.
- **Do not use when:** the subject needs faces and emotion up close; the shot needs a real perspective camera move; the scene has only one object.
- **Common mistakes:** perspective creeping in -> orthographic only; objects drifting off the 2:1 slope -> snap motion to the iso grid; camera rotation -> group pan only; buildings popping in whole -> base, wall rise, roof 2-3 frames late.
- **Source:** Vincentwei1021/mg-styles-15 `prompts/03-isometric.md` (MIT).

### Isometric cutaway (`isometric`, hand)
- **Look:** flat vector planes on a true 2:1 isometric (dimetric) grid built the way an iso illustrator works: world x maps to screen (u, u/2), y to (-u, u/2), z to (0, -1.2247u); every horizontal edge a 2:1 line, verticals vertical.
- **Medium-specific moves:** each material has exactly three tones (top lightest, one side middle, other side darkest), curved things obey the same cube rule; no contour line, tone meets tone; no gradients anywhere; one light source steps a warm tint in three flat distance bands and casts single flat shadows. Order: iso grid ruled, translucent massing blocks, walls and floors, interiors, props, light and shadow, details. Motion: construction time-lapse in painter's order, a plane traced then filled top-down, light poured last; the camera never moves in the process film. Figures authored in screen space over the projected foot point, about 7.5 heads.
- **Palette:** cool ambient on warm materials with one saturated warm source, or as the brief needs.
- **Sound:** soft plucks and clicks on each landing.
- **Use when:** how a place or system works, cutaways, processes, operations, real estate interiors.
- **Do not use when:** emotional character stories; organic subjects.
- **Common mistakes:** outlines or gradients -> three flat tones per material; non-2:1 slopes -> use the true grid.
- **Source:** alexgreensh/anidoodle `references/styles/isometric.md` (Apache-2.0).

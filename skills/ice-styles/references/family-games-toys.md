# Family: games and toys

Pixel art, game-trailer looks, game-show graphics, toy bricks and miniatures. Read `style-method.md` first. Every entry's examples are illustration only.


## Shared rules for this family

- Never name or imitate real games, consoles, mascots, toy brands or their trademarks. Describe grammar only, and invent all characters, items and logos.
- Grid styles are exact: integer scaling with smoothing off, a declared palette, whole-pixel motion. A pixel that is not in the palette is a defect.
- Game and toy motion is quantized: sprites and parts step (8-12 fps for sprites, on twos for stop-motion); the camera may move smoothly but snaps to whole art pixels in pixel styles.
- Every object, character or scene this family draws in code follows `code-illustration-craft.md` (three tones under one light, cast and contact shadows, a texture layer, one line policy, named groups); section 8 there says how this family renders each rule.

## Pixel art (merged: four sources)

All four sources agree on these invariants: a low internal resolution (between about 120x120 and 480x270 depending on the variant) integer-scaled with nearest-neighbour; a declared palette (8 to 36 colours); no anti-aliasing, no alpha-blended gradients (half tints are ordered dither), no rotation or non-integer scaling of sprites; sprites step between authored poses (about 8-12 fps; 3-frame holds give a 10 fps feel), never tweened positions; parallax by whole pixels; any CRT pass must not blur the pixels; on-screen text in thick pixel letters at least two grains wide.
- **Use when:** game trailers and recaps, nostalgia marketing, retro music videos, developer or gaming communities, playful onboarding.
- **Do not use when:** luxury, healthcare or legal sobriety; detailed realistic products; long dense text.
- **Sound:** chiptune (square, triangle, noise channels; at most 4 voices; no reverb), about 150 BPM for energetic pieces; jump, coin, chest, menu and hit blips.
- **Common mistakes across sources:** large pose changes in one step at 12 fps (sit, curl, turn) -> add an in-between; tails and ears teleporting -> step them over two drawings; a screen-door dissolve -> a ring dissolve from the centre or a block transition that starts revealing at once (never three or more near-black frames); smoke and sparks as grid patterns -> irregular dither blobs; dialogue boxes closed as soon as typing ends -> hold at least 1.2 s after the last character.
- **Scoped variants:** 16-bit RPG film (story with typing text boxes), 8-bit motion piece (10 s run-and-jump), cell-by-cell process hand (a still that lays itself), 8-bit home-console art-history card (speedrun segment).

### 16-bit Pixel RPG (`pixel-rpg`)
- **Look:** A short film that looks, sounds and behaves like a mid-90s console role-playing game: 320x180 native pixels scaled x6 nearest-neighbour to 1080p (text included, all in the framebuffer), a limited indexed palette (~32-colour master plus a few protected duplicate indices), ordered-dither gradients, sprites ~20-34 px tall animating in a handful of frames, 32x32 3/4-view portraits for facial acting, and story told through game systems (dialog boxes, menus, status bars, battle messages, save files, item fanfares) taken seriously as a language for feeling. NOT the pixel sprite diorama (no depth of field, bloom or real-time light), not CRT nostalgia (no scanlines, no grain), not a parody.
- **Medium-specific moves:** indexed framebuffer through a lookup table so a whole palette changes in one frame; camera moves in integer pixels, each parallax layer rounding its own offset; light is index stepping along ramps, glows are Bayer-thresholded rings, firelight in low-colour eras uses a warm remap (greens, browns, rust); 4x4 Bayer for gradients, light shafts as a flat 50 % checker with 25 % edge; colour-depth tables (16-bit identity, ~15-colour 8-bit set with a dark green and dark brown, 4-shade luminance thresholds drawn natively at 160x90 and doubled, faded ~7 blue-greys); sprites from a capsule rig with 3-tone upper-left shading and a 1 px ink outline, hero gets a silhouette mark; output 24 fps, sprites at 8 fps (walk) to 12 fps (flames, blinks); game-native acting (hop forward to act, cast pose, knockback, kneel, KO blink at 12 fps, breath = shoulders up 1 px); windows slide in ~0.3 s ease-out in integer px, hand cursor bobs 1 px at 4 fps and stops when a character hesitates; transitions: thumbnail expand + mosaic, mosaic cross with palette switch at the peak, encounter swirl (polar twist + mosaic + Bayer dissolve to white), screen shake (3-2-1 px over 8 frames), 4-level palette step fades; never alpha fades.
  - Camera: side-scroll with parallax layers; locked frame with UI on one third; battle side view (enemy / party / windows); top-down overworld pan; integer-pixel tilt up something tall; hold while the palette changes; back view at a threshold; zoom by mosaic into a thumbnail. Characters stay above the dialog box; menus leave the action visible.
- **Palette:** palette = feeling: colour depth, fades and swaps are story tools, changed by table, never by alpha; fewer colours and lower resolution can stand for older times; one or two protected indices exempt from every effect become the film's thread and enable match cuts between eras; keep warm hues (orange, pink, plum) in reduced subsets or the first step turns a scene solid red; map by luminance, never by rank.
- **Type and captions:** the dialog box is the subtitle, drawn in the framebuffer in the bottom quarter: layered rounded border (ink / white / silver), dithered dark-blue fill, 32x32 portrait on the left, an original variable-width pixel font (cap ~7 px) with 1 px shadow, typing with the voice at ~38-40 chars/s (slower in older eras), a blinking down-arrow when done. Skins follow the era (light box for 4-colour, black with white frame for 8-bit, blue for 16-bit). Battle and system messages in a thin top window; every box >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s), the next box cuts the previous; srt from the same array. Titles in the pixel font at 2x, 8-neighbour ink outline, row-by-row gradient, Bayer-dissolved in and out.
- **Sound:** chiptune voices: pulse waves at 12.5/25/50 % duty, 4-bit triangle bass, LFSR noise drums, wavetable leads, console-style echo (a few taps ~180-370 ms, feedback ~0.4, low-pass in the loop), original melodies only; fidelity can follow colour depth (4-colour mono with 4-bit volume steps and low sample rate, 8-bit mono, 16-bit stereo with echo, faded present muffled); options: regional variations of one motif, one layer lost per palette step, key change or full band on a colour flood, a phrase left on the dominant and resolved later, an item-get fanfare as punctuation. Synthesized bit-crushed foley per era: cursor and select blips, per-speaker text blips, echoing footsteps, fire crackle, triangle-arpeggio magic shimmer, charge and blast, HP tick-down, KO blink, bit-drop per palette step, save ticks, door grind, coin and item jingles, menu error buzz. One organic sound (a breath, a heartbeat) can make a character human once. True zero silence including echo tails. Music ducks ~7-8 dB under voice; -14 LUFS, grain 0; optional first-person voice or text blips only.
- **Use when:** stories framed as a journey, quest, levelling up or turns; personal or company histories across eras; loss, change or memory told through palette; progress, inventories, skills, team members joining or leaving; nostalgic gaming-flavoured brand stories.
- **Do not use when:** the brief needs high resolution detail, photoreal products or readable dense UI; it calls for depth of field, bloom or modern lighting (use a sprite-in-diorama style instead); retro CRT or VHS artefacts are the point; the tone must avoid any game framing.
- **Common mistakes:** rank-based palette mapping crushes to black -> map by luminance with a gamma; subsets without warm hues turn a sunset solid red -> keep warm hues; 8-bit set without dark green or brown turns night grass blue, generic brightening near fire turns grass neon -> warm remap; post-quantising full colour to 4 shades is mud -> draw low eras natively at half resolution; smooth-dome pixel hair reads as a helmet, single-pixel alternation as noise -> 2-3 big spikes; pale clothing reads as skin; held props cross the face in side view -> use the back hand; dark plank door reads as a jail -> panels, bevels, studs; negative modulo indexes undefined -> ((n % m) + m) % m; dialog box covers the floor -> keep the floor line above it; protected-colour match cuts drift -> compute both screen positions.
- **Variation slots:** structure: an overworld tour with each town a chapter, or a story told only through items bought, used and sold, or a turn-based duel where each move is a scene (illustration only); opening: PRESS START title with an attract-mode demo, or a battle already in progress with low HP, or a boot screen that fails and is retried (illustration only); ending: GAME OVER with an open retry/give-up choice, or credits over the overworld as characters walk home, or the last save slot written and the menu closing (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/pixel-rpg/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Pixel Art / 8-bit (`20-pixel`)
- **Look:** Big low-resolution pixels with hard edges, a limited palette and low-frame-rate sprite animation. Retro game aesthetic.
- **Medium-specific moves:** internal 320x180 canvas integer-scaled x6 to 1920x1080 with nearest-neighbour only (image-rendering: pixelated), no anti-aliasing; limited palette of 8-32 colors (16-32 typical); sprite frames at 8-12 fps: walk cycle 6-8 frames, breathing 2-4 frames; steps() timing instead of smooth easing; dithered gradients (for example sky); 3-4 parallax layers (sky, far, mid, near) moving by whole pixels at different speeds; optional CRT pass: scanlines + barrel distortion + phosphor glow.
- **Technical route:** Canvas at 320x180 internal resolution, integer scale x6 nearest-neighbour, sprites authored as pixel arrays in code, limited palette.
- **Palette:** fantasy-console-like fixed 16-32 color palette with dithered transitions; palette shifts (day to sunset) done by swapping within the set; no hex seed given.
- **Sound:** chiptune, recipe `chiptune` 150 BPM (120-170), C major, I V vi IV; SFX: coin, jump, power-up, 1-up, 8-bit explosion, CRT on.
- **Use when:** gaming and indie-game content, nostalgia campaigns, playful brand Easter eggs, 8-bit music visuals.
- **Do not use when:** fine detail or small text must be legible; the brand is luxury or medical; the brief requires smooth premium motion.
- **Common mistakes:** blurry scaling -> nearest-neighbour integer scale only; sub-pixel drift -> move layers by whole pixels; smooth tweens -> steps() timing; too many colors -> lock 8-32 and dither.
- **Source:** Vincentwei1021/mg-styles-15 `prompts/20-pixel.md` (MIT).

### Pixel art, cell by cell (`pixelArt`, hand)
- **Look:** cells laid one at a time on a 120 x 120 grid, each exactly one of 16 palette colours declared up front, blown up by a whole number with smoothing off; machine crispness is the medium (no anti-aliasing, taper, wobble or paper).
- **Medium-specific moves:** marks are the single cell, the bucket fill and the dither cluster; a half tint is a 2x2 ordered dither, never an alpha blend; glows are authored stamps; clean staircases (consistent run lengths, no doubled corners, no orphan cells); faces and ears placed by hand as sprites. Order: flat mid colours (coarse 2x2 first), silhouette cleanup, shading clusters and sky dither bands, highlights and rim light, light sources and glow dither last. Motion: cels held 3 frames (about 10 fps feel); character loops pose to pose with whole-cell teleports between authored poses, never tweened positions.
- **Gate:** every rendered pixel is a palette colour (0 off-palette pixels).
- **Source:** alexgreensh/anidoodle `references/styles/pixelArt.md` (Apache-2.0).

### 8-bit home-console pixel art (`14_8bit`, implemented, quality 3 stars)
- **Look:** Inspired by the NES era. 240x135 logical pixels scaled 8x with no smoothing, 14-colour sprite palette, 1px black outline around every part, checkerboard dithering for gradients, chunky pixel HUD.
- **Medium-specific moves:** draw everything on a 240x135 canvas (1 logical pixel = 8 screen pixels), upscale once with smoothing off. Backgrounds per pixel: 6px cross-dot wallpaper offset 3px on alternate rows, wood planks with a black seam every 24px, bricks 16x6 with 1px mortar offset 8 and a 1-pixel cream highlight on top and left edges; sky in three blues with a +/-0.05 band of (x+y) mod 2 checkerboard dither at each boundary (the only gradient of that console era). Sprites: render each part on its own layer at low resolution, drop alpha < 110 (kill anti-aliasing), quantise to the nearest of 14 palette colours, then outline each layer separately in 1px black (any transparent pixel with an opaque 4-neighbour). Features placed pixel by pixel (eyes 1x2, highlights 1px, blush 2x1). Small props drawn directly in low-res, never scaled-down vector. HUD at full resolution in a pixel font at 48px (1 font pixel = 6 screen pixels) with an 8px black drop shadow; icons as 8x8 string bitmaps. All loops are stepped: tail 4-pose cycle at 15fps; HUD heart flashes every 2 frames; foliage shakes whole pixels [0,1,0,-1]; a block bumps a coin up 9px on a half sine; clouds shift 1px every 2 frames; pixel steam rises 1px per frame at 30fps on a 6px loop. Signature transition: pixel blocks scale up then down (pixelate in, resolve out).
- **Palette:** sky `#0856ef / #47c4f8 / #aae9fb`; brick highlight `#fbe2a6`; trousers far leg `#0838a8`; 14-colour sprite palette.
- **Sound:** not specified in source; score in instruments native to the period or medium (chiptune square and triangle waves fit the medium).
- **Use when:** gaming, retro tech, 1985 speedrun stop, playful onboarding, achievement or level-up moments.
- **Do not use when:** the brand needs premium or soft realism; long reading of small text; fine facial expression matters.
- **Common mistakes:** outlining the whole figure once merges hair, face and arms into one blob -> outline every part on its own layer; un-premultiplied pixel reads divided by alpha again corrupt colours -> do not divide; scaled anti-aliased vector props blur at 6px wide -> draw them natively in low-res; a 64px pixel font made HUD lines collide -> 48px.
- **Known weakness:** shared layout left a head overlapping a ledge (layout issue, made more visible by black outlines). Fast: 2.5-6ms warm.
- **Source:** alchaincyf/huashu-art-motion `references/风格配方/14_8bit.md` (MIT, translated).

## Game-trailer looks

### Pixel Sprite Diorama (`pixel-diorama`)
- **Look:** Inspired by the HD-2D look of recent console RPGs. Hand-made flat pixel sprites standing like paper cut-outs inside a lit, fogged, fully 3D miniature diorama of pixel-textured boxes, cylinders and instanced billboards, seen from a high far three-quarter camera through a tilt-shift focus band so the world reads as a tabletop model; light is the subject (practicals throw sprite shadows, shafts, fog, dust, rain, strong bloom, heavy vignette), topped by a small RPG UI. Key contrast: characters stay flat, low-res and stepped while camera, light, particles and focus move smoothly. NOT a voxel game (no cubic characters), not a flat 2D pixel RPG (real 3D set with real light), not a tilt-shift photo of a real miniature (pixel textures, not materials); no film grain.
- **Medium-specific moves:** constant texel density (e.g. 24 px/m world-space UVs, nearest filtering), sprites ~30-60 px tall with 1 px selout outline; colour from 3-6-swatch ramps through a narrow 4x4 Bayer dither; emissive pixel windows, pixel fire atlas at ~10 fps, quantized-ripple water; light rig per set: one dim shadow-casting key, hemisphere fill, practical point lights with shadows, a front fill ~2 m before the hero, an additive glow sprite on every source; post chain: supersampled render, physical DOF with tilt-shift folded into one signed circle of confusion, bloom, vignette/grade, filmic tone map; sprites step (walk 4 frames at 6-10 fps, two-frame idles at ~1-2 Hz, blinks every few seconds, instant pose cuts) while camera, lights, particles, beams and focus update every output frame (60 fps); lights breathe (summed sines ~+-20 %), one level drives sprite drawing, point light (intensity ~ level^1.3), glow opacity and scale; procedural slanted rain, lightning as a scripted flash curve (strike, dip, re-strike, decay); the set never moves, world shakes only on impact; transitions: short fades to black or cuts between sets, no wipes, no 3D spins.
  - Camera: always looking down, never eye level; monotone spline paths with no overshoot, focus recomputed every frame; very high very far slow push with the band on one tiny subject; medium-high 3/4 near-static drift for two figures; lateral tracking with blurred foreground passing; dive low and close when a figure kneels; rising pull-back with focus handed from figure to object; half orbit round a building; straight top-down with the band as a strip; rack focus in a locked frame; return to an earlier framing changed. Characters ~1/12-1/8 of frame height, band clamped ~.2-.8 of screen.
- **Palette:** two temperatures: one large ambient key (cold, pale or dusty) against small warm or saturated practicals that carry the story; darkest value a warm ink, never pure black; brightest is the bloomed light source; each set owns one fog colour and one light key; the hero wears the one saturated costume colour no set uses; UI is one metal (gold, silver, copper) plus paper white over a dark translucent plate. Examples: night harbour (blue fog, amber windows); autumn market at dusk (violet sky, pink paper lanterns); snowy mine at noon (grey-white daylight, green mineral glow).
- **Type and captions:** Cinzel (wide tracking) for chapter labels and titles; Cormorant / Cormorant Garamond italic for subtitles, dialogue and taglines. Narration subtitles: italic serif ~46-54 px centred low on a soft dark gradient band, one line per clip, in ~0.35 s before the voice. On-screen speech gets a framed dialogue box: dark plate, thin metal border, corner ornaments, name plate on its top edge, text typed over ~90 % of the voice, blinking caret, soft chime on entry. Chapter and title cards belong to the grammar; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s); srt from the overlay data.
- **Sound:** chamber-fantasy or cinematic colours (harp, celesta, strings, horns, low choir, timpani) or small folk instruments (lute, recorder, hand drum); either one piece whose arc matches the story cut inside at downbeats, or an original score, with downbeats landing on visual events. Silence: hard stop into a reverb tail, a pad alone, one heartbeat when the light nearly dies, a short breath < 1 s when something fails. Foley follows the diorama's matter: footsteps per surface (wood, dirt, stone, snow), fire crackle, cloth, UI chime for dialogue boxes and menus, small bells for things switching on, thunder after each flash, deep boom with shimmer for a big ignition; crossfaded ambience per set. Storybook narrator, slow (speed ~.82-.90); on-screen characters low-passed ~7 kHz; per-line auto-duck ~10 dB; -14 LUFS, grain 0.
- **Use when:** storybook journeys and chapters, places revealed as precious maps, onboarding or histories told in eras, light-centred metaphors (something powering up, lights joining a network, a carried light as an emotional meter), cosy fantasy-flavoured brand stories.
- **Do not use when:** the brief needs eye-level human drama or readable close-up faces (characters are tiny flat sprites); products, UI or data must be shown in detail; a flat pixel game look without depth or bloom is wanted; a hard-edged modern or corporate tone is required.
- **Common mistakes:** checkerboard everywhere -> narrow the dither band (~.22 on sprites, ~.45 on textures); a light burns out the sprite holding it -> move it ~0.45 m toward the camera and cap sprite lighting; lights behind a billboard don't light it -> always the front fill; camera-facing cylinder beams give NaN black blocks in bloom -> billboard beam cards that fade toward the lens; dark scenes unreadable -> lift environment mid-tones ~x2-2.5 except the one beat that must go dark; distant glows hidden by fog -> disable depth test and raise render order; no character to track -> fixed band height per shot; tilt as a separate blur pass breaks occlusion -> fold it into the DOF's signed CoC; headless render scripts never exit -> exit explicitly once done prints.
- **Variation slots:** structure: a day-cycle map of one set lit by different practicals each hour, or a party of three chapters converging in one room, or a top-down overworld diving into one location per point (illustration only); opening: a dialogue box already typing in a close 3/4, or straight overhead tilting down into 3/4, or a bright noon wide with no practicals so the first lamp is an event (illustration only); ending: resting at a glowing save point, or the focus band narrowing onto one sprite, or a locked wide holding after the party walks out (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/hd-2d/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Microgame Frenzy (`microgame`)
- **Look:** A rapid-fire microgame compilation film (best at 45-60 s): each microgame = a shouted command word + 2.5-4 s + one action + one result (success or funny failure), each drawn in a completely different visual medium (crayon, ink wash, terminal, risograph, pixel, blueprint, Swiss type, clay, cut paper, woodcut), held together by one fixed home look (a flat toon show stage with host, lives counter, stage number and a frame-within-the-frame), accelerating as it goes. NOT a single-style game-show film (no rounds of bars or call-and-response), not a style sampler reel (every game has stakes, lives connect them), not a montage (each game's rule is grasped in under a second).
- **Medium-specific moves:** home stage: ~7 px near-black outline, flat fills, one hard-edged shadow (shade colour clipped and refilled shifted up-left), fixed furniture (TV/cabinet/phone/window screen, lives board, stage badge, trigger prop); each medium is a simplified 100-200-line re-drawing built on one channel trick (drawn media store pressure or wetness, print media store plate densities with halftone and misregistration, screen media are low-res buffers, technical media are line systems); the hero is redrawn per medium but keeps two or three silhouette marks, with a model sheet per medium first; five things accelerate together at each speed-up (tempo, game length, stage dwell, command hold, character frame rate from twos/8 fps to ones at the climax), camera, transitions and timer spark always on ones; visible on-beat timer (e.g. a fuse with one knot per beat), frozen in replay; unified transition through the frame-within-the-frame (push in ~0.25 s to start, shrink back ~0.33 s with a check or cross stamp), no fades or blank frames; failure is performed (overshoot, splat, bonk, roll off by the medium's physics); instant replay (freeze, half speed, push-in, letterbox, caption in the medium's type); host ticks on every beat, stopping dead is the strongest emotional tool.
  - Camera: start inside a game with the command already slamming; pull back out of the game screen; locked frontal home frame; slot reels of earlier frames stopping one per beat for a speed-up; extreme insert on the trigger prop; lights cut to one top spot for the last life; tile flip of past games' last frames into a new frame for a finale; push into a detail and hold; snap back to wide on one beat; split screen of two media. Game screen ~40 % of frame width; one focal chain in mixed-media frames, info panels in a corner.
- **Palette:** home stage owns a saturated party palette: deep ground behind a rotating sunburst, two or three loud accents, plus fixed success and fail colours used only by stamps and the lives board; each medium brings its own traditional palette (wax colours, one ink plus vermilion, amber phosphor, three riso plates, limited pixel palette, white on blue, black-white-red), never tinted with the home palette; one accent follows the hero across media where possible. Example home palettes: violet `#2a0f5c` with magenta `#ff2e88`, gold `#ffc928`, cyan `#1fd1d1`; teal ground with coral and lemon; black stage with hot orange and electric blue.
- **Type and captions:** the command word is the subtitle: heavy display face (e.g. Titan One), white with thick ink outline, coloured drop and burst, skewed a few degrees, slams in over ~3 frames (overshoot, settle), holds, then shrinks into a corner tag before the first action; one-off banners fly out so tags never pile up; command word goes in the srt only. Other lines: rounded home-palette card with home outline and hard shadow, Lilita One ~50 px, small speaker icon, above the timer; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s), never under a big banner. Text inside a game uses that medium's own typography.
- **Sound:** music first: tempo grid and cue table before animation, every picture hit on a listed cue; a constant groove (kit with ghost notes, slap or synth bass, brass stabs, keys) plus one colour instrument per medium (toy piano for crayon, shakuhachi or zither for ink, square beeps for terminals, vibes for print, pulse arpeggios for pixel, typewriter for drafting, claves for Swiss, kalimba for clay, koto for woodcut). Foley follows the medium (wax squeaks, wet splats, teletype relays, paper crunch, bit-crushed noise, ratchets, stamps, hollow bonks); home stage: spring trigger click, glass crack for a lost life, card flips, reel whirr, sparse claps. Acceleration options: rising brass run into speed-ups, key up a step per round, halving note values, groove dropping to a heartbeat on the last life. At least one real silence before the biggest hit; the loudest moment follows the longest silence. Host shouts each command a hair after the stab; hero has very few words; duck music ~11 dB, foley ~6 dB; -14 LUFS.
- **Use when:** energetic short-form content: steps of a process shown as quick tasks, a day or shift under rising pressure, roles in a team, retries and chances, social-media promos, playful recaps, finales that combine earlier pieces.
- **Do not use when:** the film runs much longer than ~60 s (frenzy is lost); the brand needs one consistent visual identity throughout; the content is calm, serious or needs time to read; a single verb per beat cannot describe the steps.
- **Common mistakes:** luminance-ramp ASCII figure is mush -> hand-authored glyph art with structural glyphs and an inverse accent glyph; ink shapes read as smoke -> designed silhouette (short blot phase, then structure with seams and light edges); failure unread at high tempo -> overshoot into something or add an instant replay; alpha-coverage media show the background through gaps -> full-pressure base where solid; drawing helpers hit the wrong canvas offscreen -> set and restore the context; command tags pile up -> secondary commands fly out; a 100 % white flash reads as blank -> cap at ~60 %; TTS single-word commands gain a vowel tail -> generate a two-word phrase, cut after the first word via ASR timestamps, verify each clip.
- **Variation slots:** structure: two rivals alternating with the lives board as a tug-of-war, or one day hour by hour with morning and evening rush speed-ups, or a relay where each result is handed into the next game (illustration only); opening: the home stage asleep until the host wakes on beat one, or a game-over screen rewound, or the lives board alone clicking down (illustration only); ending: a high-score table where the hero types their name in each medium, or credits as one more microgame, or the stage packing away into the screen as it switches off (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/microgame/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

### Game Show Flat (`game-show`)
- **Look:** A rhythm-game variety show in flat vector (45 s with three rounds up to ~3 min with eight): toy-like bean mascots with thick ink outlines on candy-coloured stripe, polka-dot and sunburst sets, every hit on one fixed fast tempo grid, call-and-response patterns, round cards and a judgement word when each round is won; variety-show telops with thick outlined captions. NOT a microgame frenzy (no per-game timer, no art style change per round), not a mid-century cartoon (no textured painted backgrounds, no character acting), not a keynote (no slow reveals, no gradients).
- **Medium-specific moves:** vector only at 1920x1080: flat fills, one near-black outline, 8 px on characters and props, 5-7 px on small parts, round joins and caps; no gradients, blur, texture or soft shadow; depth = hard offset shadow (same shape in outline colour shifted ~10 px for stickers and banners, ~14 px for cards); sets = one saturated background + same-hue pattern one step lighter (slow-scrolling diagonal stripes, ~20 % white offset dots, rotating sunburst) + darker floor band with an ink top line; one body construction for the whole cast (bean body, stubby arms, mitten hands, oval eyes with catch-light, swap-to-open mouth), identity by colour, belly patch, one head accessory and a name tag; render is a pure function of time; idle = parabolic hop per beat (8-30 px) with neighbours on the off-beat so crowds ripple; hit = damped wobble (~30 rad/s, ~0.35 s), small squash every downbeat; entrances drop from the sky with ease-in landing on their beat; pops back-ease, exits ease-in, nothing fades in slowly; shout pose (arms up, mouth open) ~half a beat; typewriter reveals on beat fractions with a click every few characters; scene changes are hard cuts on bar lines with a very short white flash; nothing moves off the grid.
  - Camera: frontal symmetric proscenium, the frame moves; beat punch (zoom 0.8-5 % around centre, decays ~0.16 s); several punches in a bar; locked frontal wide; hard cut on the bar line + flash; split stage of two sets; shrink a finished bit to a thumbnail slot; vertical scroll floor to sky; snap zoom to 2x on one prop and back next bar. Keep summed simultaneous punches below ~0.06; banners top-left, judgement words centre-top; hard cuts only.
- **Palette:** ink + paper + candy: near-black ink, white, warm cream for cards and paper props, about eight saturated candy hues (yellow, orange, red, pink, green, lime, sky, blue) plus two or three deep hues with darker partners for night or finale sets; one set = one hue (background, pattern and round card share it), neighbouring rounds never share a hue; a contestant's body colour is fixed identity and never matches the set behind it; big words white or yellow with thick ink stroke, judgement words pink or yellow. Examples: tangerine ground `#FFB870` with `#FFA85A` stripes; teal with mint dots; navy with a gold sunburst.
- **Type and captions:** heavy rounded sans for Latin and numbers, a black-weight sans for every other script on screen (Arabic: the ice-arabic skill), a hand-lettered face only for handwritten props (families chosen per the ice-direction skill, `references/type-pairing.md`). Telop text: white or yellow fill with ink stroke ~14 % of font size painted under the fill, titles built glyph by glyph to pop. The telops are the subtitles (round cards, corner banners, labels, prop text, judgement words), short (<= ~20 CJK characters per banner line, <= ~12 on a plate, Latin ~1.6x wider); with narration, subtitles are telop plates at the bottom in the same outlined type. Hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s); a judgement word may be shorter.
- **Sound:** all synthesized from the picture's events: sine kick with soft saturation, band-passed noise snare and clap, hats, slap bass, detuned-saw brass stabs with closing filter, square lead with vibrato, marimba-like pluck, music-box bell, triangle pad, square arp, cowbell; bright major pop harmony on a short loop, one chord per bar, a flavour per round (funk, quiz, electro, dream, heavy), not a new song. Toy-like pitched event foley (pops, plop + boing landings, stamps, typewriter clicks, buzzers, dings, servo whirs, splats; pitched pips can spell a melody as a crowd drops in). Shouts not sentences: one- to three-word calls 20-30 ms before the beat so the consonant lands on it; crowd shout = several voices stacked a few ms apart and panned. Intensity options: jingle (crash + rising brass + snare roll) into each round, transposition up for a finale, drums only for a bar before a big hit, a half-time bar for a reveal, a bar of silence broken by one shout. Mix: voices and foley on their own bus, music ducked from its envelope, soft-clip not hard limit, -14 LUFS, true peak <= -1 dB.
- **Use when:** step-by-step or staged topics turned into rounds (recipes, startup stages, tours), turn-taking dynamics, stats as scores and counters, year-in-review and line-up remixes, playful launches, kids-friendly or social content, films that can run on telops and shouts without narration.
- **Do not use when:** the topic needs calm, serious or premium tone; characters must act subtly or emotionally; content is a single slow reveal or dense explanation; the brand forbids flat candy colours or mascot characters.
- **Common mistakes:** stateful updates break parallel renders -> recompute every pose from t and compare single stills with a sequential render; cue lists drift when flashes, jingles and arrangement are separate lists -> derive all from one table; fixed-length audio buffers cut tails -> size from duration; text measured before fonts load overflows plates -> load fonts, then build; TTS misreads digits and symbols in shouts -> spell them out; real products as characters -> names on tags, original mascots, no logos or UI, real people only as generic stand-ins.
- **Variation slots:** structure: a tournament bracket ending on a split stage, or one long round escalating a single pattern from quarters to sixteenths, or a three-contestant quiz where each question is a fact (illustration only); opening: the judgement word first on an empty stage then rewind, or a lone contestant hopping until the crowd drops in, or the scoreboard counting back from the final number (illustration only); ending: a group photo freeze becoming a polaroid card, or a level-select map of lit nodes, or a curtain falling bar by bar under the last shout (illustration only).
- **Source:** lemomo-ai/lemo-opuscar `styles/game-show/STYLE.md` (MIT; demo media CC BY 4.0 LemoLab).

## Toys and miniatures

### Toy bricks (merged: two sources)
Both sources agree: never name the trademark; rigid parts, no squash or stretch; parts seat along their insertion axis; build steps land on beats. The film variant shoots bricks as macro stop-motion; the hand variant is an instruction-booklet build in orthographic view.

### Brick Toy (`brick-toy`)
- **Look:** Stop-motion films of a world built from interlocking plastic toy bricks with round studs, set on a real surface (wooden desk, kitchen table, windowsill) and shot like macro toy photography: glossy ABS with clean rectangular softbox highlights, studs on every top face (no lettering), seams darkened by contact shadow, real household objects out of focus as landscape, characters moving in held poses on twos. Characters are built from bricks (1x1 columns for legs, a 2x2 brick torso, round bricks for arms, a round 2x2 helmet); never use any toy-brick brand name, logo or a trademarked figure silhouette. NOT a low-poly isometric island (no flat facets, no floating diorama), not clay or felt stop-motion (bricks are rigid, never deform), not a clean product render of a toy (this world is played with, handled, knocked over).
- **Medium-specific moves:** plastic: strong clearcoat ~0.85 with clearcoat roughness ~0.07, roughness map with faint fingerprints and scratches (base ~0.17, scratches up to ~0.65), bevelled edges; unit = 1 stud pitch (8 mm), brick height 1.2, plate 0.4, bevel ~0.045 stud, large baseplates matte; real props at true scale (1 m = 125 studs); neutral daylight studio HDRI, never tungsten; directional warm-white key with shadow frustum fitted per shot, two or three rect-area softboxes (key front, cool rim behind, one over a big set), environment ~0.45, contact-shadow blob behind hero objects; GTAO mandatory; 2x supersampling; physical DOF scaled with shot size (widest for close-ups), focus on a character's head or the brick being placed; effects (fire, smoke, water, light, weather, sun and moon) are built from plates, round and translucent pieces, jittered or rebuilt per drawing, never particles or soft gradients; characters and bricks step at 12 fps, camera, focus, fire and flying vehicles on ones at 24 fps; bricks fly in on arcs and snap with a tiny overshoot (fraction of a stud to 0 in ~4 frames), every snap a click; big readable held poses (V arms for pride, arms flung up for alarm, bowed head, hop for joy), walk legs swing ~+-0.55 rad with small bob, joints rotate, nothing bends; collapses use pre-simulated rigid-body physics (tip about the base edge, lower-than-true gravity, restitution ~0.3, friction, settling); bricks never stretch, squash or morph; transitions: a hand-placed brick covering the lens, a whip along the desk, a pull out of one set into the next, cuts on beats or snaps; no generic dissolves.
  - Camera: extreme macro on one brick or stud with the room blurred; low angle at toy-figure eye level; high wide over the whole surface for the scale reveal; low wide lonely frame with a small character; frontal close-up with hands and brick in focus; handheld shake on impact; tracking alongside a moving object with destination ahead; top-down plan; rack focus between a real object and a brick; orbit around a finished build. Keep clutter off frame edges, check headroom, check every real prop against every camera.
- **Palette:** toy palette of a handful of saturated brick primaries plus white, black, greys, tan; each built object one dominant colour with at most one or two trims, characters and heroes get the most saturated; the real world stays natural and muted (wood, paper, ceramic, daylight); whites must read white (grade slightly cool, soft contrast, saturation +~10 %); translucent orange, yellow, clear and blue reserved for effects. Example palette: red `#c91a09`, blue `#0055bf`, yellow `#f2cd37`, green `#237841`, white `#f2f2ee`, black `#1b2a34`; alternatives: pastels and tan, greys with one accent.
- **Type and captions:** a rounded friendly OFL face (e.g. Fredoka) for titles and subtitles; subtitles on a toy element (dark rounded pill low in frame with a small brick icon, or a printed tile, or a sign built into the set), white on dark, <= 2 lines, a second speaker type gets its own icon colour or tag; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). Titles pop in on 12 fps steps like a piece being placed, with a short sub-line in a brick colour.
- **Sound:** small, acoustic, playful instruments (glockenspiel, toy piano, pizzicato, ukulele, whistling, claps, melodica), a brass or orchestral swell for scale, a heartbeat drum for suspense; licensed or original, edited to picture (cut on bars, bar-aligned jumps, matched harmony at joins) rather than looped. Synthesizable foley: plastic creak before a failure, tiny plastic footsteps, a crash of many clacks, a brick click (short transient + resonances near 2.9 kHz and 4.6 kHz), clack on wood, rattle of a stirred parts pile, throw whooshes, a discovery "ding", bricks sliding on paper, motors from filtered noise. Silence: cut music dead at a failure or surprise, leave room tone and one real-world sound (ticking clock, fridge hum, distant bird); or drop music before a big moment leaving a pulse or countdown. Warm storyteller voice with breathing room; secondary voices band-passed ~380-2800 Hz with soft saturation, optional beeps; whisper-verified; music ducked ~40 % under voice; -14 LUFS.
- **Use when:** stories about building, assembling, growing or rebuilding (companies, products, neighbourhoods, recipes), pivots and recycling (break without harm), playful explainers on a desk, weather or science demos, scale reveals of big ambitions, family and kids-friendly brand stories.
- **Do not use when:** the brief needs organic deformation, realistic people or soft materials; any recognisable trademarked toy-brick branding or figure would be required; the tone must be serious, premium-corporate or minimal-abstract; fast smooth character animation is needed (stepped stop-motion only).
- **Common mistakes:** tungsten HDRI gives orange whites -> neutral studio HDRI and a slightly cool grade; clearcoat baseplate reflects softboxes as a coloured wash -> make it matte; flat jagged first renders -> softboxes + low environment, GTAO, 2x supersampling, cool grade, camera on ones; too much aperture blurs the hero at mid distance -> scale aperture with shot size; a fast-moving object becomes a dot on a blank wall -> track it with its destination in frame; curved transparent parts vanish inside cylinders -> use an open-cylinder panel; real props sneak into later shots -> check props against every camera; random jitter as a material index goes negative -> clamp.
- **Variation slots:** structure: a catalogue of builds each answering the same question, lined up at the end, or two builders whose builds grow toward each other, or a disassembly explaining what is inside (illustration only); opening: a hand pours a bin of bricks that settles into the first shape, or a real object first then rack focus to the tiny world at its base, or a plan view of an empty baseplate filling in (illustration only); ending: one leftover piece held in close-up, or the room lights go out leaving one lit brick window, or a real hand picks the character up (illustration only).

### Toy brick (`toyBrick`, hand)
- **Look:** moulded plastic toy bricks rendered flat in orthographic view (azimuth 45, elevation 30) in real units, so proportions are right by construction. Never name the trademark anywhere.
- **Medium-specific moves:** one tone per face by the cube rule (top +0.10, lit side 0, far side -0.22), a bevel highlight where top meets side and down visible vertical corners, a dark seam hairline between parts, studs as real cylinders (shadow crescent, lit and dark side bands, top ellipse, highlight crescent); the only blur is the ground shadow. Order: instruction-booklet grammar: bare backdrop, baseplate drops in, step by step on the beat, at most four parts per step in 5-frame slots, bottom up and back to front, parts fall straight down their insertion axis on an ease-out ghosted from 35 % alpha and seat with a one-frame press-fit; the current step's parts wear a highlight outline until the next step. Rigid: no squash, no stretch, no bounce; take-apart is the build reversed frame for frame.
- **Source:** alexgreensh/anidoodle `references/styles/toyBrick.md` (Apache-2.0).

### Tilt-Shift Miniature (`tilt-shift`)
- **Look:** A real-looking photoreal place seen from high above through a tilt-shift lens (only a thin horizontal strip is sharp, everything above and below melts into blur), with saturated sunny "model-railway paint" colour and time-lapse stutter, so the whole world reads as a tabletop model. NOT a toy or clay render (the world is photoreal, only lens and clock make it small), not a game's top-down view (no UI, no clean flat shading), not drone footage (band, saturation and stutter are mandatory); no real landmarks, brands or logos.
- **Medium-specific moves:** always high: oblique 45-55 degree pitch from 100-200 m with a moderately long lens (~27-30 degree vertical FOV), or straight top-down; street level only for a deliberate intimate medium close-up with a blurred foreground object framing sharp actors filling ~1/4-1/3 of frame height; blur band = physical DOF mixed with a screen-space band, CoC clamped ~30 px, band put on the subject and slid with it every frame, narrowed for intimacy, gathered after a 2x supersampled render with spiral taps so far samples never bleed onto sharp near ones; grade: neutral tone mapping, strong saturation boost, soft contrast, slightly warm highlights, vignette, lower saturation at night; hard sun with sharp shadows, low hemisphere fill with warm ground bounce, sky-gradient environment, sun azimuth cheated per shot to front-light visible facades; model-like detail (varied window types, 1.6-3 m bays, dark reveals, grime, reflective glass, walls darkening toward the ground, roof clutter, awnings, signs, worn markings, vehicles with dark glass, contact shadows and brake lights, AO); people as small colour capsules, close-up actors 20-30 % larger than real; time-lapse stutter is the soul: in fast-forward the world (vehicles, people, lights, sun) updates at a low rate and holds each position several frames (8 Hz = 3 frames at 24 fps) with jumps on the musical grid, while the camera stays smooth every frame; below ~x2 the stutter disappears and motion turns silky, so a drop to real time hits hard; simple pre-computed simulations (car-following, signal cycles, crowds) with cycle offsets landing changes on bar lines; hero actors get art-directed beat-fitted paths; close-up acting: anticipation squash, stretch, land squash, settle, a failed attempt before success; transitions: cuts on the beat, speed ramps, dives and rises, avoid dissolves.
  - Camera: high oblique wide with very slow slide; band follows one subject across a wide; rack of the band between subjects; slow push on an intersection, station or yard; straight top-down slowly rotating; continuous dive from high to actor height with a speed ramp; band narrowing to a sliver; vertical rise with logarithmic height; lateral track along a rail, river or road; locked frame across hours.
- **Palette:** saturated, sunny, freshly painted model: pale warm ground of buildings with a few candy accents, very saturated greens, dark ground surfaces so white markings and vehicles pop; shadows stay blue and clean, never grey; night and dawn are the only low-saturation states, dawn must read as early morning (warm low fill from the sunrise side, few lit windows); the subject to be found is the one saturated thing in the sharp band or moves against the flow. Examples: Mediterranean harbour (whitewash, cobalt shutters, terracotta, turquoise water); snowy ski village (white, pine green, red gondolas, long blue shadows); desert market (sand, saffron, indigo awnings, dusty olive).
- **Type and captions:** subtitles belong to the world's signage: a panel in the graphic language of local signs (road sign, platform board, harbour notice) set in an open sign-revival face (illustration only: Overpass; choose per the ice-direction skill, `references/type-pairing.md`), sitting inside the lower blur band; hold >= max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s). A time-lapse clock with a monospace speed readout (e.g. "x24 TIME-LAPSE" / "x1 REAL TIME") whenever speed changes carry meaning. Titles live in the world (letters on a roof, sign or field, unblocked, lit or revealed on the beat).
- **Sound:** tuned percussion carries the style (marimba, glockenspiel, vibraphone, woodblocks, kalimba, celesta), pulse-based minimalist grammar (phasing, voices added one at a time, pulsing chords), plus plucked strings or a small reed section, not generic piano and strings; options: world events gate notes of a fixed phrase skeleton with a base fill rate, tuned horns or bells as chords, a tape-stop into silence when time slows, single notes for single events, phasing two identical lines, a music-box theme for the smallest scale. Sound follows speed: in time-lapse music first with a faint granular sped-up hiss, clacks and relay clicks; at real speed the world turns suddenly real (idling engines, distant traffic, birds, small voices, footsteps); on release engines rev and wind rises. Silence marks the arrival of real time (cut music, keep one or two real sounds). Relaxed voice, few short lines, numbers written as words, whisper-verified; music ducked ~8 dB; -14 LUFS.
- **Use when:** places and systems seen whole (ports, airports, farms, markets, cities, festivals, construction sites), routines and rush hours, logistics and flows, one small actor or moment that matters inside a big system, day-to-night or before/after time-lapses, tourism and urban storytelling.
- **Do not use when:** the brief needs eye-level human drama throughout, interiors, or product close-ups; the world must look like a toy or stylised render; real landmarks or brands must be shown; the content has no place or system to observe.
- **Common mistakes:** tall buildings plus shallow pitch hide the ground -> low to mid-rise buildings, pitch 45 degrees or more; subject hidden in a canyon or behind a facade -> route it on the camera side and let the band follow; instance caps silently drop actors -> cull by distance (~4x camera height) and raise the cap; simulated vehicles stop short of lines -> subtract the jam gap where contact matters; focus lags a frame -> update actors before the camera; top-down to oblique dive twists -> rotate the top-down so screen-up matches the target shot; close-up from high up is too far -> go low and close with a small near plane; clouds wash out a rise -> unlit white, low opacity, nothing below the camera; light pools read as bubbles -> denser, larger, fainter pools; scene backlit and grey -> cheat the sun per shot; dawn reads as midnight -> warm low fill, fewer lit windows.
- **Variation slots:** structure: one locked vantage from dawn to night with one shop's light the last to go out, or a relay where the band hands attention from actor to actor, or a construction time-lapse from empty lot to opening day with one real-time moment per stage (illustration only); opening: mid-bustle with the band already on one oddity, or a top-down pattern slowly tilting into a place, or real time first at actor height before the lens lifts and time speeds up (illustration only); ending: a drop to real time that stays there, or lights going out one by one from high above, or the lens un-tilting until the model becomes a real place (illustration only).

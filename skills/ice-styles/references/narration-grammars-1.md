# Narration grammars, part 1: selection, flat science world, investigator collage, whiteboard, continuous derivation

Every entry's examples are illustration only. Hex values record what the measured channels used; they are evidence, not a palette: keep the role structure (ground, line, one accent in its tones, highlighter, concept colours) and take every value from the film's palette in `ice/DIRECTION.md` (the ice-direction skill).

A narration grammar is the visual language of a voiceover-led film: what the picture does while a voice explains. The nine grammars below were measured from the channels they are named after (shot lengths, still ratios, easing, writing speeds, colours). The names describe a genre of explainer only: never use those channels' logos, characters, mascots or names in a film, and never imply affiliation.

Part 1 (this file): selection rules, y1, y2, y3, t1. Part 2 (`narration-grammars-2.md`): y4 storytime, y5 kinetic type, t2 keynote UI, t3 finance chart, y6 presenter explainer.

The explainer workflow (script, VO, word timing, clip contract, assembly) is owned by the ice-explainer skill. Camera density rules are owned by ice-motion, caption rules by ice-type, VO pacing by ice-audio, film gates by ice-qa. This file holds the look and motion language of each grammar.

## 1. Catalog and selection

| id | Use for | Core grammar | Typical shot length | Main easing | Main transitions | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y1 flat science world | scale, "what is inside", mechanisms, calm science voice | the abstract idea becomes a concrete object in an endlessly zoomable glowing world; camera travels across scales; environment gently moves, subject changes on VO nodes | hard-cut shots 7-9 s; visual unit changes every 4.6-6.7 s | cubic-bezier(.75,0,.25,1); loops Easy Ease (.333,0,.667,1) | dive into an object (lens reveal), fill-then-pull-out scale cut (log zoom) | real people, events or archives (use y2); steps (y3); tiny budget |
| y2 investigator collage | real archives, news, screenshots, quotes, "look at this line" | the argument laid on a desk of real material; the camera is an investigator's eye; lines and pens drawn in steps | slow tier median 3.3 s (cards 3-7 s); fast tier 1.4-1.8 s | camera sine in-out or long tail; elements stepped at 12 fps | hard cut, pan along a red string, push to fill a photo then cut | pure concepts without real material (becomes a fake archive) |
| y3 whiteboard | reasoning chains, steps, "why" | say a line, write a line; camera moves on one big board; a final pull-out shows the whole map | median 6.6 s per block | board pan sine in-out 1.2-2.4 s; far jump 0.8 s whip with blur | same board (the camera move is the transition), closing pull-out | precise data charts, complex spatial action, high emotion |
| y4 storytime | first-person anecdotes, fails, jokes | the voice is the star, the picture is a reaction sticker: 0.1 s pose snaps then hold; jokes via a cut to a reaction close-up and silence | ASL 2.43 s (median 1.87 s) | pose expo-out 0.1 s plus a squash settle; character 24 fps | cut, smash, whip, jump-in | dense tutorials or data, no "I", an unfunny script |
| y5 kinetic type | lists, "X stages", quotes, number openers, chapter cards | words are actors: key words slam on accents, main word readable at a glance, one focus | one bar per shot at the film's own tempo (<tempo from ice/plan/cue-sheet.json>; the measured channels sat near 120 BPM, about 2 s per bar, evidence only) | expo-out in, spring slam, expo-in exit (faster) | fill-zoom, colour bands, push | stories and emotion, long dense VO, more than 5 list items |
| t1 continuous derivation | how one mechanism turns into another (networks, matrices, gradients) | only the thing being discussed on black; transform the previous concept into the next; colour = concept name; almost no cuts | single animation 1-3 s plus a 1 s wait; one concept 8-20 s | smoothstep 6t^5 - 15t^4 + 10t^3 almost everywhere | transform continuation, zoom through, fade-shift | product launch, emotion, real data trends, quick cuts under 8 s |
| t2 keynote UI | product and feature launches, updates, tutorial steps, specs | one thing per screen; the product screenshot or object is the hero and gets pushed into; nouns and numbers settle when spoken | keynote 5-8 s per screen; product quick cuts 1.5-3 s | spring (bounce 0-0.15), ease-out | blur push, shared-element expand | mechanisms (t1), trends (t3), bad news, no hero to enlarge |
| t3 finance chart | revenue, prices, share, inflection points | build axes, grow data, annotate one thing; values always honest | 2.5-6 s per chart | quint-out; lines smoothstep | slide push, zoom through, hard cut keeping layout | a single number (use t2 or y5), unreliable or unsourced data |
| y6 presenter explainer | business history, product mechanisms, emotional reactions | a cartoon presenter links paper, charts and keywords | per section | per section | page turn keeping presenter and top bar | no frame library for the presenter |

Selection rules:
- Choose by what the voice is doing in that section. A film may switch grammar by section, never mix two inside one section. At most two grammars per film is the comfortable default for parameterized clips; all sections share one theme palette.
- Concept or mechanism: t1 (derivation, formulas) or y1 (scale, friendly), not y2.
- News or history with screenshots and quotes: y2, not y1.
- Personal experience or jokes: y4, not t2.
- Lists, stages, a quote: y5, not t1.
- Product, tutorial, specs: t2, not t1.
- Numbers, growth, trend, comparison: t3; a single number in a section gets no chart (t2 or y5 big number that settles when spoken).
- Step-by-step argument: y3, not full-screen page swaps.
- Real screenshots exist: t2 (tutorial, clean) or y2 (argument, evidence).
- Measure before you define a new grammar: break down 2-5 representative works (shot length, still ratio, transition kinds). Impressions of "signature features" are often wrong: torn-paper transitions appeared zero times in five measured collage-explainer films; whiteboard films never pushed into thought bubbles; a storytime channel averaged 2.4 s shots with about 70 % fully static frames.
- Same voice, split by explanatory task: real posts or drafts suit y2 desk tracing and highlights; rules, controlled variables and sound-picture relations suit t1 continuous transforms. Lock voice, subtitles and timeline before replacing visuals.

Craft shared by the nine grammars of both parts (each grammar's own values are in its section):
- **Craft profile (family default):** needs: voice (the voiceover leads: every grammar is the picture of a voice, the `ice-audio` skill, `references/voiceover.md`), rhythm (the picture changes on the voice's nodes at the grammar's shot length, table above), camera (the grammar's own camera: travel across scales, an investigator's eye, a board pan, a push into the hero), transition (the grammar's main transitions, table above), type (words on screen synced to the voice, in the grammar's own text treatment); serves when: data (a number, a trend or a comparison in the speech: the t3 chart rules, values always honest), footage (real documents, screenshots or product UI named in the speech: as y2 and t2 use them), sfx (an action, a landing or a write-on: one sound under the voice), music (a bed under the voice, ducked, or none under speech where the grammar says so), silence (the key line or a joke: the bed drops out), depth (a world or a desk of material: scale layers or stacked cards).

## 2. y1 Flat science world

- **Look:** Inspired by Kurzgesagt. Flat shapes with no outlines; shadow is the same hue one step darker (never grey); highlight is a white ellipse or arc; everything rounded; glowing bodies always carry a radial glow. Adding outlines or mid-brightness backgrounds slides it into children's-book or corporate flat illustration.
- **Medium-specific moves:** write the visual metaphor first (the abstract concept as a cell, gear, pipe, city or drop), never a glowing ball with capsule labels; at least one state change per VO sentence (pop, morph, dive, pull-out), two sentences without change means add an event, not a float or a slow push; the subject settles then holds, the environment (twinkle, particles, far clouds) may keep moving gently, no floating or breathing on subject and labels; before a dive the target holds at least 0.3 s and lights up; zoom is log-interpolated.
- **Facets:** two-tone facet per shape (shadow band bottom-right, thin highlight top-left; offsets about 10-12 px, widths about 4-5 px); characters get the same treatment so they do not look pasted.
- **Composition:** subject on the lower third line, sky above for stars and labels; 5-6 parallax layers (stars depth 0.05, moon 0.12, far hills 0.3, near slope 0.65, subject 1.0, blurred dark foreground leaves 1.5); layer offset linear in depth, scale z^depth; front leaves blur about 7 px, colour lifted from near-black so they do not read as dirt.
- **Timing:** normal action about 50 frames at 60 fps (0.83 s), crisp 30/20/10 frames, pop 5-10 frames; pop reaches 90 % in 0.25 s, overshoots to 108 % (up to 114 %) around frame 20, back to 100 % by about 0.5 s; labels back-out 0.5 s; stagger same-type elements 10-20 frames, limbs 3-4 frames, a 20-frame pause between actions; blink 5 frames closed, 10 open, irregular intervals, eyes never shrink to 0.
- **Camera:** about one third of time zooming, about 10 % panning, about 5 % rotating; pull-outs outnumber push-ins; zooms are scale travel and reveals, never stall fillers. Hard cuts about 5 per minute, 0-0.2 s before a sentence starts; other changes are 0.3-0.6 s designed transitions every 4.6-6.7 s; about 168 words per minute, about two sentences per hard-cut shot.
- **Transitions:** scale transition (push through, the object colour fills the frame, new scene, about 1.4 s, accelerate then decelerate, peak mid): log push 0.45 s cubic-in to the fill colour, dissolve within plus or minus 3 frames at the midpoint to a matching light, then cubic in-out 1.45 s pull from about 42x to 1x; never expo-out (the first frame jumps most of the scale). Dive-in lens reveal about 1.0 s, log push 1.1 to 60x, next scene in a growing circle. Fast pull-out reveal 0.2-2 s long tail. Light flash fill about 0.5 s. Sweep wipe about 0.4 s with speed lines. Duplicate-to-fill 3-6 s.
- **Callouts:** thin white leader line, then typed text about 40 characters per second; one callout per sentence; hold about 3 s then erase character by character; a chapter title card every 2-3 minutes, full screen, word by word over a blurred background; label capsules have a big radius, saturated fill, bold sans.
- **Palette:** one dark base with 2-3 high-saturation contrasting accents (orange-yellow against indigo, cyan against magenta). Do not mix eras of the look (recent dark films are about 61-68 % dark pixels with saturation median 0.84-0.89; older films were light grey-white and low saturation). The measured deep-purple base is analysis only: in production use a dark ink or flat navy base, because the blue-purple default-look gate (ice-qa) applies.
- **Use when:** scale journeys, biology, physics, space, "what is inside", calm science narration.
- **Do not use when:** real archives, people or events; step-by-step procedures; bad news that needs sobriety.
- **Common mistakes:** "always moving" turned into everything breathing -> only the environment moves, subjects settle and hold; linear zoom or bitmap zoom -> log zoom on a vector world canvas; bright mixed palette or outlines -> one dark base, 2-3 accents, no outlines; the demo's centre planet copied -> the centre object follows the subject; static opening fixed with a uniform slow push -> move the first event earlier. Beat punch zoom stays off by default in explainers.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: light (flat shapes with no outlines: each shadow the same hue one step darker, never grey, a white highlight ellipse or arc, a two-tone facet per shape, glowing bodies always with a radial glow, entry Look and Facets), depth (5-6 parallax layers from stars to blurred dark foreground leaves, layer offset linear in depth, entry Composition), camera (about a third of the time zooming on a log scale and pull-outs outnumbering push-ins; zooms are scale travel and reveals, never stall fillers, entry Camera and Common mistakes), rhythm (at least one state change per sentence of the voice; the subject settles then holds while only the environment keeps moving; a target holds at least 0.3 s and lights up before a dive, entry moves and Timing), type (callouts with a thin white leader, typed at about 40 characters per second, held about 3 s and erased character by character, one per sentence; lifts `lint:T1` and `slop:N9` for those callouts, entry Callouts), transition (the scale transition through a matching light with a dissolve of at most plus or minus 3 frames at its midpoint; lifts `slop:N24` for that dissolve, entry Transitions); never: footage (real people, events or archives move to y2, entry Do not use when).

## 3. y2 Investigator collage

- **Look:** Inspired by Vox explainers. An argument on a desk of real material: archive photos as halftone grey at 45 degrees with a white border and two translucent tape strips; clippings torn and yellowed; labels as a black bar with white bold text; ruled index cards; static paper texture (no per-frame grain: that is a different, film-grain documentary look); weak shadows (blur about 10, offset (3,6), alpha about 0.28). It should not look perfect, which would read as an ad rather than editorial.
- **Medium-specific moves:** real material is the main picture (if material width stays under 60 % of the frame for more than two consecutive shots it has become a picture-frame layout); rotate reveal actions (already there at the cut, push in, glide to the next, highlighter sweep, red string, stamp, tear-off), never the same reveal more than 3 times in a row; text that must be read is drawn last, on top, regardless of its object's layer; camera landings computed from what must be read now (target plus margin), never hand-typed, and half-visible neighbour labels pushed out of frame; an action shown in the wide shot is not replayed after the push-in.
- **Timing:** camera smooth at 24 fps; elements stepped at 12 fps on twos (about half of motion frames show the move-stop-move fingerprint); camera moves median 1.29 s (0.71-1.96), never linear; long moves sine in-out; reveals and string-following cubic-bezier(.33,0,.2,1). Archive photos and clippings simply exist at the cut (no pop, no drop); only in timeline tracking does the next card slide in tilted 5-15 degrees and flatten (about 0.8 s). Red underline 0.5-1 s; red ellipse about 1 s in one stroke, ends not closed and slightly crossing; highlighter one line 1.5-3 s following speech; typing about 13 characters per second on twos.
- **Rhythm:** two tempo tiers: slow about 12 cuts per minute (cards 3-7 s) and fast 25-33 cuts per minute (images still, rhythm from cuts). Highlighter, typing and line drawing lock to speech, not music beats: each spoken word should match an action in the picture. Music changes about every 20 s. First event within 0.3 s (photo already there, highlighter or string starting) or a 0.17 s slam opener; never a slow push opener.
- **Transitions:** hard cuts dominate, on sentence punctuation; pan along a red string 1.3-1.8 s long tail; push into a paper or photo then cut to the same image's high-resolution scan (1.0-1.4 s S-curve); pull out from one photo to the whole collage (about 1.0 s); map fills and route draws 2.5-3.5 s on twos with a red dot head; hand page-flip.
- **Palette:** near-black `#171716`, paper white `#E0DCDA` to `#EEEDED`, one red in two tones (line `#B65255`, fill `#93403C`), highlighter `#DACF08`, old map `#E4DCCF`, parchment `#CFB195`; no pure white, no pure black; never a brand-logo yellow as a large fill.
- **Type:** names and places in small white serif under photos; black timeline titles typed at 13 characters per second with a full-width red line; quotes in handwriting with an ink bleed (about 2 s), then a red bar reads along.
- **Use when:** news, history, investigations, policy, any argument built on documents and screenshots.
- **Do not use when:** no real material exists (it becomes a fake archive); playful product launches; pure abstract concepts.
- **Common mistakes:** everything moving silky at 60 fps -> camera smooth, elements stepped; template tricks (torn-paper transitions, every image bouncing in, jittering paper grain) -> hard cuts and investigator camera; too many colours -> black, paper white, one red, plus one accent; later cards covering text -> draw readable text last; adjacent cards forming a wrong sentence on the cumulative desk -> read all on-screen text in reading order after each new card.
- **Scoped variant:** a social-short "collage explainer look" (torn paper cards with small drop shadows, halftone, paper and fine film grain, colour fringing at frame edges only, bold condensed sans, motion stepped on twos, no typewriter) is a different, stylized look for 30-60 s vertical shorts. Keep the measured y2 rules for narrated long-form explainers; allow torn-paper cards only in the short-form variant.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: footage (real material is the main picture: archive photos halftoned at 45 degrees with a white border and tape, clippings and screenshots; under 60% of the frame for more than two shots has become a picture-frame layout, entry Look, moves and Do not use when), composite (weak paper shadows on cards and photos, readable text drawn last and on top whatever its object's layer, entry Look and moves), finish (scoped: for long-form, static paper texture with no per-frame grain, yellowed clippings, nothing perfect; the social-short variant keeps its own fine grain and torn cards, entry Look and Scoped variant), rhythm (two tempo tiers, slow cards of 3-7 s and fast still images at 25-33 cuts per minute; elements stepped on twos while the camera stays smooth; highlighter, typing and line drawing locked to speech, not music; the first event within 0.3 s, entry Timing and Rhythm), camera (an investigator's eye: landings computed from what must be read now plus a margin, sine in-out on long moves, never linear; an action seen in the wide never replayed after the push, entry moves and Timing), type (names and places in a small white serif under photos, timeline titles typed at about 13 characters per second with a red line, quotes in handwriting with an ink bleed and a red bar reading along; lifts `lint:T1` and `slop:N9` for the typed titles in the long form only, the short form having no typewriter, entry Type, Timing and Scoped variant); serves when also: data (a place or a route: map fills and route draws over 2.5-3.5 s on twos with a red dot head, entry Transitions), music (music under the voice: a bed that changes about every 20 s, entry Rhythm).

## 4. y3 Whiteboard (big-board and sheet-per-page variants)

- **Choose one sub-grammar, never mix:** one big board with the camera moving about 5.3 times per minute (inspired by RSA Animate), or one sheet per page with hard cuts about 8.1 per minute and a static camera.
- **Look:** white `#FBFBFB`, black line `#0A0503`, one orange (text `#EB701F`, fill `#F3742D`, hatched light orange `#E9A67C`) used on about 22 % of ink pixels, only on keywords and hatching. Sheet-per-page variant: pinkish paper `#E7DBE1`, pencil yellow `#EDE6A2`, light blue `#B5CBE8`. Line width 0.6-0.7 % of frame height (6-7 px at 1080p), hatching about 0.3 %, title cap height about 5.5 %; sheet variant about 0.8 %. One marker, one width (details 0.6-0.8x, emphasis 1.4x).
- **Medium-specific moves:** say a line, write a line; plan the final full image first, then draw toward it; 3-6 groups per block; smooth pans move only about 0.23 of the frame width (about 75 % overlap) so the previous block's edge stays visible; the ending reveals the whole board as a long horizontal scroll. Line types are fixed from the first stroke: solid = happened, dashed = planned, dotted = cross-section leader. Board content is only erased or kept, never faded. Everything is drawn on with the hand; nothing pops in, except some keywords that pop without the hand (0.3 s back-out).
- **Hand:** a real right hand holding a black marker enters from bottom right, forearm diagonal, covering 15-25 % of the frame, and fully exits when not drawing; it is in frame about 40-49 % of the time. The hand must never hide a read; draw first, then let anything else act after the hand leaves.
- **Timing:** body writing median about 16 characters per second, opening title at half speed (about 7.5 per second); speech about 13 characters per second, so writing roughly matches speech. Icons 0.5-1 s, portrait heads about 2 s; new ink median 0.25 % of the frame per second. Complex illustrations are never drawn on camera: pre-draw and reveal them with a camera move, or hard-cut them in and only colour them. Stroke time = length / pen speed, minimum 0.04 s per stroke, 0.02 s pen lift, sine in-out inside each stroke; gaps under 0.25 s the pen glides on an arc lifted about 30 px; longer gaps the hand exits (0.35 s cubic-in to bottom right) and re-enters 0.3 s before the next stroke. Glyphs without stroke-order data are revealed in three zigzag rows with the pen tip leading; figures by a 0.7 s zigzag reveal over the bounding box.
- **Sync:** writing starts 0.2-0.8 s after its phrase begins (median about 0.5 s) and finishes within 1 s after the phrase ends: write slightly behind, never ahead. Visual setups may be pre-drawn. Transitions fall on topics, not pauses. The hand keeps drawing through speech pauses.
- **Transitions:** board pan 1.2-2.4 s sine in-out; whip about 0.8 s over 0.5-1.2 frame widths with motion blur only during the whip; ending: grey wash, 0.6 s fast pull-out, a long pan along the scroll, final full view; cloud or explosion wipe about 1 s; hard cut to a pre-drawn image. Pushing into thought bubbles and page flips do not belong to this grammar.
- **Sound:** the one-big-board variant has no music under speech; the sheet variant keeps music about 9 dB under the voice.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: camera (scoped: one sub-grammar per film, never mixed: one big board with smooth pans of about 0.23 of the frame width and a closing long scroll, or one sheet per page with a static camera and hard cuts, entry Choose and moves), composite (a real right hand holding a black marker enters from the bottom right, covers 15-25% of the frame, never hides a read and fully exits when not drawing, entry Hand), rhythm (say a line, write a line: writing starts 0.2-0.8 s after its phrase begins and ends within 1 s after it, at about the speed of speech; stroke time from length and pen speed, the hand gliding over short gaps and exiting on long ones, entry Timing and Sync), finish (one marker, one width of 0.6-0.7% of frame height, hatching about 0.3%, one orange only on keywords and hatching; the sheet variant about 0.8% on its own paper and colours; board content only erased or kept, never faded, entry Look and moves); serves when also: music (a bed: scoped: none under speech on the one big board, entry Sound).
- **Use when:** causal chains, processes, "why" questions, training and onboarding narration.
- **Do not use when:** precise data charts, complex spatial action, high emotion.
- **Common mistakes:** drawing little and writing a lot (a storyboard problem) -> each block names its object or gag first, a handwritten sentence is at most one line, and if more than half the board text duplicates the VO it is too wordy; repeating one block template ("year, object, orange label") -> at most 3 in a row, alternate comparisons, arrow flows, detail zooms, one big drawing; board full before speaking, or speaking before drawing; full-screen cut to blank each block; many colours, thin uniform lines; hand always on screen; shapes that can read as indecent (round heads, flared sides, round bottoms on tall objects) -> pointed tops and flat bottoms; the pen covering a word at the end frame and inverting the meaning -> check the last frame; spline rectangles bulging into ellipses -> draw rectangles with straight segments; reading time for a list counted from drawing start -> count from the last item finishing.

## 5. t1 Continuous derivation

- **Look:** Inspired by 3Blue1Brown. Only the thing being discussed, on pure black `#000000` (a deep navy base is a myth about this look); colours at about 1.5x saturation; blue `#58C4DD`, red `#FC6255`, pure yellow `#FFFF00`, teal `#5CD0B3`, green `#83C167`, greys `#DDD #BBB #888 #444 #222`. Colour equals concept name: write the colour table first, at most 4 semantic colours, and symbols in formulas share the colour of their objects. Almost no glow and no bloom.
- **Medium-specific moves:** each shot names "who turns into whom" (a grid into circles, pixels into neurons); if there is no transform object, use y5 or y3 instead. The first frame is the object (axes, grid, curve); titles and formulas come later. At most two points on screen at once; a third requires earlier ones to become a picture or leave. Hold still during waits (no slow push, no breathing, no floating). The question is written before the demonstration; values are counted with fills, never before the picture.
- **Geometry:** stroke about 4 px, axes and networks 2, grid main 2 and subdivision 1 at 0.25 opacity; frame height = 8 units, 1 unit = 135 px; neurons white 2 px stroke with fill opacity = activation, radius 0.15 units, at most 16 drawn per layer then an ellipsis and a brace; edge width proportional to (|w|/max)^3; formulas in Computer Modern style serif; title and formula fixed in the top eighth of the frame, not moving with the camera.
- **Timing:** smoothstep 6t^5 - 15t^4 + 10t^3 for almost everything; linear only for writing, constant rotation and a passing flash; there-and-back for indicate; rush-into and rush-from for crash in and out. Default run time 1 s and wait 1 s (most common in practice: 2 s, then 3 s, then 1 s, then 4 s). Lagged starts with lag ratio 0.05-0.1. Writing: linear, under 15 sub-objects 1 s, otherwise 2 s, outline then fill (fill fades in over the last 60 %).
- **Transitions:** transform (resample both shapes to equal point counts with aligned start points, interpolate points and colour); fade in or out with a 24-40 px shift; transform continuation (last frame of a shot = first frame of the next); zoom through an object's circle; matching-term formula transforms (about 2 s); 3D orbit 0.5-3 degrees per second only for spatial relations.
- **VO sync:** lay out the animation first, then record the voice; one sentence = one animation; the animation begins on the action verb and waits about 1 s after the sentence. Every movement on screen should be deliberate with an identifiable purpose. Chinese or Arabic on-screen text: at most one title line plus one note line.
- **Use when:** mathematics, algorithms, machine learning, physics derivations, any "this becomes that" mechanism.
- **Do not use when:** product launches, emotional stories, real data trends, quick-cut pieces under 8 s.
- **Common mistakes:** bloom and navy gradients (reads as tech slides) -> pure black, no glow; linear or generic easing with many fast animations -> smoothstep and 1 s waits; colour coding changed midway or too many colours -> fix the colour table up front.
- **Craft profile:** family default; needs also: voice (scoped: the animation is laid out first and the voice recorded to it; one sentence is one animation, starting on the action verb and waiting about 1 s after the sentence, entry VO sync), rhythm (smoothstep for almost everything, a default run of 1 s and a wait of 1 s, lagged starts, at most two points on screen at once; hold still during waits, entry moves and Timing), camera (scoped: still during waits, never a slow push, breathing or floating; a zoom through an object's circle for a change, entry moves and Transitions), type (titles and formulas fixed in the top eighth, written linearly outline then fill, symbols sharing the colour of their objects; the question written before the demonstration, entry Look, moves, Geometry and Timing), transition (a transform of the previous concept into the next, a transform continuation or a zoom through an object's circle; scoped: a fade in or out with a 24-40 px shift only when the next concept has no shape the previous one can be resampled into, one object leaving or entering while the rest stays put, never two texts or cards blended across a scene change; lifts `slop:N24` for that fade-shift, entry Transitions); serves when also: data (a value in the speech: scoped: counted with fills, never before the picture; real data trends belong to t3, entry moves and Do not use when), depth (a spatial relation: a 3D orbit of 0.5-3 degrees per second, entry Transitions), light (an indicate: a passing flash only; scoped: pure black with almost no glow and no bloom, entry Look, Timing and Common mistakes).

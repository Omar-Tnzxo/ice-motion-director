Transition selection rule of thumb: at shot boundaries, prefer the **six motion-carry moves** (one card each, sharing
a lead/tail overlap of 12~16 frames + the momentum-handoff discipline of same direction on both sides, code is likewise `template/motion-systems/transitions.tsx`):
use push-through for progression, overexpose-flip for page turns, whip-pan for parallel items, black-slam for the biggest reversal (once per film at most),
pullback-cool for wrapping up, particle-weld for a change of form; **one move per boundary, never use all six in one film** (a 100s film has 5~7 boundaries,
reusing the same move gives more stylistic coherence than collecting them all). shape-wipe is the lightweight substitute when there is no camera system; color-slam-beat-card is the zero-tween hard-cut beat version;
for spatial-narrative passages use long-take-world as one continuous shot for the whole passage. **Graphic relay** is the third family (since 2026-09-05): `line-carry-transition◎` lets a line in shot A travel by itself into shot B to become a container:
the six camera moves hand off momentum, this one hands off a **graphic**; use it only once per film as the signature transition slot, and the information on the A side must be finished before the lateral move.

Below, the seven categories list **one line per card**: `slug` batch mark, name · P level · energy tier : one sentence (including how it differs from sibling cards).
Parameters / the critical point / known pitfalls / placement self-check are all in `references/cards/<slug>.md` (**the single source of truth**, not repeated here), the implementation is in `template/cards/<slug>.tsx`.
Two library-wide conventions (set after the 2026-09-22 audit): **energy has only three tiers: low / mid / high** (criteria: low = only fade-in / unblur / ≤1.02 scale / small displacement; mid = clear displacement, back.out overshoot or ≥1.05 scale; high = slam / rapid push / whole-screen change / shake);
the tsx `meta.durationInFrames` = in-card animation length + a 0.4s tail pad, the md "duration" is written as the animation length, so a 0.4s difference between them is not an error. Six exceptions (meta differs from animation length by more): behind-text-title (hold is a looping idle, meta 3.17s is just a cutoff), focus-dim-spotlight (meta 9.17s is 1.2s longer than the timetable), media-pop-in (idles after landing until meta ends, no exit segment), magnifier-detail (infinite scanning, meta 3.37s is a cutoff), typewriter-reveal (tail pad about 2s), info-term-card (after leaving frame at 3.96s, meta runs on to 6.57s with an empty stage).

## Captions & kinetic type

> Plain bottom captions iron rule (source of truth design-language.md §5): read-along captions get **no motion at all**, the whole sentence appears hard; the only exception is keyword-pop-highlight, at most 3 times per film.
> All other cards in this category act on **independent text layers such as titles / punchlines / key points**, not bottom captions.

### Interface theater subtype (a whole interface plays itself from a script)

The sibling cards ui-flow-theater◇ / terminal-typing-log◇ / chat-message-flow◇ / chat-gpt◇ / claude-code◇ / glass-code-walk◇ / cursor-actor-demo◆ share the discipline below.

When a product / tool talking-head video explains "how to use this thing" or "what the AI is doing now", the three common routes all have hard flaws: screen recording (a 12px cursor is invisible,
the pace is set by hand speed, and once you need to redact it is ruined), hard-cut screenshots (lose the causality of "this step led to that step"), animating each control separately
(one timeline per control, by the third one it falls off the beat). These cards are the fourth route: **treat the whole interface as one actor and give it a script**
: the interface plays itself along a scripted timeline, the audience sees a causal chain of "this action caused that change", and the maker maintains only one table.
The difference from the other cards in this category is the tense of the asset: those put evidence that has already happened **onto the stage** (past tense), this subtype has the interface **perform on the spot** (present progressive);
the difference from "Camera moves" is what moves: in camera moves only the camera moves and the interface itself does not change a single pixel, in this subtype the camera stays still and the interface changes state by itself.

Shared discipline (common to this subtype):
- **Time enters from one place only**: one timetable (`STEPS` / `FRAGMENTS` / round count) is the single time source,
  and the cursor, controls and results are all functions of it. Alignment relies on shared constants, not on counting by hand in two places: this is the whole reason it can run past three beats without falling apart.
- **State changes are hard cuts, not animations**: an interface state change lands in one frame (`transition: none`) or very briefly (one 0.27s tier shared by all cards).
  Give state changes a transition and it becomes "styles animating", and the audience cannot read which action triggered it.
- **The timetable accumulates automatically from content length**, so changing copy does not require re-timing; each action's duration is a **feel constant** (it does not scale with speech rate:
  when the speech is fast, cut the number of beats instead of speeding up each beat).
- **Neutral grayscale skin + a single semantic color**: all controls are grayscale wireframes, the semantic color is reserved only for the closing beat (success toast / the brand-red line).
  Make it in a real product's color scheme and the audience starts reading the platform's brand, and you take the blame for the imitation.

## Camera moves

A static asset (web screenshot / image / document / UI interface) dies the moment it is pasted on screen: the audience has no reason to keep their eyes on it.
**Web evidence goes one step further: static pasting is forbidden, it must be "shot"**: the selection table for the four shooting methods scroll / tour / zoom / swipe is in
shot-design.md §2④ "Shooting web pages", and the capture spec (full-page 2× long capture + DOM coordinate JSON) is in broll-sources.md "Capturing web page footage".
The first seven cards do not change a single pixel of the asset, only the **camera** moves (the last one, pip-zoom-box◈, is the exception, see below): the asset fills the frame inside a "camera layer", and the camera layer is the only element that gets transformed.
Selection: **use slow-push-in by default** (the baseline-noise camera move for any static asset); when the asset is information-dense and its scale needs revealing, use slow-pull-reveal;
to present the page as a "product / work" use tilt-3d-page; when the asset is much wider than the frame use sway-parallax;
when the page must stay on screen for a long time (long narration) use orbit-drift; when the asset exceeds the frame and the "camera should stop wherever the narration is" use stage-keyframe-tour◇;
when the target itself is moving (cursor / progress dot / a field being filled) use cursor-locked-zoom◇;
to show one detail for a long time while keeping the full view, use pip-zoom-box◈ (the only one that does not move the whole frame).
**One move per asset segment**: pushing while panning reads as a shaky hand.
The two 2D moves (push / pull) and the three 3D moves can be chained: tilt-3d-page establishes the elevation pose → orbit-drift takes over the baseline noise during the hold is the recommended combo.
The first five are all **single-segment motion + hold** (one pose target per segment), the last two ◇ are **multi-target addressing**: one walks a preset path of points of interest,
one locks onto a moving target whose position is only known at runtime: this is the dividing line between "single-segment camera moves" and "path / follow shots".

Shared discipline (common to the first five, and the biggest difference from the other categories):

- **The camera is never still**: during the hold it must keep going at the end velocity of the main segment. The ease of the first five cards is a custom curve with "non-zero end velocity"
  `p + (1−r)·p²·(1−p)` (start velocity = average velocity, end velocity = r×average velocity), **you cannot use `power2.out`**: its end velocity is zero, so the camera dies on the frame it arrives.
  There are two exceptions: the stand-up segment of tilt-3d-page is a "placing" action (an event with a start and an end), so it may come to rest;
  **stage-keyframe-tour◇ and cursor-locked-zoom◇ are reversed entirely**: their hold must be **truly still**
  (the tour's information is in the hold, and the follow shot must leave time to finish reading), stillness in those two cards is a function, not a mistake.
- **Duration follows the narration, the amount of motion does not**: `zoomTo` / `ampRot` are perceptual constants (they do not change when the duration changes), duration is the rhythm variable.
- **Readability is a hard ceiling**: 3D tilt ≤25° (beyond that the far-side body text is unreadable), pan ≤190px/s@960 width (beyond that the audience's skim-reading cannot keep up),
  push ≤1.2 (beyond that the bitmap goes soft). The perspective value for 3D cards is 900~1200px: too small reads as fisheye distortion, not space.
- **The shadow of the three 3D moves must follow the pose**: it lives in the same `preserve-3d` space as the page (automatically flattened by perspective) + its density deepens with tilt
  + its position shifts to the side opposite the tilt. A shadow that does not move = the page reads as a sticker rather than a solid object floating in space.
- Asset resolution ≥ frame × max zoom (pushing a 1080p frame to 1.15 needs a screenshot ≥2210px wide), otherwise the camera move turns it blurry.

- **slow-push-in** Slow push-in close-up · P0 · low : the default baseline-noise camera move for static assets: the camera pushes very slowly and evenly toward the point of interest, and during the hold it keeps pushing at the end velocity without dying, the push half of Ken Burns.
- **slow-pull-reveal** Slow pull-back reveal · P0 · low : pulls back evenly from a local detail to the asset at its original full frame to reveal scale, semantically the opposite of slow-push-in, one segment can only pick one direction.
- **tilt-3d-page** 3D elevation display · P1 · mid : rests head-on, then tilts into a 3D elevation, turning the page from "information" into an "object", used to showcase a work / product or to demote the page into a background prop.
- **sway-parallax** Side-to-side pan · P1 · low : when the asset is much wider than the frame, the camera sweeps horizontally and evenly across the long page with a very slight rotateY following along; tall vertical pages belong to evidence-scroll-tour.
- **orbit-drift** Orbiting micro-drift · P1 · low : an orbiting micro-drift where two axes of sine waves with offset phases combine into a closed elliptical path, the premium version of "camera breathing", the only camera move in this category that can last indefinitely, used when the page must stay up for a long time.
- **stage-keyframe-tour** ◇ Long-page point-of-interest tour · P1 · mid : a long page larger than the frame lies on a 3D stage, the camera follows a multi-keyframe path, stopping at preset points of interest in turn before pulling back to the full view; the camera moves and the page does not, the counterpart of evidence-scroll-tour where the page scrolls itself.
- **cursor-locked-zoom** ◇ Cursor-locked follow shot · P1 · mid : the only solution for reading out a long command on a small screen: the camera locks onto the cursor and follows it, driven by typing progress, the target position is only known at runtime, and it pulls back to the full view at the end.
- **pip-zoom-box** ◈ Picture-in-picture zoom · P1 · mid : a viewfinder frame flies to the side with the picture inside it and settles as a picture-in-picture, the only card in this category that keeps the full view; use magnifier-detail for a quick look and retreat, use this card to keep it hanging for a long time.

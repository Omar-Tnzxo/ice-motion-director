# Script writing for narrated explainers

Spine stage 03, script step (sections 2, 3 and 9 also feed the angle at stage 02). Output: `ice/plan/script.md` (display script, what the screen and captions may show) and a pronunciation script (what the voice is fed). Paragraphs are picture units: one blank-line paragraph usually becomes one segment.

## 1. Source modes

| Input | Rule |
|---|---|
| Topic only | Research first (`references/research-and-facts.md`), then write from the situation the audience is in |
| Article or document | The document is raw ore, not an outline. Never film it section by section or paraphrase it in order. Extract: on-ramp (a familiar moment), tension, turn, mechanism, receipts (proof). One storyline per film; everything else demotes to receipts or is cut. The narration never refers to "the document" or "this article" |
| Pull request or code | See `references/grammar-selection.md` (section "Code, pull request, product UI and website explainers") |
| Product | One promise the film proves, told from the user's side. A row of features is a spec sheet, not a story |

Beats the source cannot fill honestly are cut, not faked.

## 2. The spine

- One spine, the shuffle test, motivation before mechanism, one thesis sentence per chapter, every sentence drawable: the `ice-ideas` skill, `references/script-writing.md` sections 3 and 4.
- Each chapter hands off to the next (section 7).
- One running example carried through the whole film, used by every segment.
- For each drawable sentence, note the visual relation it expresses from the shot pattern table in `references/card-library.md`.
- Value before evidence: the value claim lands by the second beat; evidence follows. Self-check by deleting the evidence beats (does the point survive?) and the value beats (is it still a film?).

## 3. Openings

- Hook rules (open inside a concrete situation, the first sentence carries the hook) and hook timing by film type: the `ice-ideas` skill, `references/hooks-and-openers.md` sections 2 and 3. Also never open on a company description or a greeting.
- Vary the hook type across films (illustration only: a number, a conflict, a question, a counterintuitive claim, a stake, a scenario).
- Short social explainers: first surprise within 5 s.
- The main idea is clear by the second scene.

## 4. Sentence rules

Sentence rules (one information point, the word cap, plain words first, varied rhythm, no restatement, lists, division of labor, voice): the `ice-ideas` skill, `references/script-writing.md` section 4. Ear rules (words per sentence, who does what): the `ice-audio` skill, `references/voiceover.md` section 2.

Explainer additions:
- CJK scripts: about 35 characters per sentence at most.
- A list read aloud works only when each item appears on screen.

## 5. Numbers turned into felt size

- Felt scale for numbers: the `ice-ideas` skill, `references/script-writing.md` section 4. Spoken versus shown figures and the rounding direction: the `ice-assets` skill, `references/truth-sheet.md` section 3.
- Numbers belong in the script only when they carry stakes, not inventory.
- Plan a short pause after a key number in the script itself, so the number can settle on screen as it is spoken.
- A section with only one number gets a big settling number, not a chart.

## 6. Analogies and examples

- One analogy, only when it enables a true inference, carried through; every abstract noun gets a concrete instance: the `ice-ideas` skill, `references/script-writing.md` section 4.
- Never one analogy per section by template.
- Write down where each analogy misleads; avoid leaning on that part.
- A real example (illustration only: a command that runs, a number with a source, the real screen) beats three boxes of general words.

## 7. Chapters and seams

- Chapter counts by length, chapter seams (end on a question or gap, open by naming what was achieved) and chapter names as navigation labels: the `ice-ideas` skill, `references/script-writing.md` section 3. No cold starts.
- A pivot sentence (a turn such as "but this time it is different", illustration only) belongs to the next segment as its first beat.
- HUD names follow the same label rules as chapter names; in CJK scripts at most about 6 characters; no metaphors.
- Show chapter names and the seam sentences side by side at script approval.

## 8. Endings

- Ending rules (return to the opening and reframe it, never a summary list or a farewell): the `ice-ideas` skill, `references/hooks-and-openers.md` section 7. A plain closing sentence that states the main point: the `ice-audio` skill, `references/voiceover.md` section 2.
- Collage-style shorts: the thesis is at most two sentences with no metaphors; the close mirrors the open and ends on facts the viewer did not have; the on-screen takeaway is five words or fewer.
- A call to action only when the brief has one (section 5 of `references/research-and-facts.md`).
- The last frame shows the main idea clearly; spoken thesis may sit in the decay of the last music hit before the logo.

## 9. Structures

Pick by topic and log the reason. Never reuse the previous film's structure without a reason (`ice-direction` fingerprint).

| Structure | Shape | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Hook, intuition, formalize, reveal, extend | Math, science, "why" questions |
| Problem and solution | Problem, why it hurts, the fix, how it works, what changes | Tools, methods, products |
| Comparison | Two options, one axis at a time, verdict with limits | Choices, trade-offs |
| Build-up | Simple case, add one complication per step | Systems, algorithms |
| How-to | 3 to 6 steps on one consistent stage | Processes, tutorials |
| Listicle | Items with one strongest; rule of three is strongest | Tips, causes |
| Story explainer | A person and a date, what happened, the mechanism underneath | History, events |
| Documentary | Thesis, evidence, counterpoint, conclusion | Arguments, news |
| Journalistic bones | Hook image plus claim, explicit question on screen, context (map or place), a big number then comparison, a 2 to 3 date timeline, a self-drawing chart, impact on the viewer, close echoing the hook with sources | News and current topics |
| Familiar to hidden | Familiar habit, dig (person and date), story, the artifact, mechanism, second wind, thesis, close mirroring open. Weights for 60 s: 15, 5, 15, 10, 20, 15, 10, 10 percent | Collage explainers of everyday things |
| Discovery story (retros, reviews) | Context, first fix, workaround, turning point, answer, decisions | Team or project explainers |

Emotional arc across any structure: negative valley, pivot, build, resolution. Tempo shape for teaching films: the `ice-motion` skill, `references/anti-slideshow-and-density.md` section 3.

## 10. Length budgets

Length and language must be known before writing; the word budget formula, the 15 percent rule and the sentence and chapter counts per length: the `ice-ideas` skill, `references/script-writing.md` sections 1 to 3. Pace (words per second for the language, voice and film type), film word totals and the speed-up cap: the `ice-audio` skill, `references/voiceover.md` sections 3 and 5. Latin word counts always come from that pace, never from a fixed per-minute number.

Explainer additions (not held by the owners):

| Length | CJK characters | Sentences per scene |
|---|---|---|
| 45 to 120 s (default range when unset) | about 200 (Chinese) or 270 (Japanese) per minute | 1 to 3 |
| 2 to 3 min | 650 to 880 | |
| 3 to 5 min | 1100 to 1400 | |
| 5 to 8 min | 1650 to 2200 | |

- Vertical concept shorts: seconds per scene = words divided by the `ice-audio` pace in words per second.
- Write to the engine you will use: measured rates differ by engine and voice (the `ice-audio` skill, `references/voiceover.md` section 3).

## 11. Writing for the voice

- The two-layer script (display side and spoken side), initialisms, domains, homophones, product names, numbers written as said: the `ice-audio` skill, `references/voiceover.md` section 2. A human recording needs a script that matches what was said: same file, section 1.
- Keep display and pronunciation in sync with a pair notation per term, written as shown text plus spoken text.
- Numbers exception: when the aligner or speech check matches digits better than spelled numbers in that language, follow the aligner (record which in `ice/DIRECTION.md`).
- Arabic: dialect, numerals and pronunciation rules come from `ice-arabic`.

## 12. Banned tells

Copy tells, cut on sight in any language: the `ice-qa` skill, `references/slop-scan.md` section 6. Explainer additions:
- Metaphors in the thesis.
- Callbacks to "the memo" or "the source" (the narration never refers to its own source material).

## 13. Script approval

Why approval comes here: the `ice-ideas` skill, `references/script-writing.md` section 1. The approval is not an extra stop: in step mode, show the user on the 03 review card the script, chapter names and seams side by side, the planned length, and the voice plan; in auto mode the approver checks the same items against the 03 row before the voice step. Record the approval in `ice/state.json` (`approvals`, by user or approver role).

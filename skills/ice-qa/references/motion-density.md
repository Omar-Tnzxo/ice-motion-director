# Motion density, dead air and holds

How much the picture moves, where it stops, and when a stop is a defect. Sources disagree on purpose, so every threshold below is scoped by film type (type names from the architecture: product launch, app ad, brand film, logo sting, kinetic type, data story, social short, hype promo, UI walkthrough, music video, art film, industry spot; explainer; edit types long-form, podcast, talking-head ad, shorts and reels, B-roll overlay, showreel). Motion grammar and how to fix a hold are owned by the ice-motion skill. Sources: motionmaxxing (Apache), huashu-art-motion (MIT), motion-video-kit (MIT), anidoodle, reelmimic (MIT), herk-edit-kit (MIT), claude-code-video-toolkit (MIT), explainroo, hyperframes and hyperframes-community-skills (Apache), claude-motion-design (MIT), claude-remotion-skill (MIT), founder skills; PolyForm explainer sources (ideas rewritten).

## 2. Rules that hold for every type

- Still-type metrics (still share, longest still, longest gap without an event) are never gates on their own: they flag good "hold, burst, hold" films. Use them to decide where to look (huashu).
- Never add floating, drifting, breathing or idle loops to a static object just to pass a motion metric; if there is no verb action, let it be still, or add story, or move the camera with intent. A slow uniform push lowers the static number but still reads as a slideshow; the right fix for a static opening is an earlier first event (huashu, hyperframes motion doctrine, PolyForm ideas).
- A creeping camera changes every pixel and satisfies the tool while the eye sees a still: check by eye too (anidoodle).
- A frozen final second (bit-identical consecutive frames) is the cheap-motion tell you can test for (hyperframes docs).
- Distinguish true stillness from tiny motion: at full resolution, under about 800 changed pixels is true stillness (add a verb action); 800-2500 is tiny motion (enlarge the motion, do not add elements) (PolyForm ideas, rewritten).
- Small thin-line motifs barely register in small frame-difference analyses; visible motion needs locally large moving areas (huashu).
- Skip the first 0.35 s after a transition when measuring in-shot motion (a punch zoom makes the whole frame move) (huashu).
- Grain can cause false motion positives; dark grounds can make a rest metric over-read small detail; a narrated film is never quiet, so an "audio quiet" leg fails structurally (founder skills, PolyForm ideas).
- Motion density compared against a reference: median and p90 frame difference at the same sample rate, plus a count of low-change intervals (founder skills; climax and reference work in `climax-duel.md`).
- 0.5-8% motion area mostly looks good; more than 40% static frame pairs reads as stuck, except in explainer, story and text grammars where holds are correct (huashu qa).
- The motion note band (mean luma change 0-255) is a question, never a gate; band and questions: the `ice-motion` skill, `references/anti-slideshow-and-density.md` section 4 (Human motion band).

Conflict scoped: "every 100 ms has an animating element" and "no dead air over 1 s" (herk legacy short-form rules) hold for shorts and reels only; "holds exist: at least 3 moments of stillness" (claude-remotion-skill) holds for product and brand films with music; "elements hold dead still after landing; life in holds comes only from the camera" (PolyForm ideas) holds for explainers built on a moving camera; "I'd rather have no motion than bad motion; no lazy breathing, no slow pan in the back half" (hyperframes product-launch-video, founder skills) holds for product launch and UI walkthrough. Variety: something new every 2-4 s in launch and social films (claude-motion-design, founder skills).

`look.py` reads the film type itself (`--type`, else `ice/state.json` `subtype`, else the `film_type` row of `ice/BRIEF.md`) and takes the limit from section 6; it prints the limit and where it came from. `--hold-warn <limit>` overrides it (`0` turns the end-hold check off for loops). When the end card carries the facts a viewer needs to act (the storyboard tags its rows `CTA block`, or the cue sheet declares the CTA start as its own event), the limit becomes the reading time of the CTA block text (the `ice-type` skill, `references/reading-time.md` section 1, display text with no voice: letters / 15 + 1.5 s), and `look.py` prints the reading time it used.

The CTA share is measured from the CTA start when one is declared, else from the last cut. Declare it as an event in `ice/plan/cue-sheet.json` whose `kind` is `cta` (the `ice-film` skill, `references/film-types/industry-spot.md`), or in the events file as `{"type": "show", "t": <seconds>, "label": "cta"}` (the renderer's `window.__events`, or `<film>.events.json` next to the render). A film that reaches its CTA with a push-in or a morph instead of a cut needs this, or the whole last shot counts as the CTA. Intended holds (a finished first frame held 0.7 s, a rest the motion plan declares) go in the `quiet` list of the cue sheet (start, end, reason; the `ice-audio` skill, `references/cue-map-and-beat-sync.md` section 2) or in the events file as `{"type": "hold", "t": <start>, "end": <end>}`; `motion_check.py` reports them apart from freezes, and `film_gate.py` leaves them out of `in_shot_motion`, `longest_still` and `empty_half`.

## 5. Calibrated numbers and their limits

- `film_gate.py` fast-motion gate: calibrated on 13 author-judged 1-2 min films (approved minimum 0.068, rejected maximum 0.021); read it as "no counterexample in these 13". Clips under 20 s are downgraded to yellow except the factual gates; run it on the assembled film. Push-in counting is a lower bound (live footage camera moves are not estimable); a slow 20% push over about 1 s scores like a 0.28 s fast push, so never speed moves up to pass (huashu).
- Push-ins: `film_gate.py` reports push-ins per minute; yellow at the warning rate in the owner's density limits: the `ice-motion` skill, `references/camera.md` section 7. Same move in every shot: the `ice-motion` skill, `references/holds-and-stillness.md` section 2.
- Measured progress example: frozen time went from about 10 s of 36 s to 0.7 s of 28.6 s over four critic rounds; none of the builder's own checks had flagged the first (motion-video-kit).
- A 7-window "wow" gate for short ads: split a 20 s film into 7 windows of about 2.9 s; each must be a YES; PARTIAL fails (motion-video-kit case study).
- Minimum visual beat: the `ice-motion` skill, `references/holds-and-stillness.md` section 2.

### 6.1 Richness floor: read from the owners, never copied

The measures are defined in the `ice-motion` skill, `references/composition-and-staging.md` section 7.2; the numbers per type are the "Richness floor" table of each `ice-film` type file (`references/film-types/<type>.md`); explainers use the defaults of `composition-and-staging.md` section 7.3. `film_gate.py` and `lint.mjs` read those files for the film type at run time and print each number with the file it came from. A prose cell is read clause by clause (split on `;`): a clause that names the other orientation is skipped (`16:9`, square or landscape against vertical), notes in parentheses are ignored, and when a cell gives two numbers (energetic and calm spots, feature and everyday shots) the lenient one is the red line and the strict one the yellow line. A cell with no number (for example "only the end card") is printed as "judge by eye". Edit types have no richness floor; they have the coverage floor of section 6.2. An unknown film type has no floor; the numbers are reported only.

How `film_gate.py` measures them on the render (4 fps for areas on the whole frame, 12 fps for motion with the subtitle band masked):
- Windows: the film is split at cuts, `show` events and the CTA start, and every part longer than 4 s into 4 s windows; each window is read in its settled second half; windows under 1 s are exempt (burst frames, transitions).
- `hero_share`: the bounding box of the largest connected region of visible detail (8 px cells at 320 px wide with edge strength 14 or more, closed, filled, and opened so a thin line such as a rope or a page edge does not join the box), as a percent of the frame. The gate reads the median over windows; windows under the floor are listed.
- `frame_fill`: a 4 x 4 grid; a cell carries content when at least 10 percent of its 8 px cells have detail or sit outside the large flat ground. Median over windows; the CTA window may go 4 cells below (`composition-and-staging.md` section 7.2).
- `empty_half`: seconds in which every cell of one half (top or bottom when vertical, left or right otherwise) is empty, outside declared holds.
- `in_shot_motion`: share of in-shot frame pairs (cuts and crossfades left out, declared holds left out) where at least 3 cells of 8 px still change by 2/255 or more after the camera move is compensated, so camera-only movement does not count.
- Page flips: a cut with nothing moving in the 0.5 s on either side swaps one still page for another. `film_gate.py` reports it as `page_flips`, never as a hard cut; it is not a visual event, and a still run continues across it.
- `longest_still`: the longest run of in-shot pairs without such a change, outside declared holds. When the type's cell says the CTA hold is exempt, the CTA window is left out.
- Declared holds: the `quiet` list of `ice/plan/cue-sheet.json` and `hold` events in the events file; the CTA start: a cue-sheet event whose kind is `cta` (or whose text names the CTA) or a `show` event labelled `cta`.
- `depth_layers` and `hero_width` are read only from an HTML composition that marks them (`lint.mjs`: elements with `data-ice-layer="<name>"` (`data-layer` is also read outside HyperFrames), or `window.__layers(t)` returning the layer names drawn at time t; `data-hero` on the hero element). A page that marks none fails when the film type's floor names `depth_layers` (unmeasured is never `n/a`); a canvas film exposes `window.__layers(t)`. A type with no `depth_layers` floor prints `n/a`. `surface_detail`, `light_events` and `real_material` are judged by the critic (`critic-loop.md`), not by a script.
- Calibration (2026-10-19), measured against the owners' floors: the pilot films the founder judged sparse read red (a 16:9 launch on a book page: `hero_share` 21, `in_shot_motion` 82; a vertical app ad with a small basket on a paper field: `hero_share` 13, `frame_fill` 6 of 16, `empty_half` 0.75 s); the fuller real-estate pilot passed with one weak beat (`hero_share` 61, one window at 32); two full reference films passed (`hero_share` 76 to 80, `in_shot_motion` 98 to 100). Limits: a textured or shaded ground counts as filled for `frame_fill` (it is a surface by the owner's definition), so a sparse film on paper can pass `frame_fill` and fail `hero_share`; a thin hero such as a line drawing reads small. Treat a red as "go and fix the frame", and a reading that contradicts the stills as a script limit to report in `NOTE.md`.

### 6.2 Coverage floor for edit types (lesson L17, blocking)

Scope: every edit type of the `ice-edit` skill (long-form, podcast, talking-head ad, shorts and reels, news reel, B-roll overlay, showreel). Trial: edits were delivered with a speaker talking and nothing else happening for long stretches (one plan held 19.75 s without an event, another leaned on one locked camera for 30 s), and no gate counted it because edit types had no floor.

Why the film floor of section 6.1 is not copied onto edits: its measures read designed frames, and on speaker footage a lit person in a lit room already fills `hero_share` and `frame_fill`, so a bare speaker passes them. An edit is measured by what happens over the speaker instead.

| Measure | Kind | Definition (read from the edit plan `assets/plan.json`, schema: the `ice-edit` skill, `references/clock-and-grouping.md` section 3) |
|---|---|---|
| `element_events_per_min` | floor | Visual events per minute of runtime, holds excluded: every entry of `events` whose `type` (or `kind`) is not `hold`, plus every scene cut (a scene start after 0), counted once when an event and a cut fall in the same frame |
| `longest_bare_talk_s` | ceiling | The longest stretch inside a speaker scene (layout `face`) with no element, camera or caption-emphasis event: each stretch is bounded by the scene's start and end and by every event inside it. A scene with B-roll beside the speaker (`split`, `broll-split`) is not bare |
| `trigger_coverage` | floor | Percent of the plan's content triggers (`triggers`: the kinds of content listed in the `ice-direction` skill, `references/treatment.md` section "Craft plan", found in the transcript with their times) answered by a visual event within 1.5 s of the trigger, or declined with a recorded reason (`notUsed`, the `not_use` reason of the plan). A banned reason (the list in the same section) counts as no reason |
| `broll_share` | floor | Percent of runtime in scenes that show material other than the speaker: B-roll, archive or screen material (layouts `broll`, `broll-split`, `split`, or a scene with `cut: "archive"`) |

- **Numbers.** Each edit type file holds its own "Coverage floor" table (the `ice-edit` skill, `references/edit-types/<type>.md`): the measure as the first cell, the number in the second, where the number comes from in the third. Cells are read like the richness cells of section 6.1: clauses split on `;`, notes in parentheses ignored; two numbers give a lenient line (the script fails past it) and a strict line (the script warns). When the cell names the case of each number (a calm or a normal ad, a light or a medium density), a warning in the strict case is fixed before 07 like a fail. `n/a` means the measure does not apply to that type. `trigger_coverage` is 100 percent in every type: a trigger is answered or declined with its reason, never skipped.
- **With a reference.** When the plan carries `reference` (lesson L9, section 7), the reference's own values replace the type's first two rows: `element_events_per_min` at its events per minute x 0.90, `longest_bare_talk_s` at its longest gap x 1.10 plus one frame. A reference's measured B-roll or presenter share sets `broll_share` through an exemption row (below) whose source is the reference.
- **Never filler.** The floor is met by real elements on real triggers and by the camera row of the craft plan, never by floating, pulsing or idle loops added to pass a number (section 2).
- **Who checks it.** The plan check is the `ice-edit` skill's `scripts/validate-plan.mjs` at 04 and 06 (the type comes from the plan's `type`, else the craft plan's `type`; a type whose file has no table is reported, not failed); it prints every measure with its floor and the file the floor came from. The render-side twin is `film_gate.py` gate 4 at 07 and 09.
- **Cleared only by** changed material re-checked (an element on the trigger, a camera move or a graphic inside the bare stretch, B-roll on the named reality), or by an exemption row of the craft plan that lifts `plan:coverage` with its source (the row format and the pass rule: the `ice-direction` skill, `references/treatment.md` section "Craft plan"); the validator prints `exempt plan:coverage by <row id> (<source kind>: <source ref>)`.

### 6.3 Rhythm row without a reference (blocking)

Scope: every film and edit type, when no reference is followed; with a reference, its own measured numbers win (section 7). Trial: pace was checked on the plan and against a reference only, so a video made without a reference had no pace gate on the render, and the house numbers (a shorts average shot of 1.1-1.4 s, none over 2.8 s, a first cut at 0.6-1.0 s) were never read by a script.

- **Row.** Each type file holds one line in its rhythm section: `Rhythm row: mean_shot_s <range or ->; max_shot_s <ceiling or ->; first_cut_s <range or ->; max_event_gap_s <ceiling or ->; holds excuse|never.` (the `ice-film` skill, `references/film-types/<type>.md`, "Rhythm ranges"; the `ice-edit` skill, `references/edit-types/<type>.md`, its rhythm section). Values are seconds: `<a>-<b>` is a range, one number a ceiling, `-` not gated. Every number comes from the file's own rhythm section or structure options, or from the `ice-edit` skill's `references/house-style-numbers.md`; when the options of a type disagree, the most lenient number that holds for all of them is the row's, and a field no number holds for every option stays `-`. The line after the row says where each number comes from. `holds excuse` leaves a declared hold's span out of an event gap (the type's rhythm lets a hold with its reason stand); `holds never` counts it (a presenter hold is carried by inner events: section 3, row "News reel").
- **Measured.** `film_gate.py` gate 5 on the render, at 07 and 09. Shots split at the detected cuts and fades, never the declared ones; a span under 3 analysis frames joins the one before. `mean_shot_s` is the mean shot, `max_shot_s` the longest, `first_cut_s` the first detected cut, `max_event_gap_s` the longest gap between events: detected cuts other than page flips, fades, fast-motion run starts, and the events file's declared visual events, merged within 0.3 s. The CTA or end card from its declared start on is left out of the longest shot and the gaps; its hold is gate G3 (section 4).
- **Declared events must be visible.** At 07 and 09, when the render has an events file, run `motion_check.py --pacing-only --events <render>.events.json --measure <render measure>` (section 7, "On the render"): an event listed but not seen fails there, so the gap here counts only events the render shows.
- **Gate.** Red outside the row by more than one analysis frame (1/12 s), and red for a `first_cut_s` range when no cut is detected; yellow inside the row within 10 percent of a range's width from an edge, or above 90 percent of a ceiling. A red blocks delivery like the richness floor.
- **Cleared only by** changed material re-checked, or an exemption row of the craft plan lifting `film:mean_shot_s`, `film:max_shot_s`, `film:first_cut_s` or `film:max_event_gap_s` whose source kind is user, reference or style (a content row is ignored: restraint the agent chose is not a source; row format and pass rule: the `ice-direction` skill, `references/treatment.md` section "Craft plan"). It prints `exempt film:<key> by <row id> (<source kind>: <source ref>)`.
- **With a reference.** When the craft plan names a reference, or a reference analysis holds `events.json`, the row is reported, not gated.

## 7. Pacing against the reference (lesson L9, blocking)

Scope: general, any video made with a reference (a remake, an edit that follows a reference, a style recipe rebuilt on new material), whatever its film type. Trial: a news reel was delivered with 19.75 s without a single event (2:13 to 2:43) and a slower rhythm than its reference, and the warning was read as a note. The rule: the longest gap and the event density are measured on the reference, and the film is not delivered while it exceeds them. Second trial (0.2.6): the same reel cut at a mean shot of 6.96 s against the reference's 4.02 s while the gate read only the declared events, and a planned "camera in" never showed in the render; so the gate also reads the render (below, "On the render").

- **What counts.** An event is every authored visual change: a cut, a camera move start, a graphic in or out, a flash, a colour wash. A `hold` entry is not an event, and neither is a sound cue. How the reference's events are measured (from the analysis output and the TIMELINE.md rows): the `ice-remake` skill, `references/measuring.md`, "Pacing events".
- **Files.** One format for both: `{"duration": <s>, "fps": <optional>, "events": [{"t": <s>, "kind": "cut|camera|graphic|flash|sound|hold", "end": <s, holds only>, "note": "..."}]}`. Reference: `ice/reference/<ref-id>/analysis/events.json`. Film: `<render>.events.json` next to the render (written from the plan's events and the build's timeline). The older film events file writes the kind under `type`; both keys are read, and a bare list is read when the duration comes from the video or `--duration`.
- **Numbers.** Longest gap = the largest difference between consecutive event times, with 0 and the duration as bounds. Events per minute = events / duration x 60. Both are always recomputed from the events; stored `longest_gap`, `longest_gap_at` and `events_per_minute` fields are information only.
- **Gate.** The film fails when its longest gap is over the reference's longest gap x 1.10 plus one frame (1 / fps; 0.04 s when no fps is known), or when its events per minute are under the reference's x 0.90. A declared hold does not excuse a gap: a presenter hold is carried by inner events (section 3, row "News reel").
- **Run.** `python scripts/motion_check.py --pacing-only --events <film events.json> --reference-events ice/reference/<ref-id>/analysis/events.json --out ice/review/motion` (standard library only, no ffmpeg or numpy), or on the render: `python scripts/motion_check.py <render> --out ice/review/motion --reference-events <...>` (the film's events from `--events`, else `<render>.events.json`). It prints the `pacing` block as JSON (`reference` and `film`, each with `longest_gap`, `longest_gap_at` (the pair `[start, end]` of that gap in seconds, as the reference writer stores it), `events_per_minute`; `ok`; `blocking: true`; `reasons`; `render_measured`), writes `ice/review/motion/pacing.json`, and exits 1 on a fail.
- **On the render.** At 07 and 09 the gate also reads the render's measured changes: pass `--measure` with the JSON of `craft_gate.py measure <render> --out ice/review/motion/render-measure.json --text`, or run `motion_check.py` on the render, which measures it with the same functions and writes that file (its sample step defaults to the reference measure's). Measured changes: detected cuts; camera moves, each from where its motion begins to where it ends; a camera jump outside a move (a punch-in); text or card boxes appearing or leaving (the measure's text boxes, or `--boxes` from `lint.mjs --boxes-out` for HTML builds); flash transitions.
  - **Declared, not visible.** Every film event (holds and sound left out) needs a measured change of its class within 2 frames of its time, plus the sample step of the measure it is read from (a measured time sits on that grid; measure at one sample per frame, `--every` 1/fps, for the strict 2 frames). A cut needs a cut; a camera event a move start or end, a punch-in or a cut; a graphic (also show, text, title, caption, card, label, overlay, cta) a box in or out, or a cut; a flash or wash a flash transition or a cut; any other kind any measured change. An event on paper and not on screen, or a move too small to measure, fails; a graphic with no box source fails until it is measured.
  - **Shots.** Mean shot length (duration / (cuts + 1)) and cuts per minute on detected cuts, film against reference, each within plus or minus 30 percent (the stats contract: the `ice-remake` skill, `references/shot-mapping.md` section 4). The reference side is its own measure: `ice/reference/<ref-id>/analysis/craft-measure.json` (`craft_gate.py measure` of the reference, read from the events file's folder, or `--reference-measure`); a missing one fails. A reference with fewer than 2 detected cuts gives no shot statistic (noted); two measures at different sample steps are noted.
  - `pacing.json` then says `render_measured: true`. A `pacing.json` with `render_measured: false` is the stage 06 check on declared events only and does not count at 07, 09 or 10.
  - **Without a reference**, `--pacing-only --events <render>.events.json --measure <render measure>` runs the declared-event check alone, writes `declared.json` and exits 1 on a fail (section 6.3, "Declared events must be visible").
- **Where.** Earliest at 06 on the animatic's events (the plan-level twin: the `ice-edit` skill's `scripts/validate-plan.mjs` with `plan.reference`, documented in `references/clock-and-grouping.md`), then blocking at 07 on the draft render and at 09 as the confirmation, both on the render; at 10 delivery is refused while `pacing.json` says `ok: false`, says `render_measured: false`, or was never written for a film with a reference (`delivery-checks.md` section 5).
- **Cleared only by** changed material re-checked (more inner events, a hold split by a camera move or a graphic, a cut moved into the gap; AGENTS.md rule 14), or by the user's own words accepting a slower pace, quoted and logged in `ice/DIRECTION.md` and named in the delivery note. A builder's note, an approver's acceptance or a declared hold never clears it.

## 8. Depth and camera against the reference (lesson L12, blocking)

Scope: general, any video made with a reference. Trial: the references used a 2.5D photo-card field with a push-through, 3D flags, the speaker pushed back in depth and a camera moving in 38-63 percent of frames, yet none of the trial videos had any depth or 3D and the reference analysis never detected it. Every aspect of a reference is measured and carried over, not only its colours and text: the principle and how each item is detected live in the `ice-remake` skill, `references/measuring.md`, "Depth, 3D and camera".

- **Files.** One format for both: `{"camera_moving_share": <0..1>, "layers": <n>, "parallax_ratio": <n or null>, "push_through": <n>, "elements_3d": [<card_field, flag, globe, extruded_text, wrapped_text, cards_in_space, set, other:<word>>], "dof": {"present": true|false, "blur_px": <n or null>}, "triggers": [{"kind", "t", "words"}]}`. Reference: `ice/reference/<ref-id>/analysis/depth.json`. Film: `<render>.depth.json` next to the render.
- **From pixels.** The gate reads the render's `<render>.depth.json` as `craft_gate.py measure <render>` writes it from the pixels (dense optical flow with the camera removed: planes, parallax ratio, push-throughs; the `ice-remake` skill, `references/measuring.md` section 12), and the reference's `depth.json` measured the same way, so pixels are compared with pixels; a depth file written by hand from the plan is not evidence at 07 and 09.
- **Gate.** When the reference has 3D, 2.5D or camera movement, the film has the same kinds and close values: `camera_moving_share`, `parallax_ratio` and `push_through` within plus or minus 30 percent of the reference's (relative; a value the reference does not have is not asked for), every kind in the reference's `elements_3d` present, and depth of field present when the reference has it. A missing film depth file fails.
- **Run.** `python scripts/motion_check.py --pacing-only --reference-depth ice/reference/<ref-id>/analysis/depth.json --depth <render>.depth.json --out ice/review/motion` (with or without `--reference-events`), or on the render with the same flags. It prints the `depth` block (same shape as `pacing`), writes `ice/review/motion/depth-check.json`, and exits 1 on a fail.
- **Where.** Earliest at 04: every depth or 3D element of the reference maps to a shot with its build route (the `ice-remake` skill, `references/shot-mapping.md`; routes: the `ice-motion` skill, `references/depth-and-3d.md`, "Build routes for depth and 3D"), and storyboard lint check 21 reds a depth or 3D row with no `build:` route (`storyboard-lint.md` section 2). At 05 the style frames show the depth planes; at 06 the animatic carries the camera moves; blocking at 07 on the build and at 09 as the confirmation; at 10 delivery is refused while `depth-check.json` says `ok: false` (`delivery-checks.md` section 5).
- **Cleared only by** changed material re-checked by the same command. When no route can build an element on this machine (for example Blender is not installed and no other route fits), the user is told in plain words before 07, and only their own words dropping that element, logged in `ice/DIRECTION.md`, clear it.

# Ice Audio

Owns every sound decision in a film: whether there is music, where it comes from, how it is timed, which effects play, how the voice is written, paced and cleaned, how it is mixed, and what loudness ships. Workflows (ice-film, ice-explainer, ice-remake, ice-edit, ice-lottie, ice-ideas) call this skill; they never restate its rules.

Not owned here: caption timing and on-screen text (ice-type), Arabic dialect narration (ice-arabic), engine audio attributes and render commands (ice-engines), motion timing and transitions (ice-motion), asset sourcing outside audio (ice-assets), the final delivery gate and fingerprint file (ice-qa), creative direction and research (ice-direction).

## 1. Core rules

1. Sound is half the film. Never deliver silent unless the user asked for "no audio". "No narration" is not "silent".
2. The agent cannot hear. It measures every step, never says a mix "sounds good", and ends every audio job with a human listening check (mix-and-master.md section 8).
3. Plan in data, never adjectives: a cue sheet with times, a music brief with numbers, a sound plan with one row per event and its visible cause.
4. One clock per film: the voice (voice-led) or the beat grid (music-led). Write it in the cue sheet.
5. Every sound has a visible cause or a written reason. Restraint is the design; silence is scored, not leftover.
6. Recorded, never blocked: every music and SFX file has a recorded source, owner and license status (`licensed`, `unknown`, `needs purchase`) checked against the stated use case; rights are the user's (the `ice-assets` skill, `references/sourcing-and-licenses.md` section 0). A reference's own track, the user's track or any available track is a valid choice. Every track gets the sync license check (music-choice-and-licensing.md section 5) before it is placed in the cue sheet; its result is a status for the delivery note, never a reason to drop the track.
7. AI voice, music or SFX generation is optional, user-initiated and disclosed. Real people's voices, clones included, follow the `ice-assets` skill, `references/ai-media-policy.md` section 9.
8. Measure the exported MP4, not the intermediate WAV.
9. Anti-sameness: the sound axis (lead: score, sound design, voice or silence; genre; tempo band; dramaturgy) is logged in `ice/DIRECTION.md` with reasons; its definition and the distance rules: the `ice-direction` skill, `references/anti-sameness.md` section 1.
10. No extra stops. The router's stops table (`ice-motion-director/SKILL.md` section 5, "Stops") is the only list of times the run waits for the user. Wherever a file of this skill says to ask, or to let a human pick by ear: in auto mode decide by measurement (tempo, energy curve, landing on the end card, audibility), log the choice in `ice/DIRECTION.md`, and list the alternatives in the delivery note so the user can swap by ear later; in step mode put the options on the stage's review card. Consent items (a download, an install, a paid or cloud service, an account) are asked only inside the router's allowed stops; without a yes, take the path that needs none (a code score, code-made SFX, every word on screen instead of a voice) and say in the delivery note what a yes would add.

## 3. Sound policy by type

Use the film and edit type names from the architecture. Each row is a default; the brief overrides it.

| Type | Mode | Music default | SFX density | Loudness profile |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| product launch | voice-led or music-led | licensed track back-timed to the end card; code score for code-native styles | signature riser and impact once each, UI sounds on real actions | social -14 |
| app ad, social short, shorts and reels | hybrid or voice-led | licensed or code score, hook in 2 to 4 s | at most about 15 per minute | social -14 |
| brand film, industry spot | voice-led or free | licensed human-made track, one continuous piece | sparse, material-true, recorded | gentle -16 (social -14 if posted to feeds) |
| logo sting | music-led | code score or short licensed button | one hero hit plus a few landings | social -14 |
| kinetic type | music-led (under about 15 s) | code score on a whole-frame tempo | hits only on landings | social -14 |
| data story | voice-led | steady bed leaving 1 to 4 kHz | counters and landings | gentle -16 or social -14 |
| hype promo, showreel | music-led | licensed track with bar-line splices or code score | dense but every hit has a cause | social -14 |
| UI walkthrough | voice-led | quiet bed | clicks, typing texture, small UI | gentle -16 |
| music video | music-led | the user's track only | as the track allows | reference match or social -14 |
| art film | free or music-led | code score in the style's palette | material sounds, designed silences | gentle -16 |
| explainer (ice-explainer) | voice-led | steady bed or none under speech | one sound per moment, quiet typing | gentle -16 |
| long-form, podcast | voice-led | optional bed | minimal | gentle -16 |
| talking-head ad | voice-led | bed 10 to 14 dB under the voice | on visible actions only | social -14 |
| B-roll overlay | follows the host edit | none of its own | soft landings | host profile |
| Lottie and UI motion | none or UI sounds | none | per interaction, optional | not applicable unless rendered to video |

## 4. Order of work on the spine

Stage 08 runs in two parts: 08a beside the 07 build, from the locked cue sheet; 08b after 07, on the draft. The spine stage of each step:

| Step | Spine stage |
|---|---|
| 1. Decide the mode, the clock and whether music exists (music-choice-and-licensing.md sections 1 and 2). Log it | 05 (sound lead), confirmed at 06 |
| 2. Voice-led films: write the spoken side, estimate duration, take the voice the user chose at 01, generate or take the recording, align words, fix pace (voiceover.md). The voice comes before any frame | 03 |
| 3. Music-led films (hype promo, music video, kinetic type cut to a track, showreel): take or choose the track, run the sync license check, measure the beat grid with the kick-band phase check (cue-map-and-beat-sync.md section 3) | 01 |
| 4. Write the cue sheet before animating (cue-map-and-beat-sync.md section 2) | 06 |
| 5. Music for the other films: choose a track and run the sync license check, or compose by code (music-by-code.md); measure the beat grid; plan the landing on the end card | 08a |
| 6. SFX pass from the cue sheet events: select and place by sync point (sfx.md) | 08a |
| 7. Picture locks to the cue sheet (the workflow and ice-motion do this); export real event times from the draft composition | 07 |
| 8. Re-place SFX on the exported times, level for audibility; mix: voice priority, carve or duck, frequency slots, silences (mix-and-master.md sections 1 to 4) | 08b |
| 9. Master once to the chosen profile; measure the exported MP4; fix and re-measure (sections 5 to 7) | 08b |
| 10. Human listening check: 5 timestamps and one 8 s excerpt for the user, in the delivery note or the review card (section 8) | 10 |
| 11. Hand the audio report to ice-qa (section 9). Add the audio rows to `ice/assets/CREDITS.md` | 08b, then 10 |

## 5. Music source decision (scoped conflict)

"Never synthesize music or SFX" and "generate the score by code" are both right in scope:

| Use recorded and licensed audio | Use code-generated audio |
|---|---|
| The user supplied a track | Code-native film types and styles: art film, kinetic type, logo sting, pixel, synthwave, HUD, glitch, liquid, lo-fi collage, Bauhaus and similar |
| Client brand film, industry spot, product launch for a paying client (library tracks beat AI scores in client tests) | Zero third-party assets requested, or the user chose a new score |
| Real-footage edits (long-form, podcast, talking-head ad, reels) | UI loops and abstract UI sounds |
| Physical contact sounds in realistic worlds (recorded foley) | Synthetic picture worlds (blips, chiptune, HUD, cartoon) |

When both are open, prepare two contrasting options; who picks follows core rule 10 (step mode: the user by ear on the review card; auto mode: by measurement, the other delivered as a music-only alternate). A generated score still passes every guard in music-by-code.md section 4. When the brand's product is music, music-choice-and-licensing.md section 2 (music brands) applies first.

## 6. Numbers that hold everywhere

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Hit sync | Primary contact within 2 frames or 40 ms (stricter wins), never late; secondary within 80 ms |
| Kick-band phase | Grid phase corrected on onsets below 120 Hz; residual within about 25 ms |
| Bed under voice | 10 to 14 dB under the voice while speaking; duck depth 8 to 12 dB; carve preferred over a plain duck |
| SFX density | About 15 events per minute default; at most 2 cues per 100 ms; one signature riser and one impact per film |
| SFX audibility | Fails more than 6 dB under the ducked score in its window |
| Narration pace | Budget 2.2 to 2.5 wps (explainers, TTS), 2.5 to 3.0 wps (ads); flag above about 170 wpm; slow with pitch-preserving tempo, factor at least 0.85 |
| Silence | No digital silence inside a film; no undeclared hole over 0.25 s; at most 0.5 s of silence at the end |
| Loudness | Social -14 LUFS / at or under -1 dBTP; gentle and spoken -16 LUFS / -1 dBTP; strict headroom -16 / under -4 dBTP; broadcast per spec. Full table: mix-and-master.md section 5 |
| Mono | Extra mono loss at most 1.5 dB; nothing important only below 150 Hz |

## 7. Bundled code

- `scripts/mgaudio/`: the mgaudio Python toolkit from Vincentwei1021/mg-styles-15 (MIT): synthesized music recipes per style, SFX generators, mixer, mastering and QC. Copy it into the project (`ice/audio/lib/mgaudio/`) before use. It needs only numpy: with the pinned packages installed (after telling the user) it takes the fast path, without them it runs in fallback mode with the same API; which path ran and how to choose: music-by-code.md section 1. Samples are optional (synthetic fallbacks run without them); the CC0 sample set comes only from the optional Release asset pack.
- `scripts/master_audio.py` (founder's tool): mixes the film's audio with voice, music (with `--music-start` back-timing offset and `--duck`; no fade-in unless `--music-fade-in` is passed, so a cold open hits at full level on frame 0; music edges are set only by the four flags in mix-and-master.md section 6 and recorded in `master.json`), SFX from a JSON cue list, then a measured linear gain and a 4x oversampled true-peak limiter to the chosen profile, AAC at 48 kHz, copies the video stream, measures the exported file with ffmpeg `ebur128` and renders again until the true peak meets the target; writes `master.json` with `status` and exits 1 on a miss (mix-and-master.md section 6).
- `scripts/pacing.py` (from claude-code-video-toolkit, MIT): measures wpm per voice line and, with `--max-wpm`, slows a fast take in place with pitch-preserving atempo (floor 0.85).
- `scripts/qc.py` (adapted from claude-code-video-toolkit, MIT): audio QC from the cue sheet or a folder of voice lines: lines longer than their slot, dead air, pace, per-line loudness spread, and the exported film against the profile (`--target-lufs`, `--true-peak`); `--fix` normalizes with backups. Exit 1 on errors.
- `scripts/sound_map.py` (Ice): maps a reference's soundtrack into `sound_map.json`, `SOUND-MAP.md` and a timeline SVG: tempo, key, sections, energy curve, typed sound effects, voice segments, loudness, and instrument and genre proposals the user confirms at stage 01. Needs numpy and scipy plus ffmpeg; fields, rules and limits: references/sound-map.md.
- master_audio.py, pacing.py and qc.py run on Windows, macOS and Linux with Python and ffmpeg and ffprobe on PATH at the minimums in the `ice-engines` skill's `references/versions.json` (`runtimes`); stdlib only. Run them as `python "${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/scripts/<name>.py" --help` first. Other steps are ffmpeg commands written in the references. No script downloads anything at runtime.

# Move deck and mechanism catalog

A deck of named moves to deal from so films do not reach for the same three moves. A dealt card is a prompt, never an order: keep it, adapt it to the film's subject and medium, or drop it and say why. Roles: workhorse (carries a film quietly, safe to use twice), accent (noticed when it happens, once or twice), signature (the one move that is this film's own, used at the open, the middle and the close).

How to deal: list the film's type and energy; pick cards whose scope fits; exclude every card used as a core move in the brand's last three films (the `ice-direction` skill, `references/anti-sameness.md` section 7; ice-qa runs the fingerprint check); choose 3 core moves plus at most 2 accents plus at most 1 signature; record them in the motion plan.

## 1. The 46 cards

Times are seconds; distances are fractions of frame width, so cards hold at any size and frame rate.

| Card | Role | Move | Not when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open mid-move | workhorse | Frame 0 is composed and already moving (a camera move begun before frame 0, a cycle opened at phase 0.3) | A piece that draws itself (the empty page is the opening) |
| Open iris from subject | accent | Opens through a round opening growing from the subject, 0.5-0.8 s, even in log space | Centred on nothing; the third iris in the film |
| Open detail first | accent | So close on one detail the subject is unreadable, then pull back over 1.5-3 s (zoom 4-6 to 1, monotone) | A detail not drawn to be seen at that size |
| Open cold action | accent | No establishing shot: frame 1 is mid-action, the wide shot comes second | Calm pieces; subjects unnameable from the close shot within a second |
| Matched cut | workhorse | Hard cut hidden in travel: out accelerates 0.2-0.4 s, last frame fastest, in opens at the same speed and direction and rests over 0.8-1.4 s | No room to travel, or the shots travel different ways |
| Flight-through | accent | Push about 1.2 over 0.2 s, cut, incoming grows from about 2/3 to rest over 0.5 s | What is flown into is not what the next shot is about |
| Actor carries | signature | One object crosses the seam untouched (same position, size, colour) while the world changes | The object has no reason in the second scene |
| Shape becomes | signature | A shape travels, stretches a little, and lands exactly as a shape of the next picture | The shapes share nothing the eye can follow |
| Pick and grow | accent | One item of a set is chosen and grows into the next scene, framed exactly like the item | The set was only built to be left |
| Cover from inside | accent | Something in the scene grows to cover the frame; the next scene is complete underneath | An arbitrary shape brought in to hide the join; the third time |
| Brush | accent | A loaded brush pulled across in the story's direction, 0.6-0.9 s | Clean set registers; more than twice |
| Cut on beat | workhorse | A plain hard cut, both sides at rest, on a strong beat, matching in one thing (shape, position, gaze) | Nothing matches, or no beat |
| One canvas | workhorse | Scenes side by side in one layout larger than the frame; the camera travels | Unrelated places; long empty trips |
| Leave as arrived | workhorse | Leaves the way it came, mirrored and shorter (arrived 0.5 s settling, leaves 0.25 s accelerating) | The exit is the point of the scene |
| Sound leads | workhorse | J-cut (heard 0.3-0.8 s before seen) or L-cut (heard 0.5-1.5 s into the next) | A hit or contact (its sound belongs on its frame) |
| Slow push | workhorse | Push in 3-6 percent over the whole shot, linear | A shot with lots of its own motion |
| Parallax travel | workhorse | Sideways travel where near things swing wide and something crosses the lens | Styles whose point is flatness |
| Follow with lag | workhorse | Camera follows a little late (lag 0.1-0.2 s, lead room) | The subject must stay pinned for a read |
| Impact shake | accent | One impact rings through the frame, gone within a beat, with a little rotation | More than two per film; no real contact |
| Pull-back reveal | accent | Pull back (1.5-3 s): what we watched is a small part of something larger, present from the start | The larger thing adds nothing |
| Rack of attention | accent | Attention moves inside the frame without the camera moving (dim and soften one, sharpen the other) | The two things are in different places in the story (that is a seam) |
| Whip to rest | accent | Fast pan to a new subject decaying to rest, 0.4-0.7 s, the last third slow | Past the speed ceiling |
| Vertigo realisation | accent | Subject keeps its size while the world stretches behind, 1-2.5 s, dolly 1.6-3x | Fewer than two depth planes; not a realisation; more than once |
| Drawn on | signature | Drawn in the order a hand would make it, each stroke timed by its length | A fade standing in for drawing |
| From behind | workhorse | Comes out from behind something in the picture | A mask edge in mid-air |
| By light | accent | The scene is dark from frame 0; light arrives and finds the subject | Styles with no light |
| Assemble | accent | Parts back to front, 0.08-0.12 s apart, soft shadows closing as they land, at most about 7 in flight | Parts that are not real parts |
| Count during | workhorse | A number changes while its cause changes and stops with it | A figure not true of the data on screen |
| Written | workhorse | A line written by its tool at hand speed, held long enough to read twice | Over a face or busy texture |
| One line at a time | workhorse | Lines hand over word by word; old words leave at twice the pace they came | Lines that must be compared |
| Names the thing | accent | A dot lands on the thing, a line draws out, a short label at its end; the dot follows, the label holds | Busy areas where a backing would hide something |
| Label becomes heading | signature | A label travels across the seam and lands as the next scene's heading on its first frame | The words are not what the next scene is about |
| Underline then point | accent | The one word that matters is underlined by hand after it lands, 0.25 s on a beat | More than one word a line or twice a film; strike-through |
| Freeze and name | accent | The world stops on a telling pose, a word is written beside it, the camera keeps creeping, time resumes where it stopped | Nothing moves during the stop; calm films where a stop jolts |
| Anticipate | workhorse | The body goes the other way first, 0.15-0.3 s, a quarter of the action's size | Machines; moves meant to startle |
| Follow-through | workhorse | Loose parts arrive late and go further, 0.05-0.1 s per segment | Stiff materials |
| Settle | workhorse | A little past, back, at rest by 0.3-0.5 s; weight decides how far | Type read while landing; clean set registers |
| Head stays | accent | In a run the body works and the head rides level | A bounce that is the joke |
| Blink and breathe | workhorse | Chest over 3-4 s, blink 0.1-0.15 s at uneven gaps, an ear answers a sound | A portrait whose stillness is the point |
| Contact | accent | Dust, splash or compressed shadow on the hit frame, clearing over 0.4-0.8 s | A touch with no force |
| World answers | accent | The action moves something else a moment later | It pulls the eye off the subject at the key moment |
| Hold the result | workhorse | After the biggest change, hold the result 1-2 s, alive but unbroken | Loops under 6 s |
| Time ramp | accent | At the top of the action time slows (to 0.2-0.4), stays long enough to read the shape, then returns; score keeps tempo | Nothing in flight; loops; a second time |
| Returns to open | signature | The last frame is the first frame, now understood | A film whose point is permanent change |
| Subtract | workhorse | Motion slows, parts leave, music stops on a bar line, a breath, one soft sound on the last picture | A few-second sting (it is all ending) |
| Mark from actor | accent | The travelling thing lands as part of the mark and the end card opens from it | The actor resembles the mark in no way |

Refused names (with the replacement; News reel (edit type `news-reel`, style family `family-broadcast-news.md`): a light sweep inside a named glass card or along a named map, and a measured glitch clip on a card-to-photo cut, are package moves with their triggers, `composition-and-staging.md` section 6 and `cut-catalog.md` section 13): glitch with no cause (a cut on the beat, or one shake on a real impact); light sweep over chrome (light that belongs to the world); fade-in standing in for making (drawn on, from behind, written); particle burst on everything (contact born at a real contact point); cross dissolve between unrelated pictures (shape becomes, actor carries, or the gentle move); lens flare painted on (one named light, reveal by light).

## 2. Seam mechanisms by intent

| The viewer should think | Mechanism | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| "And now this" | Foreground as transition (a headline, logo or shape scales through the camera with directional blur and stretched edges while the next scene is already in place underneath) | Not an arbitrary shape; not every seam (the third time reads as a wipe preset) |

## 3. Sixteen mechanisms from launch films

Foreground fly-through; logo as portal; selection to expansion; materializing result; persistent rails; continuous canvas; artifact ring; card stack or fan; perspective fold; exploded layers; registered decomposition; a wall collapsing to one actor; carousel emphasis; colour-field takeover (once per film); tile wipe (sparingly); request, picker, confirmation (the most usable sequence for service businesses). Physical products get material realism and exploded structure; software gets cursor and selection causality; services get request, choice, confirmation. Name three signature transformations you can describe without effect names. Borrow mechanisms, never layouts, logos or footage.

## 4. Object-carried transition ideas (illustration only, choose by meaning)

The next scene's object becomes the wipe; an object exits through the screen; a shape match cut with a change of render style; a pull back revealing the destination; a picking hand; a stepped colour field with the logo inverting at the field's edge (the field stays as the new background, at most twice); passing through a symbol; a parade pushing the frame; an edge-on spin opening onto a colour card; stacked pages rising; a growing seed; a ghost of the start state left behind. Each needs its own cause in the story; none is a default.

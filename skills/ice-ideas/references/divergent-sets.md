# Divergent idea sets

How to generate a set of concepts that are truly different from each other and from every other film on the subject, and how to write them as cards.

Owner note: the pitch round itself (how many directions, tail sampling, the silhouette check, critic, the order of presentation, mixing answers) is owned by the `ice-direction` skill, pitch round. This file produces what goes into that round.

> Sources: heygen-com/hyperframes intent and pitch references (Apache-2.0), Omar-Tnzxo video-production-studio concepts reference (founder), Tejashmakwana/motionmaxxing (Apache-2.0), AbubakrChan/product-launch-motion (MIT), bangtutorial/bang-motion (MIT), alchaincyf/huashu-art-motion (MIT), browser-use/video-use manim sub-skill (MIT), alexgreensh/anidoodle (Apache-2.0). Ideas only, rewritten: imMamdouhaboammar/motion-graphics-skills (concept library, mix-and-match), feitangyuan/onetake, vibe-motion/skills.

## 1. Inputs every set needs

- The message sentence and the ground rows from `references/idea-craft.md` section 2.
- Dated research notes from `ice/research/` (from `ice-direction`): what the category's films currently look like, what the audience responds to, cultural moments. Each concept cites at least one brief slot or research note id.
- The local fingerprint list (from `ice-qa`): structures and motion languages already used in earlier projects. A concept that repeats one is replaced.

Never start from an example in any Ice file. Examples teach a pattern; the content comes from this brief.

## 2. Generators

Pull candidates from at least three different generators. Each generator answers a different question, which is what makes the set diverge.

| Generator | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Paths** | Subject world (what does the product live inside?), emotion (what does the viewer feel before and after?), audience slice (who exactly, in what moment?), anti-pattern (what does every film in this category do, and what is the opposite?), unusual format (what everyday interface or object can carry the story?) | One candidate per path (founder, hyperframes) |
| **Central picture** | Which single picture mechanism carries the film? One element transforms through everything; one camera move through scale; a before/after split; a chain reaction; a physical metaphor; a typographic system | Candidates that differ in mechanism, not just story (onetake, ideas only) |
| **Film kinds** | Which kind of film fits this subject? (film kinds table below) | One candidate per kind that fits (motionmaxxing) |
| **Tension gaps** | Which gap does the film close? (list in `references/idea-craft.md` 2.4) | A premise built on the gap |
| **Narrative shapes** | Reveal, proof, behind the scenes, transformation, contrast, accumulation | A structure family per candidate |
| **Concept families** | See section 3 | Visual systems |
| **Concept menu** | `references/concept-menus.md` (19 concepts with fit notes) | Named skeletons |
| **Recombination** | Section 4 | Hypotheses built from transformed genes of several references |
| **Constraint route** | Impose one hard limit (one colour, one object, one shot, no text, no product shown until the end) and see what idea survives it | A candidate when discovery is off |
| **Classic divergence** (only on request or when stuck) | SCAMPER (substitute, combine, adapt, modify, put to other use, eliminate, reverse) and assumption reversal (list the category's assumptions, flip one) (video-use manim sub-skill) | Fresh angles |

Rules:
- "Never pitch five versions of the same idea." (founder). Candidates must differ in their central picture idea, "not three stories with the same cards" (onetake, ideas only).
- At least two candidates are non-obvious for this category and audience. The most typical direction may appear only labelled as the typical one, never counted among the unexpected.
- If the brief matches a stock or worked direction in any Ice file, that direction is automatically one of your candidates and automatically one you kill (product-launch-motion rule, generalized).
- Left to choose, every film reaches for the same few moves. Treat every menu item as a prompt, never an order (anidoodle).
- Two concepts with the same rough silhouette (major elements as bounding boxes) are one concept: replace one (hyperframes; mechanics owned by `ice-direction`).
- Bullet lists in the brief become visual ideas chosen for the content, never three tiles by default (bang-motion).

Film kinds table (motionmaxxing), each with when it works and how it fails:

| Kind | Works when | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| A real task documented in the UI, one case from question to result | The product is a tool people do something in | It becomes a feature tour |
| A single held shot that slowly changes | One object or material carries the subject | The world is not worth 15 s of looking |
| A joke with a character | A relatable problem, a playful brand | The character is not drawn in the brand's grammar |
| A typographic essay in the brand's voice | The value is a sentence and the voice is the brand | The writing is weak; a centred title page |
| A montage of real output | The product makes things | The output is fake or repetitive |
| Stillness that breaks | The brand sells relief, silence, focus | The break has no visible cause |
| A transformation chain (A becomes B becomes C) | Rarely: only when the chain is the product | Picked because it is the film you always make (the most over-used shape) |
| Claim, cut, proof, call to action | A clear one-line promise with a demo | Used as the default skeleton (it is one shape among many) |

## 3. Concept families (ideas only, rewritten from Mamdouh's concept library)

Visual systems to borrow as starting points. Pick by what the message needs; each is a family, not a template.

| Family | Core mechanism |
|---|---|
| One object, many states | A single object keeps its identity while its state tells the story |
| Build and reveal / deconstruct to explain | Parts assemble into the whole, or the whole comes apart to show how it works |
| Compress and expand | Scale changes carry meaning (a day into seconds, a detail into a world) |
| Corridor of proof / desk of evidence / poster wall | Evidence arranged in space, the camera walks through it |
| Camera dive / foreground handoff | Movement through depth; a foreground object hands the frame to the next scene |
| Negative-space reveal | What is missing shapes the subject |
| Light as narrator / light becomes time | Light leads the eye or marks time passing |
| One line through everything / branch and converge | A drawn path links every scene, or paths split and rejoin |
| Type as environment / type becomes layout / word as object | Words are the set, the architecture or the prop |
| Question and answer | A question posed in the world, answered by an action |
| Before, during, after / one day compressed / freeze the instant | Time structures |
| Data becomes material / one metric escapes the dashboard | Numbers turn into physical things |
| Archive becomes present | Old material comes alive into today |
| Primitive as protagonist / brand colour as event | A dot, stroke or colour is the hero |
| Logo assembled from callbacks | Earlier objects return to build the mark |
| Front stage versus backstage / after the lights go out | Showing the hidden side |
| Guest journey / input becomes outcome / feature as spatial change | The path of a user or a request through the product |

## 4. Recombination from references (ideas only, rewritten from Mamdouh's mix-and-match)

Use when the user gives several references or asks for "something like A meets B".

1. **Never average references.** Break each into transferable genes. A gene records: what you observed, the principle that transfers, the specifics that must stay with the source (protected), and the opportunity to transform it. Vague genes ("premium", "cinematic") are not genes.
2. **Modes:** the user curates the references; assisted discovery (you propose); hybrid by default (2 to 6 primary references plus at most 3 wildcards from other disciplines).
3. **Mutate every adopted gene:** swap its material, shift its scale, remap its time, make a flat idea spatial or a spatial one flat, invert it, swap roles, shift the metaphor, or transfer a constraint. Every adopted gene changes role, material, scale, timing, meaning or context.
4. **Productive pairings:** a mechanism with a material, a rhythm with a hierarchy, a constraint with a metaphor, a spatial rule with typography. One meaningful mutation beats stacked distortions.
5. **Three hypotheses** that differ by mechanism: coherent, tension, wildcard (or a constraint route when discovery is off).
6. **Wildcards** only fill a named missing dimension (spatial, material, movement, hierarchy, transition, light, sequence, constraint) from a distant domain. Stop at 1 to 3, and stop searching once a concept passes.
7. **Creative distance audit.** Fail when: one source dominates, scene order mirrors a source, a distinctive layout survives with only a reskin, references sit side by side as chunks, the wildcard is decoration, or it reads as "A plus B". Pass when two sources matter, none dominates, principles were transformed, one thesis holds it together, and you can explain it without naming the references.

Reference analysis of a video (measuring cuts, rhythm, style) belongs to the `ice-remake` skill.

## 5. Concept card

Every surviving candidate becomes one card. Template: `references/templates.md`, concept card.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Logline | One sentence that predicts the hero moment |
| Why it works | For this audience, citing a brief slot or research note |
| Hook (0 to 3 s) | What is literally on screen and literally heard |
| Carrier | What survives every boundary (object, line, colour, word, camera move) |
| Beat sheet | Rough times, one job per beat |
| Hero moment | What happens and roughly when |
| Proof shots | Which real recordings, screens or photos carry it |
| End card and CTA | The last frame and the one action asked |
| Rules out | What this concept refuses (helps the critic see the difference) |
| Look | One line of intent; palette and type are decided by `ice-direction` |
| Risk | Cliché, claim risk, cultural misread, production cost |
| Structure fingerprint | `concept . scene count . main object per scene . opening . closing` (bang-motion), written so later projects can compare |
| Sources | Brief slots and research note ids it rests on |

Pitch format per direction, when the user only needs the short version: three lines: the concept in one sentence, its visual world, its opening hook. Capabilities in plain language, never feature or tool names; a capability that would fit every pitch is decoration (hyperframes).

## 6. Selection

- The idea is chosen, not averaged; never combine all candidates (motionmaxxing). Two may merge under one parent skeleton; never blend three, never average references.
- Candidate count: a full idea set for the user follows the count and sampling of the `ice-direction` pitch round; inside a build workflow choosing its own concept, write three candidates and choose one with a reason.
- Pick for what is most convincing when seen, not what is easiest to build (bang-motion).
- New character interpretations or style directions get three options that differ in composition, not only in colour (huashu-art-motion). Step mode: the three go on the stage's review card. Auto mode: execute the critic's choice and list the other two in one sentence each in the delivery note.
- After a choice, commit and stop being creative about the concept; creativity moves to execution (product-launch-motion).
- Log the set, the choice, the reason and the critic's note in `ice/DIRECTION.md`.

## 7. Self-check

- [ ] At least three generators used; the set spans different central pictures.
- [ ] At least two candidates are non-obvious; the typical one is labelled as typical.
- [ ] No candidate repeats a fingerprint from earlier projects.
- [ ] Every card cites brief slots or dated research, never an example from these files.
- [ ] Every card has a hook, a carrier, a hero moment and a risk.

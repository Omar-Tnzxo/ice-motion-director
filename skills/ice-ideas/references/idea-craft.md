# Idea craft

What an idea is, where it comes from, how to make it belong to this brand, and how to kill a weak one before anyone sees it.

> Sources: Tejashmakwana/motionmaxxing (Apache-2.0), AbubakrChan/product-launch-motion (MIT), heygen-com/hyperframes product-launch-video and creative skills (Apache-2.0), Omar-Tnzxo video-production-studio (founder), echris6/motion-video-kit (MIT), edenfunf/reelmimic (MIT), alchaincyf/huashu-art-motion (MIT), nateherkai/herk-edit-kit (MIT and pipeline use permission), charlie947/motion-graphics-skills (MIT). Ideas only, rewritten: imMamdouhaboammar/motion-graphics-skills, feitangyuan/onetake, Vincentwei1021/anything2explainer. Quoted lines keep their authors' wording.

## 1. Idea versus topic

- The concept sentence predicts the hero moment; otherwise it is a topic: the `ice-direction` skill, `references/pitch-round.md` sections A.3 and A.5.
- One claim one film, the seven-word claim test, specific beats general, beat load per runtime, the emotional change as payload: the `ice-direction` skill, `references/treatment.md` sections 2 and 3.
- Thesis test (explainer, documentary), held only here: one thesis sentence per chapter and one spine for the whole film. It fails when the sections could be shuffled without loss.

## 2. Ground the idea

Fill these from the brief, the user's material and dated research before generating anything. Empty rows are questions for `ice-direction` or logged assumptions, never inventions.

### 2.1 PAGE
PAGE (problem, audience, goal, emotion): the `ice-direction` skill, `references/treatment.md` section 2.

### 2.2 Product truth
Audience, pain or desire, promise, the product's role, proof, CTA. "Build the sequence around the promise, not a feature list. A website is an information layout; a video is an emotional sequence." (hyperframes product-launch-video). Translate every feature into viewer value; never stack raw features.

Claim sources, in order: the product's own headline, the customers' words, one before/after (product-launch-motion). An official launch line used verbatim usually beats a written one.

### 2.3 Brand inventory
Brand or subject inventory and what stays literal versus invented: the `ice-direction` skill, `references/treatment.md` section 3. Brand asset collection and authenticity (never redraw a logo): the `ice-assets` skill, `references/brand-authenticity.md` sections 3 and 6.

### 2.4 Tension and job
- Start from a tension gap: what is seen versus what is behind it, complex versus simple, before versus after, claim versus proof, scale versus detail, expectation versus reality, chaos versus control.
- One narrative job dominates the film: reveal, explain, prove, persuade, announce, celebrate, compare, provoke, document or invite.
- Name a specific emotional register (examples, illustration only: controlled anticipation, quiet authority, earned relief) instead of "exciting" or "premium", and sketch intensity over time.
- Story-led films need an emotion arc, not just rising energy: someone wants something, meets an obstacle or a wait, gets it at the climax, so the hit means something (reelmimic).

## 3. Make it ownable

1. **Give the logo a job:** the button, the radar centre, the toggle, the cursor, a word in the sentence. "a logo that only appears at the end as a sticker is wasted. Puns on the name are the weakest ownership." (motionmaxxing)
2. **Amplify a micro-detail into weather:** a small brand detail (a sheen, a corner radius, a sound) becomes the light or rhythm of the whole film.
3. **Signature move:** take the product's core verb and make the film do it. It "repeats two or three times" and is "cheap enough to do perfectly" (product-launch-motion). Name up to three signature transformations without using effect names (motion-video-kit).
4. **Brand inventory turns into the product's action:** the `ice-direction` skill, `references/treatment.md` section 3.
5. **Native transitions:** prefer a change the product itself performs (its own mode switch, its own gesture) over an invented effect (onetake, ideas only).
6. **Claims built from objects already on screen,** not captioned over the picture (onetake, ideas only). "a claim made by type is a hook, a claim made by operation is a proof" (motionmaxxing).
7. **Cause and effect:** every animated action visibly produces a result (motion-video-kit).
8. **One running example** carries the whole film: the same customer, the same object, the same task from start to end (huashu-art-motion, anything2explainer ideas). Simplify real product behaviour for film without inventing actions or outcomes (product-launch-motion).

## 4. Brief shapes that produce ideas

- The half-understood question, the visual turn that surprises, the metaphor that carries it. Slot form: `<question the audience half understands>? And then <the visual turn>. <the carrying metaphor>.` (charlie947)
- The concept sentence describes a system (what moves, what stays, what carries the cuts), not a moodboard.
- Concept fields that must be stated for every candidate: hook frame, what carries every boundary, what it rules out, its look in one line (onetake, ideas only). Full card fields: `references/divergent-sets.md`.

## 5. Idea tests (kill before pitching)

Run every candidate through the concept tests (hero moment, the brand test and competitor swap on a blind description as the `ice-qa` skill's `references/identity-tests.md` defines them, the "and" test, reference leak, asset feasibility, any-brief): the `ice-direction` skill, `references/pitch-round.md` section A.5. Then run the two tests below. One fail kills a candidate or sends it back for one rewrite.

| Test | How | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| Truth | Every number, name and result traces to the truth sheet | Anything invented |
| Phone read | Readable on a phone, muted, at the target platform | Needs sound or a big screen to make sense (unless the platform is a big screen) |

Scoring for trade-offs: the `ice-direction` skill, `references/pitch-round.md` (section "Concept sources") section 9. Never prefer the busiest candidate by default.

## 6. Honesty rules for ideas

- Never invent a price, date, "spots left" count, testimonial, user count or result (charlie947, motionmaxxing). "A film with zero invented numbers beats a film with impressive fake ones" (product-launch-motion). Only round toward the less impressive number.
- Measured results, targets and sample data stay separate; a target is never presented as a result. With no data, a demo is labelled illustrative in the notes.
- Do not fabricate a failure, a mystery or a product result to make a better payoff (herk-edit-kit).
- When the product "thinks", show an object from the customer's world, never an AI symbol (no orbs, sparkles, neural nets, glowing brains).
- Ads (app ad, talking-head ad, industry spot): positive features only. Features that exist to handle failures (missing items, refunds, cancellations, complaints) remind viewers of the failure; leave them out unless the owner asks (founder rule). This does not apply to explainers, documentaries or support films, where honest limits belong.
- Regulated claims (health, finance, real estate, education results, children) stay out until the owner sends approved wording (founder rule).

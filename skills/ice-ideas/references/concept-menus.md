# Concept menus

Starting shapes for concepts, feature presentation, openings and closings, metaphors, and the category spines you meet or break on purpose. A menu item is a prompt, never an order: it must be rebuilt from this brief's product, audience and brand.

> Sections 1 to 4: adapted from bangtutorial/bang-motion `references/opener-konsep.md` (MIT, (c) 2026 Bang Tutorial). Changes: translated from Indonesian, condensed, examples generalized, execution numbers left to the `ice-film` skill, transition menu left to the `ice-motion` skill, dash characters removed. ## 0. Frame laws (bang-motion)

1. **Three candidates, one choice.** Before any rundown, write three concept candidates from these menus (or new ones), each a one-sentence premise naming the product. Choose one for what is most convincing when SEEN, not what is easiest to build. Show all three.
2. **Structure fingerprint.** Write one line: `concept . scene count . main object per scene . opening . closing`. Put it in the concept card and the delivery note.
3. **Different from the previous project.** When two structure lines count as different (concept is one of the five parts): the `ice-direction` skill, `references/anti-sameness.md` section 3. New colours on an old scene order are still a template.
4. **Canonical components are not defaults.** Each may appear only when the concept demands it, at most TWO per film: text that zooms in then exits left; three parallel feature tiles; a typed input or search box; a camera push-through between scenes; a light leak at the cut; logo plus CTA stacked centre as the last scene.
5. **The standard order is one option.** Hook, feature, feature, promise, logo, CTA is only one possibility and is never used by two consecutive projects.
6. **A scene need not be "sentence plus object".** Action without text, type alone, or several objects reacting to each other are all valid.
7. **Examples are not scripts.** When the user asks for "style X" or "concept X": take its principles and motion language, build new acts from the user's product and audience, choose a different opening and closing, use at most one signature moment and transform it, and derive palette, shapes and props from the user's brand.

Rejected as a template (compare against it, never build it): `hook-feature-CTA . 6 scenes . text / 3 tiles / input / mockup / promise / logo . text zooms then exits left . logo plus CTA centre`.

## 2. Feature presentation menu (instead of automatic "three tiles")

One feature demoed with the rest as 0.4 s flashes at the end; a word swapping inside the same sentence; an object transforming from feature A into feature B; cards shuffled or thrown one by one; icons orbiting the product then entering it; a mockup in use where features light up in place; a diegetic checklist ticked by actions in the world; a before/after divider moving per feature; a screen that wakes and is used, the chosen item leaving the screen as an object; items collected by hand (or paw) into a growing pile; personality stickers in the audience's own words popping at object corners; one container swapping content fast (under 1 s) and stopping on the brand's own; brand guide pages rising stacked; a claim, how, result cycle per feature; the cursor as actor (dragging, opening, ticking real UI); a UI flow used by a persona; content changes while the container stays (about every 1.5 s); running rows of capability chips; who uses it as labelled avatars; integrations bursting from the product icon and drawn back; a giant word changing together with a person; a container landing and bursting features; the brand marker fixed while the place of use changes; one big 3D object flanked by small text; a table assembled from falling category objects; a parade of lifestyle objects; the brand word as a moving outline pattern; a tour with claims; an icon tile grid whose count follows the real features (never always three).

Two consecutive projects never use the same method.

## 3. Opening menu (first 3 seconds)

- In media res: the object is already moving on frame 1.
- Sound first: a hit or effect, the picture one beat later.
- A giant word cropped by the frame edge; the camera pulls back to reveal the sentence.
- A line draws and turns out to be the product or logo shape.
- A texture close-up revealed as part of the product.
- A question typed diegetically on a world object (not a floating input box).
- Dark, then one light, then the world.
- The problem immediately: open in the middle of the chaos.
- A grey world with one coloured subject: only the actor carries the brand colour.
- An unexpected actor: frame 1 shows who really uses it; the joke reads in one second.
- An empty frame with a motif rising from the edge (sets up a loop closing).
- Result first: the product's output, very close and soft, pulls back to make room for the claim.
- Old tools scattered; the product icon is born from behind the pile.
- Symbol first, the container grows behind it.
- A stretched word (signature moment: only when the word really means length or continuity for this product, rebuilt with a new word and a new stretch).
- A dot of light in the dark blooms into layered shapes.
- A kinetic title assembling from letters entering from different directions.
- An object between the two words of a title, a zoom punch to it and back.
- A concrete small scene: hands doing something related to this film's topic (huashu-art-motion).

Never open on a logo or an empty frame unless the reveal is the story (scoped in `SKILL.md` section 5).

Text has eight doors: up, down, left, right, from depth, mask reveal, per word, typed. Pick by the sentence's meaning; two consecutive scenes never use the same door. Hook rules and timing: `references/hooks-and-openers.md`.

## 4. Closing menu

The logo is born from the last object; the camera pulls back and the world was the logo shape; the key word of the last sentence is replaced by the logo; a diegetic CTA (a button in the UI or the world is pressed); a seamless loop to frame 1; firm stillness (everything stops together, one sentence, cut); reception (the hero object arrives, the receiver's hands enter, the brand only on the object's label, no end card); a callback to the opening (the logo takes the opening object's form and leaves the way it came in); an address typed, erased, replaced by the product name; a device on a pedestal with the icon growing into the lens; brand letters as a stage (signature moment, only when the brand shape is strong enough); through a portal to the logo; the main object landing on a podium; the wordmark shrinking from blur to sharp; ending loud; ending mid-motion; ending on the subject still in use; ending on the problem, now absent (product-launch-motion).

Logo plus CTA stacked in the centre only when the brief asks for an explicit CTA. The ending reuses earlier visual logic; no new effect at the climax.

## 5. Metaphor patterns (motionmaxxing)

Pattern: an abstract phrase in the script becomes one concrete, countable picture. Ask what the abstract word looks like, or what it is the opposite of. The pairs below are illustration only; build each film's metaphors from its own script and audience.

| Phrase type (illustration only) | Picture |
|---|---|
| "top 1%" | "100 identical dots or people, everything dims except one, which takes the accent" |
| "8 principles" | "8 abstract shapes that gather into one form" |
| "getting attention" | "a light sweep across a dark field of identical things, catching one" |
| "over a weekend" | "a calendar whose last two days light up" |
| "commoditized" | "many identical objects flooding in" |
| "blank canvas" | "an actual empty artboard with a cursor about to draw" |
| "billion-dollar" | "the word at 3-4x the surrounding type, alone in frame" |
| "stop scrolling" | "a feed that halts dead on the word" |
| the product thinks | "an object from the customer's world ..., never an orb or sparkle" |

Every metaphor also obeys the grounding rule: the literal thing it stands for is on screen first or with it, so a stranger can point at both (the `ice-qa` skill, `references/clarity-gate.md` section 6). Rules: "Use each metaphor once per film; reusing a visual reads as padding." "Take props from the audience's culture, not the category's clip-art ... Not rockets for launch, shields for security, lightbulbs for idea."

## 6. Category spines to meet or break (ideas only, rewritten from vibe-motion)

These are the shapes most films in a category already use. Use them to name the typical direction in a set, then decide on purpose whether to meet the expectation or break it.

| Category | Typical spine |
|---|---|
| AI or SaaS | intent, planning, capabilities, execution, output, proof, logo |
| Physical product | hero shot, interaction, feature macro, usage context, result, logo |
| Service | context, process, evidence, outcome, promise, logo |
| Image-led brand | imagery, motif, benefit, emotional payoff, logo |

Breaking the category expectation the same way twice is itself a house style (product-launch-motion): vary which expectation you break. Full story structures and script shapes per film type: the `ice-film` skill, script shapes bank.
